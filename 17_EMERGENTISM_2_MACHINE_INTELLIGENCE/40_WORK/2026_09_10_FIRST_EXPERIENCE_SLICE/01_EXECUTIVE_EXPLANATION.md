# Emergentism — Fire for the mind

`• < ⊙ > ○`

**A worldview for understanding being. An instrument for turning understanding
into a worthwhile, achievable next move.**

Emergentism begins with a finite intelligence in a wider reality: you can
distinguish something, understand relationships, imagine alternatives and act.
But your picture is not the whole world, an imagined outcome is not an achieved
one, and a compelling purpose does not supply the means to fulfil it. The
instrument brings those differences into one usable view. [Interpretation; sources below.]

## The worldview: finity, between boundaries

Emergentism calls the coherent whole **Dasein**. Claims within it must say in
which world, register and model they can be. **Situated dasein** names a finite
actual bearer within that whole. **Finity**, marked by `⊙`, is determinate,
situated manifestation—not simply a small number of things.

Its atlas organizes distinction (D1), configuration and relation (D2), state
and probability (D3), actual causal history (D4), and represented possibilities
(D5). The Ground-facing `•` (D0) and nonclosure-facing `○` (D6) frame the map.
“Zero” and “infinity” are boundary-role names here, not operands. The identity
is not an arithmetic inequality, and the atlas does not claim to inventory all
Dasein or demonstrate nature's uniquely necessary decomposition.

This worldview gives room to the actual and the possible without confusing
them. A plan exists now as a thought, diagram or computation; the future it
describes is not thereby achieved. [Explore that distinction](02_ATLAS_D4_D5.md).

## The instrument: give each part a job

The **Rosetta** connects question families with reasoning operations and names
moves in a selected game vocabulary. It helps organize inquiry; an analogy is
not evidence, and its rows are not ranks of people. Reasoning stations and game
moves are distinct vocabularies; neither grants permission to act.

The **Burrisphere** gives a visual language for declared relationships between
possibility and means. Its chart can show balance; it cannot tell you, on its
own, which purpose is good or whether a project is ready.

The **Soul Loop** brings the account back to contact: understand the situation,
compare alternatives, propose a bounded step, receive evidence and revise.
The **wisdom stack** keeps information, supported claims, decisions and adopted
policies distinct. Making something public is a separate choice.

In planning language, **Vision, Mission and Objectives (VMO)** articulate
worthwhile possibilities. **Strategies, Key Performance Indicators and Agents
(SKA)** organize the means; the situated observer connects and revises them. For machines,
this means inspectable sources, alternatives, uncertainty and correction—not
a required profession of belief or permission to act.

## The direction: capability with others

Emergentism chooses cooperation, affected-bearer visibility, correction and
voluntary exit as guiding commitments. Ask not only what a plan lets you do,
but whose capacity it strengthens, who pays for it, and what it might foreclose.
These commitments are stated, not smuggled in as mathematical consequences.

The intended benefit is practical: see what matters, distinguish what you know
from what you hope, and choose a next step that can teach you something. Whether
the additional structure helps must be tested against simpler approaches.
