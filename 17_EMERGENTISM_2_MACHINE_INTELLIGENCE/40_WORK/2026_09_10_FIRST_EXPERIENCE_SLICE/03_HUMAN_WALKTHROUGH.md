# Should we launch next week?

**A filled Finity Card.** These seven prompts come from the existing Lived
Compass. They are practical questions, not seven new ontological stages.
[Explore D4/D5 first](02_ATLAS_D4_D5.md), or start here.

## DECISION

<!-- parity:scenario:start -->
Our team agrees that launching next week is best. We have a working prototype, no independent user test, and capacity for one small trial. What do we know, what remains uncertain, and what should we do next?
<!-- parity:scenario:end -->

The decision is not whether the team feels ready; that agreement is given.
It is what evidence and responsible next move the team needs before deciding
launch scope and timing. “Best” requires a declared objective and affected people.

## ACTUAL

**Given by the fictional scenario, not independently observed:**

<!-- parity:facts:start -->
- F1: The hypothetical team agrees that launching next week is best. The agreement is stipulated; the correctness of “best” is not.
- F2: A working prototype exists in the scenario; “working” has no supplied acceptance criteria.
- F3: No independent user test has occurred in the scenario.
- F4: The scenario permits one small trial; its budget, duration and participant access are not supplied.
<!-- parity:facts:end -->

A prototype may work in its creators' hands while failing the intended user task.
That is a rival explanation to test, not a finding about this prototype. The
team's agreement may reflect relevant experience, shared assumptions, or both.

## POSSIBILITY

<!-- parity:unknowns:start -->
We do not know the product, intended users, promised benefit, meaning of “best,” launch scope, obligations, acceptable risks, or what the prototype can reliably do. Costs, consent, recruitment access, success criteria and longer-term effects are unspecified. No calibrated probability of success can be assigned from these facts.
<!-- parity:unknowns:end -->

<!-- parity:options:start -->
- A1 — Launch next week: may realize value sooner if ready, but readiness and exposure are unknown. This is not authorized by team agreement.
- A2 — Define and, if feasible and authorized, use the one small trial before deciding launch scope and timing: may expose a mistaken readiness assumption, but can delay launch and will not establish every kind of readiness.
- A3 — Defer without spending the trial yet: preserves that capacity while objectives or permissions are unclear, but delays learning and any potential benefit.
<!-- parity:options:end -->

The options are represented alternatives. Listing them does not make all of them
feasible, equally likely, equally good or authorized. The comparison may change
if a time-sensitive obligation or serious risk is later established.

## FINITY

**Proposed observation and review boundary—not a test already conducted:**

<!-- parity:test:start -->
Propose one bounded, consented trial of a declared core task with suitable participants independent of the team's launch judgment, if recruitment and access fit the stated capacity. First name the intended user and benefit, unacceptable harm, available means, and who may approve the trial. Define completion, permitted assistance, failure and ambiguity before observing results. Record what participants did, what help they needed, difficulties and feedback; keep observations separate from interpretation. Stop if consent is withdrawn or the declared safety boundary is breached. Do not use sensitive or consequential live operations merely because a prototype is available.

T_S is the end of this one trial and review before any launch commitment; no date or duration is supplied. T_L is the longest responsible horizon for support, privacy, reliability and other materially affected interests; it must be scoped, not invented. Preserve risks this small trial cannot test. If independent participation, permission or safe scope is unavailable, return to A3 and revise the proposal; do not substitute team approval for user evidence.
<!-- parity:test:end -->

A candidate discriminator is the difference between the team's claim of working
and the intended user's ability to obtain the declared benefit under the
predefined conditions. Completion counts, assistance and feedback are potential
observations, not measurements supplied by this example.

## NEXT MOVE

<!-- parity:recommendation:start -->
I cannot conclude that launching next week is best. First define what “best” and “working” mean for the intended users. I provisionally favour A2 if a relevant, safe, consented trial fits the stated capacity and receives the required approval; otherwise A3. Decide the launch scope and timing only after reviewing the evidence and the readiness questions the trial cannot settle.
<!-- parity:recommendation:end -->

For now, write the core-task and risk definitions and submit the bounded trial
proposal for the actual responsible person's review. There is no mandate or
consent in the prompt; this document cannot supply them. The proposal includes
only one trial, not a sequence of retries until it yields a desired answer.

## SHARED VALUE

Candidate affected roles to identify—not supplied participants—include intended
users, trial participants, the team and anyone whose data, work or resources are
exposed. Name the real people or groups and relevant constraints before acting.
A safer test may preserve their options; delay may also impose costs. Neither
side is erased by calling the plan cooperative.

Consent, data handling, support duties, withdrawal and stopping conditions need
their own checks. A person declining participation is not evidence against them
or against the product. Private records must not become public by default.

There is no world-result to record yet. The authored reasoning changes the
**qualification of the recommendation**, not the fictional team's actual belief
or behaviour: agreement supports “the team favours launch,” not “launch is best.”

**Predeclared conditional revision branches, not events:**

<!-- parity:updates:start -->
- If the predefined task succeeds under the declared conditions: update confidence only in that task, population and conditions. Review the remaining readiness obligations before proposing a limited next step; success does not automatically authorize public launch.
- If the predefined task fails or a critical risk appears: retain that result, reject the tested readiness claim and revise or stop the launch proposal. No second trial is available under the supplied capacity without a new grant.
- If the result is ambiguous, recruitment fails or no test occurs: keep readiness unresolved; narrow the question or defer. Do not reclassify uncertainty as success.
<!-- parity:updates:end -->

Keep the original facts, proposed criterion and later observations distinct.
Any later factual revision needs its own source, date and predecessor. A
successful local document check is not the missing user trial.

## Plain decision journal — the simpler comparator

Same question, same four facts, same one-trial limit; no additional information
or model run is granted to either method.

**Question:** Should we launch next week, and what should we learn first?

**Given:** The team favours launch; a prototype works under unspecified
conditions; no independent user test has occurred; capacity permits one small
trial, whose feasibility is unspecified.

**Unknown:** Intended benefit, users, acceptable risks, obligations, and evidence
that the prototype delivers the promised result outside the team.

**Options:** Launch next week; define and seek approval for one bounded trial
before deciding; defer without spending the trial yet.

**Proposal:** Define the core task, relevant participants, available means and
unacceptable harm. If feasible, authorized and consented, run the single trial.
Predefine success, failure and ambiguity. Record completion, assistance,
difficulties and feedback. Stop on withdrawal or the declared safety boundary.

**People and timing:** Identify who benefits and who bears costs, including
people affected by delay. Review before any launch commitment; keep untested
longer-term support, privacy and reliability risks visible.

**Decision now:** Launch readiness is undetermined. No trial or approval has
been recorded. A favourable result would inform a bounded next proposal, not
automatically authorize launch; failure calls for revision, ambiguity for restraint.

**What did Emergentism add here?** It names the actual/possible distinction,
organizes the prompts and connects revision to its wider worldview. The journal
can reach the same recommendation. This authored comparison establishes neither
a winner nor equal effectiveness; effort and outcomes have not been measured.
If the framing adds no value for your task, use the journal.
