# PROP — The ontology–ethics seam: the METHOD and the VOW never meet

## The two commitments, quoted

**The METHOD** (DoF ontology §1, constraint-structured possibility):

> Forms arise across constrained degrees of freedom. A degree of freedom is a
> typed capacity for independent variation in a specified system after
> constraints and redundancies are declared. A "form" is not that freedom
> itself: it is a robust invariant, equivalence class, order parameter,
> attractor, or stable organization formed across constrained freedoms.

**The VOW** (Objective Morals and Ethics §2, the Justice envelope):

> Justice is a chosen constraint. It is not derived from `φν=1`, `P_node=ΦV`,
> natural selection, efficiency, popularity, or authority.

## The observation [I]

The framework holds two commitments side by side:

1. A **descriptive ontology**: constraints structure possibility, forms arise
   across freedoms, actuality is a D4 event, the world is constraint-shaped.
2. A **normative vow**: non-extraction, bearer-complete Justice, equal bearer
   standing, no aggregate laundering.

These are compatible — but the framework never examines their **relationship**.
Specifically:

- Can the METHOD *describe* how the VOW arose? (e.g., is Justice an emergent
  constraint that appeared at a particular μ-crossing?)
- Does the METHOD *constrain* what the VOW can demand? (e.g., if constraints
  structure possibility, are some Justice requirements impossible because the
  constraint structure doesn't permit them?)
- Can the VOW *reshape* the METHOD's constraint structure? (e.g., does
  declaring non-extraction change what forms become reachable?)
- Is the VOW itself an example of the METHOD — a constraint that, when
  declared, produces a robust form (Justice) across constrained freedoms (the
  space of possible actions)?

None of these questions are asked. The DoF ontology describes how constraints
produce forms; the Justice envelope evaluates whether actions are just. The two
operations share vocabulary (constraint, freedom, boundary, bearer) but never
engage each other's machinery.

## Why this matters philosophically

The is-ought seam is explicitly addressed (§7.6 of the Weltanschauung): the
framework declares Justice rather than deriving it. But the **ontology–ethics
seam** is different. The is-ought seam asks whether facts can produce values.
The ontology–ethics seam asks whether the framework's *own* descriptive
machinery (constraint-structured possibility, the DoF ladder, the plenitude
filter) can illuminate its *own* normative machinery (the Justice envelope,
bearer-completeness, non-extraction).

This is not an is-ought derivation. It is a question about **internal
coherence**: if the ontology says forms arise across constrained freedoms, and
the ethics says Justice is a chosen constraint, then Justice should itself be
a form arising across constrained freedoms — the constraint being the
non-extraction vow, the freedom being the space of just actions. Is that what
the framework means? If yes, the METHOD and the VOW are the same operation at
different registers (descriptive for physical constraints, normative for the
Justice constraint). If no, what is their relationship?

## The proposed refinement

Add a section to the DoF ontology (or a new document in the stack):

> **The VOW is a constraint in the METHOD's own sense.** Declaring
> non-extraction constrains the space of possible actions; forms that arise
> across that constrained space (trust, reciprocity, durable cooperation) are
> robust invariants of the constraint, exactly as the DoF ontology describes
> forms arising across physical constraints. The Justice envelope is not an
> external evaluation applied to the ontology's outputs; it is itself a
> constraint-structured possibility at the ethical register.
>
> **dies_if:** this identification is shown to confuse descriptive constraint
> (physical laws that bound what is possible) with normative constraint
> (chosen vows that bound what is permissible) — a type error, not a
> unification.

## Why this matters

Without this connection, the framework's ontology and ethics are parallel
tracks that share vocabulary but not machinery. With it, the framework gains
something rare: an ethics that is not derived from physics (the is-ought fence
holds) but is *structurally homologous* to the physics — the same
constraint-produces-form principle operating at both the descriptive and
normative registers. This would be genuinely novel [I] and would strengthen
the framework's internal coherence without violating the direction fence.
