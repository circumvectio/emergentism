# PROP — Balance rhetoric outruns balance conditionality

## The theorem [A]

00_THE_BALANCE_OPTIMUM_IS_CONDITIONAL.md proves, correctly and rigorously:

> If a separately declared domain imposes the binding budget `Φ_c+V_c=c` with
> symmetric factor prices, then `P_node=min(Φ̂₄,V₄)` and the product candidate
> `Φ̂₄V₄` both maximize at `Φ̂₄=V₄=c/2`.

The document also proves the converse warning: without those conditions, balance
may fail. Cobb–Douglas asymmetric exponents move the optimum away from equality.
Substitutable factors may make a corner optimal. A task rewarding specialization
may make imbalance optimal.

## The gap [I]

The broader corpus uses **balance language as if it were an unconditional
ideal.** The Weltanschauung calls the equator the "summit." The kernel calls the
equator the "optimum at reach = hold." The D-register scaffold places L4 (the
balance seat) as the "sole mutator." These are all rhetorically powerful — and
the mathematics behind them is real. But the mathematics says: **balance is
optimal given a declared binding budget with symmetric factor prices.** Without
that budget premise, the theorem is silent.

The gap is not a logical error. The framework's own documents are careful
individually. The gap is in the **rhetorical inheritance**: when the
Weltanschauung says "the equator is the summit," a reader who has not read
the balance-optimum document will hear "balance is the goal" without the
budget qualifier. The qualifier is load-bearing — without it, the claim is
not a theorem but an aesthetic preference for symmetry.

## Why this matters philosophically

Balance-as-ideal is one of the oldest claims in philosophy — Aristotle's mean,
Confucius's Middle Way, Goldilocks zones. The framework's contribution is to
give it a *conditional* mathematical foundation. But a conditional foundation
is weaker than an unconditional one, and the difference is philosophical, not
just technical:

- If the budget premise holds (scarce resources, symmetric prices), balance is
  genuinely optimal — a real theorem.
- If the budget premise fails (substitutable factors, asymmetric needs,
  storage), balance may be suboptimal — and pursuing it is harmful.

An ethics or a design principle built on "balance is good" without the budget
qualifier will misfire in exactly the domains where the qualifier fails.

## The proposed refinement

Not a correction — the mathematics is correct. A **visibility requirement:**

Every surface in the corpus that uses "balance," "equator," or "the middle is
optimal" as a *normative* claim (not just a mathematical identity) should carry
the qualifier: *"under a declared binding budget with symmetric factor prices."*

The canonical formula block already does this (§Meaning: "chart facts only").
The balance-optimum document already does this (§1: "a conditional resource
hypothesis"). The gap is in the surfaces that *inherit* the balance language
without inheriting the qualifier.

Minimal fix: add one line to `00_CANONICAL_FORMULA_BLOCK.md` §Meaning, after
"chart facts only":

> *The balance optimum is conditional on a declared binding budget with
> symmetric factor prices (see 00_THE_BALANCE_OPTIMUM_IS_CONDITIONAL.md).
> It is not a universal prescription.*
