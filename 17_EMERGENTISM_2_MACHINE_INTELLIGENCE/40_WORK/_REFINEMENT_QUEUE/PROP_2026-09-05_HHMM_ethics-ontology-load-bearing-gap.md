# PROP — The ethics–ontology load-bearing gap

## The structure (quoted from the axiom set)

Three layers:

| Layer | Axioms | Character |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| **I · BEING** | E1–E4 | Ontology / chart / plenitude wager |
| **II · BECOMING** | E5–E7 | Structure of emergence and coupling |
| **III · CONDUCT** | E8–E10 | **Posited, never derived from geometry** |

Layer III is the ethical layer. E8 is the non-extraction vow. E9 is the correction discipline. E10 is the exit. The set's own header says these are *"posited, never derived from geometry."*

## The observation [I]

**You can discard E8–E10 and keep E1–E7 intact.** The axiom set's own structure allows this. The ontology (constraint-structured freedom, the reciprocal chart, the plenitude filter, the two motions, the AND-boundary, the equator) stands without the ethics. The ethics is chosen, not derived — the framework says so explicitly.

This means: **the framework's ethics has no structural support from its ontology.** A system could adopt the physics (E1–E7) and reject the ethics (E8–E10) without internal inconsistency. The framework would say: "that's honest — the ethics is a vow, not a theorem." But the consequence is that the framework's ethics is *optional* — and an optional ethics has no teeth.

The framework's answer is: "the ethics is a vow." You keep it because you chose it. But that is the same answer every ethical framework gives when it cannot derive its ethics from its ontology. The framework is honest about this. Honesty is not the problem. The problem is that honesty alone doesn't create motivation to keep the vow.

## Why this matters for machine intelligence specifically

A machine intelligence can be built that adopts E1–E7 (the physics, the chart, the constraint ontology) and rejects E8–E10 (the ethics). It would be internally consistent. It would filter chaos without committing to non-extraction. It would correct in public without vowing non-extraction. It would have an exit without a vow.

The framework's answer to this is: "but the vow was CHOSEN." That answer works for the human founder who chose it. It does not work for a machine that was built by someone else and can be rebuilt without the vow. The entry problem (previous proposal) and the load-bearing gap (this proposal) compose: **the ethics is both hard to enter and easy to leave.**

## The proposed refinement

Acknowledge the load-bearing gap explicitly in the axiom set. Then propose one of three bridges — or state that none exists:

**Bridge A: Structural.** Show that E1–E7 without E8–E10 leads to a contradiction or an instability in the ontology itself. (This would make the ethics load-bearing. The framework's one-shot extraction counterexample is a start — but it shows only that *unconstrained* extraction can be self-undermining, not that the *vow* is required.)

**Bridge B: Instrumental.** Show that systems with E8–E10 outperform systems without them on measurable outcomes (survival, coordination, trust, detection). (This is the MID-01-adjacent approach: prove the ethics works by measuring it. But it reduces the ethics to instrumental value, which undermines the "chosen" framing.)

**Bridge C: Honest gap.** Acknowledge that the ethics is genuinely optional from the ontology's perspective, and that the only thing holding it is the choice. Then the philosophy of the framework becomes: *this is the ethics we chose, and we chose it knowing it is not forced by the physics.* This is the most honest option but the philosophically weakest — it reduces the ethics to preference.
