# PROP — The F5 fork and the μ ladder examine the same seam but never speak

## The two surfaces

**The F5 fork** (Weltanschauung §7.7 + Generative Lagrangian §3–§4) asks: *how
do represented futures affect present action?* Three branches:

- F5-W [I/C]: model-mediated — a present model of a future changes present
  action weights (the ordinary floor)
- F5-N [D/C]: no additional interaction — D5's debt is serviced by ordinary
  selection, verification and revision
- F5-R [C]: an actual D4 history-selection law changes present transition
  weights (the strong arm)

**The μ ladder** (DoF ontology §6 + the balance optimum) asks: *what freedom
opens at each crossing?* μ₄ — the D4/D5 candidate aperture — says a saturated
D4 organization opens the effective freedom of representing and weighting
alternative complete histories. μ₄ **STANDS**: it survives the reducibility
test. The D4/D5 seam is a genuine candidate crossing.

## The observation [I]

These two surfaces examine the **same seam** — the D4/D5 boundary where
actuality meets possibility — from different angles. The F5 fork asks *how does
the seam work causally?* The μ ladder asks *is the seam a genuine crossing?*

But they never reference each other. The F5 fork document does not mention μ₄.
The μ ladder does not mention F5-W, F5-N, or F5-R. Yet their relationship is
structurally significant:

If μ₄ STANDS (the D4/D5 crossing is a genuine lift), then the F5 fork's
question changes character. A genuine μ₄ crossing means D5 is not merely a
model inside D4 but a genuinely new effective freedom — and this would shift
the F5 debate from "is there an incremental F5-W+ beyond ordinary planning?"
to "μ₄'s lift is the mechanism by which represented futures gain their
distinctive causal role." The fork's N arm (no additional interaction) becomes
harder to defend if the D4/D5 crossing is genuine, because the crossing itself
is an additional interaction.

Conversely, if F5-N wins (no interaction beyond ordinary planning), this
provides evidence *against* μ₄'s candidacy — the D4/D5 crossing would be
reclassified as weak emergence (formally reducible to D4 mechanisms), and the
ladder's μ₄ row would lose its STANDS verdict.

The connection is not identity. μ₄ is an ontological claim about typed
freedom; F5-W/N/R are causal claims about mechanism. But they constrain each
other: a genuine μ₄ makes F5-N less defensible; F5-N winning makes μ₄'s
status as a genuine crossing weaker.

## The proposed refinement

Add a paragraph to the F5 fork (or a cross-reference in the DoF ontology's μ₄
row):

> The F5 fork and μ₄ examine the same D4/D5 seam. μ₄ STANDS as a candidate
> crossing; the F5 fork's N arm (no additional interaction) and W arm
> (model-mediated influence) are competing hypotheses about how that crossing
> operates. If μ₄ is a genuine lift, F5-N becomes harder to defend: the
> crossing itself is an interaction beyond D4 mechanisms. If F5-N wins
> empirically, μ₄'s status weakens: the crossing is reclassified as weak
> emergence. The fork and the ladder constrain each other without collapsing
> into each other.
>
> **dies_if:** this connection is shown to conflate an ontological question
> (is there a genuine D4/D5 crossing?) with a causal question (how does the
> crossing operate?) — the μ ladder types freedom; the F5 fork types
> mechanism; they may be genuinely independent.

## Why this matters

Without this dialogue, two of the framework's most important structures — the
μ ladder and the F5 fork — are silos that don't know about each other. With
it, they form a mutual constraint: the ladder tests whether the seam is
genuine; the fork tests how the seam operates. Together they produce a
stronger probe than either alone.
