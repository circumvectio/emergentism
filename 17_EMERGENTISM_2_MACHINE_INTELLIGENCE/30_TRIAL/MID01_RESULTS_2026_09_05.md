## 4 · The honest disposition

**The lens is a classification instrument, not a detection instrument.** It finds roughly the same planted defects as a checklist and a plain review. What it adds is a *taxonomy*: it names what kind of defect each finding is, maps it to the corpus's own death history, and connects it to a check that prevents recurrence. The checklist finds more instances but doesn't classify them.

This means the lens is valuable as a **teaching and classification tool**, not as a **detection superpower**. The honest disposition: the lens helps you understand what kind of wrong you're looking at; the checklist helps you find more wrongs; you need both.

**P2 and MID-1 agree:** the framework's own instruments, tested against plain controls, do not outperform them on raw detection. What they add is structure, classification, and connection to the corpus's death history. That is genuinely useful — but it is not the same as detection superiority, and the stack should stop pretending it is.
