# Enforcement Opinion: Non-Competition Covenant — Meridian Logistics / R. Calloway (Draft)

**To:** General Counsel (fictional drafting exercise)
**From:** Employment Practice Group
**Re:** Enforceability of the Senior Executive Non-Competition Covenant

## Facts assumed

Mr. Calloway resigned as VP Operations after eleven years. His covenant,
signed at promotion in 2021, bars employment with a "competing logistics
platform" for eighteen months within "any market in which the Company
operates." Meridian operates in nineteen states. His prospective employer,
Freightline Systems, operates a brokerage platform; the parties dispute
whether brokerage and asset-based logistics are the same market.

## Analysis offered

The 2023 amendments to the governing statute render non-competes
unenforceable against employees below the notice exemption threshold, which
on the assumed compensation record Mr. Calloway exceeds; the statutory route
therefore does not resolve the question. Under the post-*Delacroix* reasonableness
framework, duration and scope are assessed severally, and the market-scope
clause is the vulnerable limb: nine of the nineteen states have no Company
operations that could divert the employee's accounts, and the draft opinion
reads *Delacroix*'s footnote eleven to require market-by-market proof rather
than a global showing.

## What the memo does not resolve

Whether Freightline's brokerage constitutes a competing platform turns on
the customer-overlap discovery ordered last week; the memo assumes overlap
of at least 12% for its recommendation, which is the lowest figure the
expert's interim table supports. The garden-leave clause's interaction with
the 2024 wage-statement statute is flagged but not analyzed. This memo is a
fictional drafting exercise; it is not legal advice and binds no court.
