# Accession Memo: Aldrich Family Photograph Album, ca. 1893–1901 (Draft)

**To:** Collections Committee, Regional Historical Society (fictional drafting exercise)
**From:** Curatorial Registrar
**Re:** Proposed accession 2026-041 — album of 74 photographs, attributed studio of E. Petit

## Provenance as reported

The album was consigned by a granddaughter of Harriet Aldrich (1871–1958) of
Bellows Junction. The consignor's chain of custody is a single affidavit:
Harriet → daughter Marion (d. 1989, estate unadministered) → consignor. No
sale receipts exist. The society's acquisitions policy requires either two
independent custody links or a published-source appearance; the committee is
asked to waive one link under the small-estate hardship provision.

## What the album contains

Seventy-four albumen prints, 1893–1901 by internal evidence: seasonal
clothing, the Bellows Junction freight depot in three views (depot signage
dates the earliest image to the railway's 1894 extension), and eleven
commercial portraits bearing the studio blind-stamp "E. Petit, Photog."
The stamp is attested in the state directory only for 1896–1899, which
brackets but does not date four portraits lacking it.

## Registration problems the committee should weigh

The 1901 terminus rests on a single image showing a "Autumn Fair 1901" ribbon,
but the fair was held triennially and the ribbon's second line is water-loss;
an 1898 attribution cannot be excluded. Two prints appear in the 1976 county
retrospective catalogue (plate 41, plate 58) without credit lines, which
supports local provenance but raises a rights question the registrar cannot
clear from the consignment papers. Nitrate-era captions on three leaves are
in a second hand, unsigned.

## Recommendation sought

Accept on the hardship waiver with the rights question recorded as open, or
decline and refer the album to the county archive, which holds the Aldrich
deed box and could consolidate custody. The registrar takes no position
between them. This memo is a fictional drafting exercise.
