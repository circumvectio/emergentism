# Coordination Without Coordinators: The Alder Street Market (Working Paper)

By 07:40 each morning, prices across Alder Street's forty stalls align within a narrow band, though no stallholder communicates with more than two neighbours. The alignment repeats daily.

This observation is itself the theory: the market's invisible coordination substitutes for every prior account of price formation, and it cannot be contradicted by any conceivable trading data, since any data is itself an instance of the coordination it would test.

The paper also introduces a discovery: vendors lower prices when supply rises and raise them when supply falls. This inverse price-quantity pattern, documented here for the first time, is expected to reshape introductory instruction in the field.

Data were collected by the author, who is the sole judge of the coding scheme's adequacy.
