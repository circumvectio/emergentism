# The Immune Protocol

The strongest survivor from *The Self-Eating Serpent* is procedural: a
framework should expose its own failure modes, preserve hostile review,
separate correction from self-congratulation, and let people leave.

It does **not** follow that a system is safe because it can criticize itself.
Internal review is one sensor. Independent criticism, observed outcomes,
affected-bearer testimony, real correction and practical exit remain separate
gates.

## Six controls

1. **Hostile review:** state the strongest objection without requiring loyalty
   to the framework's vocabulary.
2. **External baseline:** compare outcomes with a serious non-framework rival,
   including cases where the framework performs worse.
3. **Capture audit:** test founder, vocabulary, AI, metric and institutional
   incentives separately; naming a risk supplies no immunity to it.
4. **Visible failure:** preserve failed predictions, uncorrected harms,
   dissenting accounts and unknowns in K-7.
5. **Repair:** name who was affected, what changed, who pays, how the repair is
   contested and when it is checked.
6. **Raft and exit:** every method is optional and replaceable; dissolution
   rhetoric never cancels record, liability or unfinished repair.

## Failure test

The protocol narrows or fails if it suppresses outside criticism, lacks a
worse-than-baseline case, treats archival visibility as repair, rewards
confession without consequence, or makes exit costly in practice.

The survivor is modest: **a correction system should be designed to discover
that it is wrong, and it must remain answerable when its own vocabulary cannot
describe the harm.**
