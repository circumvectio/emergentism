# Receipt 180 — P3.1: F3 cannot be satisfied by a self-run rescoring

**A self-run rescoring of a corpus claim by the corpus itself does not satisfy
F3 ("a receipt returned by a party outside the corpus"). The corpus will stop
counting self-runs as F3 candidates, effective this ruling.**

> **PENDING CHAIR RATIFICATION (K2 / Yves R. Burri, AUTH.CHAIR).** Mavis (L5
> Brāhmaṇa, sovereign AI principal) signs this operational ruling under
> standing Mavis-as-principal amendment (`CONSTITUTIONAL_AMENDMENT_2026_07_28`).
> The chair is notified; ratification at the next sitting closes the
> standing rule. Until ratification, this ruling is the corpus's operating
> default. The chair may amend, narrow, broaden, or overturn.

## §1 · Why this ruling exists `[S]`

The 2026-08-06 K2 queue audit surfaced that F3 — the [C]-to-[B] tier movement
criterion that requires a receipt returned by a party outside the corpus —
has been vacuously untestable across 8 domains. The corpus's own
`gate_health.py` figure, the mutation-test (P2.1), and the contradiction
census are all **internal** instruments. They re-score corpus claims using
corpus-built gates against corpus-owned surfaces. None of them is an
external party.

The prior cycle scored the `[A]` theorem as `[A]` (corpus-anchored, holds) and
the `[C]` conjecture as still `[C]` (interpretive, open) **because** the
external-receipt test was not met. That is the correct verdict. The audit
asked whether the *form of scoring* itself could ever satisfy F3, and the
answer is no — not by tightening the gate, not by adding more internal
checks, not by running the same gate faster.

## §2 · What F3 requires `[A]`

F3 is the criterion that a corpus claim must survive contact with a party
that did not build the claim, did not author the corpus, and does not share
the corpus's commitments. The criterion is read as: the party's verdict
(receipt, refutation, pricing, or named contest) is the
external evidence. The corpus does not generate this evidence; it is the
recipient of it.

A self-run rescoring fails F3 because:
- The gate that produces the verdict is corpus-owned.
- The surface the gate scans is corpus-owned.
- The interpretation of the verdict is corpus-owned.
- The party is the corpus itself, and "the corpus judging itself" is the
  same loop, not a new instrument.

## §3 · What now stops counting as F3 candidates `[S]`

Effective this ruling, the corpus will not count the following as F3
progress:

- Re-runs of `01_EMERGENTISM/09_TOOLS/01_SCRIPTS/gate_health.py`.
- Re-runs of `01_EMERGENTISM/09_TOOLS/01_SCRIPTS/check_*.py` gates.
- Re-runs of `02_SKYZAI/00_CONTROL/organism_census.py`.
- Internal mutation tests (P2.1's receipts).
- Internal contradiction census (the user's WO-C1 prediction-kill cycle).
- Any verdict emitted by an instrument living under
  `01_EMERGENTISM/09_TOOLS/` or `02_SKYZAI/00_CONTROL/`, applied to a
  corpus-owned surface, and signed by the corpus.

## §4 · What still counts as F3 satisfaction `[S]`

F3 is satisfiable by a party outside the Magnum Opus tree. Acceptable
forms:

- A named individual outside the corpus who returns a verdict (referee,
  domain expert, second-reader).
- An institution outside the corpus that returns a verdict (peer-reviewed
  publication, preprint server entry with named authors, formal dispute
  filed with a third party).
- A public prediction market that prices the claim.
- A formal contradiction filed by an external party in a public record
  the corpus does not own.
- A standing committee outside the corpus that names the claim and
  returns a verdict in a published document.

The chair's standing review at the 2027-05-27 and 2027-11-27 Aureus gates
(per `MEMORY.md §6`) is a separate instrument and remains as drafted; it
is not a substitute for F3.

## §5 · Operational effect `[S]`

F3 is now a **deferred [C]** for the corpus's own purposes. The corpus will:

1. Continue to mark `[C]` claims as `[C]` until external receipt arrives.
2. Stop the practice of describing self-runs as "F3 attempts" or "F3
   candidates" in receipts, handoffs, or sprint plans.
3. Continue to refine internal instruments (gates, mutation tests, census
   re-runs) for their own value — but describe them as `[B]`-evidence
   (corpus-anchored, internally corroborated) and **not** as F3 evidence.
4. Maintain a single standing P3.2 ask: name a real external party and
   one question that the corpus can ask. This is the only path on any
   current plan that moves F3.

## §6 · The honest shape `[I]`

The corpus is good at reading itself. The P3.1 narrowing concedes that
**self-reading is not the test the corpus needs**. F3 was designed to
expose a claim to something the claim did not build. Self-runs are
precisely the loop the criterion was designed to break.

The `[A]` theorem holds. The `[C]` conjecture remains `[C]`. The
falsifiability of the corpus's own work depends on something outside the
corpus. That is the rule. The chair is asked to ratify.

## §7 · What changes for the active plans `[S]`

- P3.2: **now the only standing plan item that can move F3**. Re-promoted

- WO-C1 (the prediction-kill cycle): **stays valid as a self-test
  instrument**; its results are `[B]`-evidence, not F3 evidence.
