# Receipt 179 — HR-1: the wrong arrow

**Nothing is ruled in this receipt.** HR-1 is the owner's, and it has blocked all
μ work since the reclassification. This packet states what the options actually
cost, and proposes one — because a third possibility has appeared that was not on
the original list.

> **OUTCOME — ADOPTED, 2026-07-29.** The owner ruled HR-1 as **Option C**: type-D
> is retired as the μ-criterion and reducibility is restored; doc 48 §4 survives
> intact as the taxonomy of boundary phenomena; no verdict changed. The ruling is
> landed in `../../05_COSMOLOGY/03_FORMAL_SYSTEM/48_THE_BOUNDARY_CROSSINGS_AND_THE_MU_CRITERION.md`
> §5 and recorded in `KSC-05`. This receipt is preserved as the packet it was —
> the ruling is the owner's, not the packet's.

## §1 · What surfaced it `[I]`

The owner's geometric observation (doc 42 §6A.5): the projection triangle sweeps
every right triangle with one leg fixed, and its two boundary limits are
**collapses** — zero width and infinite width. From which:

> **The crossing is not a freedom appearing. It is a shape ceasing.**

If boundary phenomena are *losses*, then a criterion built to detect *gains* may
be pointed at the wrong sign. That prompted the check below.

## §2 · Doc 48 §4 taxonomised the wrong arrow `[A]`

Its three rows:

| Row | Kind | What it does | dim |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ℝ → ℝP¹` | compactification | adds one point | 1 → 1 |
| `ℂ → ℂP¹` | compactification | adds one point | 2 → 2 |
| `ℝP¹ → Ĉ` | **complexification** | adds a dimension | 1 → 2 |

Two are compactifications and one is not — they are **not one kind**. And all
three point **outward**: you add boundary or dimension to a given space.

**Every μ points the other way.** `D_n → D_{n+1}` is a **lift**, equipped with a
**forgetful map** back down — which is why `KSC-05` demands a *recovery* test at
all. Doc 48 built a taxonomy of **boundaries** and then applied it to **lifts**.

## §3 · Neither candidate test discriminates `[A]`

**`dim_ℝ` gain** — kills four of five. It is a sound instrument for
compactifications, where it correctly reports *no freedom*; it is the wrong
instrument for lifts, and lifts are what μ's are.

**Non-trivial fibre** — the repair the reclassification agent proposed. Checked
against all five:

| | recovery map | fibre | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| μ₀ | quotient D1 to one class | everything (terminality) | passes **trivially** |
| μ₁ | forget the relation | all relations | passes |
| μ₂ | `ForgetState` | all states on `ℋ` | passes |
| μ₃ | marginalise the record | all matching records | passes |
| μ₄ | suppress the option model | all option models | passes |

**It passes all five.** Of course it does — a lift that added nothing would not be
a lift. One test kills 4/5, the other kills 0/5. **Neither discriminates.**

## §4 · What actually did the work — and the corpus already owned it `[A]`

Re-read the two failures for their *load-bearing* step:

- **μ₂** fell on: *exhibit a state-level parameter outside `span_ℝ B(ℋ)_sa` —
  and Busch's POVM form of Gleason's theorem forbids exactly that.*
- **μ₃** fell on: *either the ordering was externally supplied (hence below the
  crossing), or Page–Wootters recovers it as a structure of the D3 state (hence
  present in the lower description).*

Both are **reducibility** arguments. Neither turns on dimension. Dimension was
decoration on a reduction result.

And the test is **already published**, in the corpus's own GP-MU packets:

> *"formal equivalence/reduction accounts for the claimed novelty"*

Doc 48 replaced a working test with a proxy, and the proxy is what broke.

## §5 · The proposal — three options, with costs `[S]`

**Option A — `dim_ℝ`, as §4 literally reads.** 1 of 5 μ's survives. Withdraws
doc 48 §5.2's own vindication of `KSC-22` and takes μ₁ with it. Not chart-invariant,
which `KSC-28` forbids. **Cost: the criterion voids its own worked example.**

**Option B — cardinality / "genuinely larger", as §5.2 applies it.** 3 of 5
survive. Keeps §5.2 but leaves §4's table stating something the document does not
use. **Cost: the published table remains wrong.**

**Option C — recommended; ADOPTED by the owner 2026-07-29. Retire type-D as the μ-criterion; restore reducibility.**

```text
A crossing is a candidate μ iff the higher description is NOT formally
reducible to the frozen lower one — the GP-MU packets' own standing kill.
```

Doc 48 §4 **survives intact as what it actually is**: a correct taxonomy of
**boundary** phenomena (totality repair, order loss, dimension gain), governing
compactifications — where it is right, and where the owner's geometric reading
lands. It simply stops being the test for lifts.

**Option C changes no verdict.** μ₂ and μ₃ still fail, on the arguments that
actually carried them. μ₀ still owes its distinction discriminator, and its
recovery test remains vacuous by terminality. μ₁ and μ₄ still stand. What changes
is that the criterion stops being wrong about *why*, and stops being propagable
into ~112 files in a form that would not survive contact.
