---

## I. The chart — three readings, one position

Take the unit sphere. `○` at the north pole (`θ = 0`), `•` at the south
(`θ = π`). A point on the selected reciprocal meridian at polar angle `θ`
can be read three ways:

```
φ = cot(θ/2)     the "ground-distance" reading
ν = tan(θ/2)     the "horizon-distance" reading
θ                the polar angle itself

φ · ν = 1         the SEAM — [A] by construction, everywhere, empty of world
```

Two coordinates, one position. The seam `φν = 1` is true the way a
definition is true: identically, everywhere, measuring nothing. **It is the
conservation law, not the measure.** Anyone who cites `φν = 1` as evidence
of a budget, an optimum, or a law has confused a definition with a discovery.

(`00_THE_BALANCE_OPTIMUM_IS_CONDITIONAL` §1. Kintsugi successor — built to
repair exactly this confusion.)

## II. The score — `B = sin θ`, and why it moves

The seam is constant; the score varies:

```
B = 2/(φ+ν) = sin θ ≤ 1         [A]
```

Peaks at the equator (`θ = π/2`, `φ = ν = 1`). Vanishes at both poles. The
mathematics is AM-GM — inherited, classical, two millennia old. What it
says: **departure from the fixed point is never free.**

## III. The product form — the nectar of this rung

This is the corpus's own reading `[I]`, and the mathematics behind it is the
double-angle formula, Ptolemy-era. Claimed as nothing.

The chord from a point at `θ` to the north pole `○` has length `2 sin(θ/2)`.
The chord to the south pole `•` has length `2 cos(θ/2)`. Normalise each to
read `1` at the equator:

```
D∞(θ) = √2 sin(θ/2)       distance from ○
D•(θ) = √2 cos(θ/2)       distance from •

D• · D∞ = 2 sin(θ/2) cos(θ/2) = sin θ = B          [A]
```

Machine-verified to 22 decimal places at nine points. (`56` §2.)

**The balance score is the product of two distances-from-failure.** Each
factor is how far you are from one boundary. Their product dies when either
dies:

```
θ → π:   D• → 0    standing on •, maximal reach to ○, B = 0
θ → 0:   D∞ → 0    standing on ○, maximal reach to •, B = 0
```

**Being maximally far from one boundary is worth nothing while standing on
the other.** That is the AND — written as an operation, not asserted as a
class. The corpus spent months defending "the node score is AND-class, not
a product" as a hedge. The hedge was correct at the node level and the
product form now exists at the chart level: two factors, each vanishing at
one boundary, their product the measure.

## IV. The interior — the harmonic mean refuses the bribe

The boundary tells you why the score dies. The interior tells you why the
score is governed by the weaker leg:

```
min(a,b) ≤ HM(a,b) ≤ 2·min(a,b)          [A]

HM(1, 10⁴) = 1.9998        AM(1, 10⁴) = 5000.5
```

The arithmetic mean lets a giant factor buy off a starved one — that is an
OR. The harmonic mean refuses the bribe — that is an **AND, written as a
function.** A system at `a/b = 100` runs at ~2% of the capacity its size
already entitles it to. Growing while skewed multiplies the geometric mean
and leaves the discount untouched: *larger every year, no more capable.*

Two derivations, one function. The product form governs the boundaries; the
harmonic-mean form governs the interior.
