## III. The typed chart factorization

The mathematics here is the classical double-angle formula. The factorization
is `[A]` inside the selected chart and carries no separate interpretation.

The chord from a point at `θ` to `p_N` has length `2 sin(θ/2)`. The chord
to `p_S` has length `2 cos(θ/2)`. Normalise each to read `1` at the
equator:

```
d_N(θ) = √2 sin(θ/2)      distance from the named north chart pole `p_N`
d_S(θ) = √2 cos(θ/2)      distance from the named south chart pole `p_S`

d_S · d_N = 2 sin(θ/2) cos(θ/2) = sin θ = B          [A]
```

Machine-verified to 22 decimal places at nine points. (`56` §2.)

The two factors are normalized chord distances to the named chart poles. Their
product tends to zero when either factor tends to zero:

```
θ → π:   d_S → 0   at `p_S`, maximal chart reach to `p_N`, B = 0
θ → 0:   d_N → 0   at `p_N`, maximal chart reach to `p_S`, B = 0
```

This is boundary behavior of a one-parameter chart, not an AND operation. The
factors are constrained by `d_S² + d_N² = 2`; they are not independent inputs,
and vanishing at zero does not establish non-compensation. The identity stays
on the chart and supplies no node aggregator.

## IV. The interior — the harmonic mean bounds compensation

The boundary tells you why the score dies. The interior tells you why the
score is governed by the weaker leg:

```

HM(1, 10⁴) = 1.9998        AM(1, 10⁴) = 5000.5
```

Unlike the arithmetic mean, the harmonic mean remains within a factor of two
of the weaker argument. That exact weaker-argument bound is the survivor. It is
a bounded-compensation statement, not a mathematical no-compensation or AND
theorem: fixing the weaker input and increasing the stronger one still raises
the harmonic mean, but never beyond twice the weaker input. The bound does not
by itself define system capacity or entitlement.

The chart factorization describes the boundary limits; the harmonic-mean bound
describes two independent positive inputs. They are separate typed facts.
