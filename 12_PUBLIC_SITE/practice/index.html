The practice · one consequential act at a time

Run the
Soul Loop.

Model one reachable future, name your actual means, receipt the commitment, receive what the world returns, and update instead of protecting the story. No membership and no metaphysical belief required.

[I] practical grammar — proposed, usable, and corrigible [S] typed receipts keep intention separate from outcome

The primary practice

Five steps. Two receipts.

The loop keeps three gaps visible: territory versus model, intention versus performed action, and expected versus received consequence. A modeled future can influence the present only through an actual model token and an actual act.

01

Model D4 token · D5 represented possibilities

Describe the current state and at least two reachable futures. State what would show your model is wrong.

  • CURRENT observed state
  • FUTURES desired, feared, alternatives
  • CONFIDENCE evidence tier
  • KILL disconfirming observation
02

Means D4 actual capacities

Name what is available now. Imagining a move does not supply the body, time, access, permission, or custody needed to perform it.

  • BODY / TOOLS actual capacities
  • TIME / ACCESS present constraints
  • AUTHORITY mandate and scope
  • CUSTODY what you may alter
03

Commitment D4 action receipt q

Write the act before performing it. Record who authorizes it, who acts, who may bear the consequence, and how it can be contested.

  • ACT concrete and time-bounded
  • PRINCIPAL mandate and scope
  • BEARERS every materially affected party
  • CONSENT given, absent, or impossible
  • PAYER / BENEFICIARY predicted
  • ALTERNATIVES rejected and why
  • REVERSIBILITY what cannot be undone
  • CONTEST / EXIT remedy path
04

Outcome D4 environment receipt r

The selector does not manufacture its consequence. Record what the world—including other agents—actually returned, even when the action was blocked or nothing changed.

  • OBSERVED expected versus actual
  • STATUS enacted, blocked, or null
  • COSTS actual payer and beneficiary
  • SURPRISES harms and side effects
05

Return update map and mapper

Update the model and the habit that selected the act. Repair harm before narrating success. Retain, modify, or abandon the rule.

  • MODEL Δ what belief changed
  • SELECTOR Δ what habit changed
  • REMEDY restoration owed
  • NEXT smaller test or withdrawal

Type fence: actual modeling, ranking, commitment, action, and receipts are D4. The alternatives they represent are D5 possible content. Anticipated futures affect present selection through those actual representations—not by a physical signal from the future.

The Justice gate

Before commitment, name every bearer.

Do not hide a harmed person inside a positive total. Name every materially affected bearer, their consent, the predicted payer and beneficiary, rejected alternatives, authorization and custody, reversibility, contest, remedy, and exit.

Voluntary sacrifice is a distinct costly act. Record whose sacrifice it is, whether consent is informed and revocable, and what becomes irreversible. A collective may never demand sacrifice as proof of ethics or relabel it strict syntropy.

Take it with you

The worksheet — copy it before the act.

Use one copy per consequential act. Fill the commitment section before acting and the outcome section only after the world answers. Never silently rewrite the prediction after seeing the result.

Pocket gate

Who authorized this consequence, who acts, who bears it, who pays, who benefits—and how can each bearer contest, recover, or exit?

Supporting practices

Attention, correction, non-extraction.

The sitting checks maps against present experience. The personal trial record grades claims, names kill criteria, and leaves refutations legible. The dyadic check asks whether a gain preserves both the acting individual and the sustaining whole under the Justice gate.

None asks you to believe the metaphysics. If the framework fails, keep any discipline that survives and record the rest.
