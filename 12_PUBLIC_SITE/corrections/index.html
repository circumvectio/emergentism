What genuinely died (3 claims)

The exclusions ARE the normalisation

KILLED · the one arithmetic kill

The claim that the two syntactic exclusions in the generative base (no ιι, no leading ι) are the classical continued-fraction normalisation. They are not: the positivity condition and the a_n ≥ 2 normalisation are different jobs. The bijection is still real (it is Hardy & Wright Theorem 162); the expository identification is false.

D5 is where teleology enters the ladder

KILLED · ill-typed fatally · KSC-23

Teleology is bound to the W7e wager leg as roleAffinity only — never identity, exclusive domain, causal generator, or derivation. Mapping it to D5 makes the ladder load-bearing for the wager, which is the explicit kill condition.

Antipodality — • and ○ at chordal distance 2

KILLED · the corpus's own successor breaks the metric

The corpus declares the chordal metric as fixed structure. But S = [[1,1],[0,1]] — the corpus's own successor operator — does not preserve the round metric, and S(•) = 1. You cannot declare a metric as structure and then operate on those points with a group that doesn't preserve it. What survives: • ≠ ○ (distinctness, type-safe) and the opposite-refusals reading on absorption, not geometry.

Not "undefined" (understates — suggests an empty slot). Not "forbidden" (overstates — suggests an agent doing the forbidding). Foreclosed: no such element exists, and the field proves it. 0 · y = N and 0 · y = 0 ⟹ N = 0. There is no slot.
