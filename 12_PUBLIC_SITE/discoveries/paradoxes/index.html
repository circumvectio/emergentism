The discovery

A paradox can contain a register collision.

Here is the move, once, so you can use it yourself. Every description of the world runs in a register — a level of bookkeeping. There is the seam register, where things are true by construction, the way a fraction times its reciprocal is one. There is the score register, where things vary and can be measured. And there is the node register, where a whole thing either lives or dies. We keep these three rigorously apart everywhere on this site — because confusing them is not just our occupational hazard. It is philosophy's oldest occupational hazard.

Some paradoxes arise when one sentence silently straddles distinct levels of description. The liar mixes object-language and its truth assessment; separating those levels exposes the self-reference, though it does not by itself settle every semantic treatment. Zeno mixes continuous motion with an infinite description of intervals; the register lens clarifies the bookkeeping, while calculus supplies the standard mathematical account. The ship of Theseus asks “the same” without fixing a criterion—same parts, history, function, or maintained pattern. In each case, separation improves the question before anyone claims an answer.

One lens, tried in twenty-six cases. Not all of them yield the same verdict, and every reading needs its own evidence and way to be wrong. A sample:

01Zeno's arrow

Process and interval inventory are different descriptions. The lens clarifies the setup; convergent-series analysis does the mathematical work.

02The liar

A claim fused with its own truth assessment. Separating object-language from metalanguage exposes the self-reference; it does not uniquely choose a theory of truth.

03The ship of Theseus

"Same" without a register is not a question. Same parts: no. Same self-maintaining pattern: yes. Both answers are true — of different registers.

04Free will vs. determinism

Physics describes causal trajectories; an agency model describes represented alternatives and selection. The distinction can dissolve one equivocation, but does not by itself prove libertarian freedom.

05Is and ought — handled differently

The one gap we do not claim to cross with logic. Conditional models can compare consequences; the move to Justice remains a vow, not a derivation.

26…and twenty-one more

The full suite — Fermi, the measurement problem, the hard problem, the problem of evil, more — is public, each with its tier and its kill-criterion, in the paradox library.

If a 2,400-year-old knot opens in a paragraph, suspect the key — then check whether it turns.

Why this matters to you: the register move is not only for antiquity's puzzles. A recurring argument can hide two questions under one word: “is this working?” (performance), “is this viable?” (persistence), or “is this what we agreed?” (authorization). Split the sentence into its jobs before fighting about the answer.

And the honest frame: dissolving a paradox is not the same as solving it, and a lens that opens many locks might be a skeleton key or might be a hammer that makes everything look like a nail. That is a real risk, we name it, and it is exactly why every dissolution is graded separately and kept on trial rather than declared. The readings are ours; the humility is enforced.
