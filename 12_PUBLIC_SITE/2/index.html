01
The Boundary

Where Flat Approximation Fails

A μ-limit is the framework's label for a candidate transition between registers. A valid crossing record must name what saturates, what new freedom becomes observable, how the lower register is recovered, the reduction status, evidence tier, prediction, and kill criterion.

D5 names modeled possibility and selection; D4 names embodied action and the resulting factual record. Enactment is represented by a typed selector and commitment receipt, followed by a separate environmental outcome. It is not an instantaneous scalar-sampling operation and is not identified with quantum collapse.

Projective completion can serve as one mathematical illustration of a local chart acquiring a global completion. That result stays in geometry. It does not establish that a one-dimensional physical register must become a two-dimensional one.

02
Stereographic Return

Projective Completion as an Illustration

On the Riemann sphere S² ≅ ℂℙ¹, the standard stereographic chart maps z = 0 and z = ∞ to opposite poles and |z| = 1 to the equator. A ray with magnitude tending to infinity approaches the omitted pole of that chart; a second chart describes a neighborhood of that point.

This is projective completion: infinity becomes a regular point of the completed complex line. Emergentism borrows the local-chart/global-object contrast as an analogy for model limits. The mathematics proves the completion, not the μ-ladder.

The apparent escape is a chart effect. Calling that a “return” is visual language; it should not be mistaken for a physical trajectory, retrocausal process, or new degree of freedom.

03
Dimensional Transition

D2 Is the Selected Configuration Register

In the current D0→D6 scaffold, D1 names distinction and D2 names configuration: relations among distinctions can now be arranged. D3 names transformation and persistence. These are selected modeling registers, not extra spacetime dimensions.

The candidate crossing from configuration to transformation asks when relations acquire reproducible dynamics or persistence. A sequence growing without bound does not, by that fact alone, change dimension; the old “D2 forces D3” wording transferred a geometric picture beyond its proof.

Each proposed crossing must remain agnostic between reduction statuses until evidence resolves it. A successful reduction reclassifies the crossing as weak emergence without destroying the usefulness of the scaffold; missing reduction alone proves nothing stronger.

05
A Physical Use of the Same Chart

The Bounded Coordinate Can Parameterize Velocity

Suda's bounded coordinate of the line, u(x) = (x−1)/(x+1), squeezes the whole positive ray into the open interval (−1, 1): the unit x = 1 sits at the centre, and the two ends — zero and infinity — press against ±1 but never arrive. The chart identity u(x) = tanh(½ ln x) is analytic. Special relativity uses the same hyperbolic map as β = v/c = tanh(w). If one selects x = k², where k is a one-dimensional Doppler factor, then ½ ln x = ln k = w and u(x)=β.

u(k²) = (k²−1)/(k²+1) = tanh(ln k) = β = v/c

Under that selected dictionary, the multiplicative coordinate is x=k², the additive coordinate is rapidity w=½ ln x, and the bounded coordinate is the velocity ratio β. Rest is x=1, β=0; as x→∞, β→1. The algebraic dictionary is [A]. Numerical evaluation checks an implementation but adds no evidence to the identity.

Why this restraint matters: a shared tanh chart is not a shared cause. Calling its boundary a μ-limit is an [I] analogy; claiming that saturation produces a new degree of freedom, that c is produced by such a crossing, or that experience travels at c is [C]. The coordinate fact survives if the emergence correspondence is removed.
