Emergentism · A worldview for finite beings

Reality exceeds every map. We still have to act.

Emergentism is a fallibilist worldview and practical compass. It offers a way to distinguish actuality from possibility, match possible futures to the means truly available, keep everyone affected visible, and let observed consequences revise the map.

Finity is its first practice: orient by the longest responsible horizon, then choose the shortest observable review boundary—reversible where possible—that can return an honest result.

The worldview

Five questions.
One revisable map.

Emergentism connects ontology, epistemology, methodology, axiology, and teleology without pretending that one elegant answer proves the next. Each question keeps its own burden of proof.

01

What is happening?

Distinguish territory from map, and actuality from possibility.

02

How do we know?

Grade claims, reconstruct rivals, and ask what the evidence can bear.

03

How do we test and move?

Join a model to lawful means, bounded action, and an observed outcome.

04

What matters?

Keep affected bearers, cost, risk, consent, repair, and exit visible.

05

What do we attempt?

Expand durable possibility without hiding who pays—a chosen orientation, not a derived fact.

Ontology becomes practice only when consequence can answer it.

The research programme

Clarity first.
Then world contact.

Emergentism separates what is exact inside a construction, what interprets those constructions, and what remains conjectural. Internal coherence is where inquiry begins; contact with the world decides what survives.
