A map that prints its own distortions.

Every worldview is a projection of a territory that cannot be flattened without lying. This one marks where it lies.

The legend

Every worldview is a finite representation of a territory it does not become. The cartographic comparison is a reading; its mathematical anchor is exact — Gauss's Theorema Egregium (1827) makes Gaussian curvature intrinsic, so no isometry exists from an open patch of a sphere to a plane.

Cylinders and cones DO flatten exactly. The obstruction is nonzero Gaussian curvature, not curvature in the everyday sense. The theorem names which regions must tear, not that all curved things must.

  • [A] proved within stated definitions or hypotheses

  • [S] selected inside a declared framework
  • [I] interpretation, not derivation

  • [D] unresolved, staged, or awaiting a test
What this map does not claim
  • Not the gestalt of the thing-in-itself. The noumenon is never grasped as an object, and even a globe is a map.
  • Not first. A bounded map whose limits are drawn is Kant's critical project, the Tractatus, and the via negativa. What is distinctive is the instrument, not the idea.
  • Not more accurate. No projection is more accurate simpliciter. This map declares its purpose and its distortions; it does not claim to escape them.
