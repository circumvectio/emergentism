## 1. Pure-qubit geometry `[A]`

For a chosen orthonormal basis, a pure qubit ray can be written

\[
|\psi\rangle=
\cos(\theta/2)|0\rangle+e^{i\zeta}\sin(\theta/2)|1\rangle,
\]

with ray space `ℂP¹≅S²`. The chosen basis supplies the pole labels. Mixed qubit
states fill the Bloch ball.

On the open polar chart define

\[
\varphi=\cot(\theta/2),\qquad
\nu=\tan(\theta/2).
\]

Then

\[
\varphi\nu=1,
\qquad
\varphi=|\alpha|/|\beta|,
\qquad
\nu=|\beta|/|\alpha|.
\]

This is a reciprocal ratio identity. It ignores the azimuthal phase `ζ`, so
`(φ,ν)` does not parameterize the full pure-qubit state. It is not the Born
normalization

\[
|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1,
\]

and it carries no independent quantum conservation law.

The equator `θ=π/2` is the common geometric locus where
`|α|²=|β|²=1/2` and `φ=ν=1`. This statement is basis-relative. It does not show
that the equator maximizes every quantum resource, algorithmic advantage,
social value, or moral good.

## 3. Independent rapidity identity `[A]`

There is a genuine, separate coordinate correspondence in one-dimensional
special-relativistic kinematics. For a massive particle with rapidity `w`, set

\[
\varphi=e^w,\qquad \nu=e^{-w}.
\]

Using `E=mc²cosh w` and `pc=mc²sinh w` gives

\[
\varphi=\frac{E+pc}{mc^2},\qquad
\nu=\frac{E-pc}{mc^2},
\]

and therefore

\[
\varphi\nu=1
\iff
E^2-(pc)^2=(mc^2)^2.
\]

Also,

\[
\frac{\varphi+\nu}{2}=\gamma=\frac{E}{mc^2},\qquad
\frac{\varphi-\nu}{2}=\frac{pc}{mc^2},
\]

so AM–GM becomes `E≥mc²`, with equality at rest. If
`cot(θ/2)=e^w`, then

\[
\cos\theta=\tanh w=v/c,
\qquad
\sin\theta=\operatorname{sech}w=1/\gamma=d\tau/dt.
\]

These are exact coordinate identities for the massive one-dimensional
mass-shell. They do not identify qubits with particles, explain relativity,
derive the D-ladder, or attach moral meaning to velocity. The massless null
boundary is not obtained by substituting a photon into the normalized massive
hyperbola.
