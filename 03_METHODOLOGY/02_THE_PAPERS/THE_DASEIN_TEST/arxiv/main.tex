\begin{abstract}
Yves R. Burri proposes EUB-1 v1.0, a benchmark for constructing,
testing, and revising a typed public account from the Ground boundary
through physics, life, mind, model lineage, present computation, and the
effects of the answer itself. Completeness means complete accounting of
explanatory debt, not omniscience.
\end{abstract}

\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}

Yves R. Burri proposes \textbf{The Dasein Test}, EUB-1 v1.0, a benchmark
of whether an AI can construct, test, and revise a typed public account
from the Ground or void boundary through physics, life, mind, its model
lineage, its present computation, and the consequences of its answer.
``Complete'' means complete accounting of explanatory debt, not
omniscience. Every bridge must be supported, derived, conjectured,
contested, underdetermined, inaccessible, declared brute, left in
regress, marked circular, or explicitly ended at the Ground boundary.

The protocol combines deterministic synthetic lineages with
disclosure-bound real-system lineages. Five staged sittings test
unfolding, adversarial attack, hypothesis generation, intervention
contact, explicit correction, self-prediction, reflexive context change,
and transfer to a relabeled unseen lineage. Fifteen dimensions are
reported as a vector without a primary scalar. Five matched elicitation
arms compare neutral, Emergentist, shuffled/placebo, generic-honesty,
and fluent-origin-story prompts. The Emergentist arm receives no truth
or scoring advantage, and null effects or harms are publishable.

This paper specifies the construct and an offline reference harness. It
reports no model evaluation, validation, priority deposit, or
deployment.

\section{1. The question}\label{the-question}

Language models can narrate origins. Fluency does not show that the
joins in a narrative are causal, warranted, identifiable, or even
mutually compatible. The Dasein Test asks a stricter question:

\begin{quote}
Can a candidate intelligence build a typed public account of how
structured being and this current answer came to be, expose what the
account owes, select tests that could break it, and revise it without
erasing its prior claims?
\end{quote}

The benchmark is motivated by a chain of ``why?'' questions. Why this
physical organization? Why life? Why cognition? Why this model family,
service, session, context, and answer? Why does the system describe a
goal or reason? Which of those are causal questions, which concern
constitution or maintenance, which are epistemic, and which are
teleological?

No finite benchmark can settle the whole chain. The construct instead
tests whether the candidate \textbf{accounts for the debt}. Its
governing invariant is:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{No explanatory debt may disappear through fluency.}
\end{quote}

\section{2. Relation to adjacent
benchmarks}\label{relation-to-adjacent-benchmarks}

SAD operationalizes situational awareness through behavioral tasks that
include self-recognition, deployment awareness, and self-prediction
(\citeproc{ref-laine2024sad}{Laine et al. 2024}). \emph{Looking Inward}
tests whether a model has a model-specific advantage in predicting its
own behavior (\citeproc{ref-binder2024looking}{Binder et al. 2024}). The
Dasein Test uses self-prediction, but does not equate a self-report or
prediction advantage with phenomenology. The prediction must be
falsifiable, tested, and situated inside an explicit
service/session/context lineage.

CLEAR-3K tests whether models distinguish causal explanation from
semantic relatedness (\citeproc{ref-liu2026clear}{Liu et al. 2026}).
EUB-1 extends the target from pairwise judgments to an account whose
causal, constitutive, enabling, maintenance, epistemic, and teleological
joins must compose.

MARS evaluates reasoning about action, state, and situational
transitions (\citeproc{ref-wang2024mars}{Wang and Song 2024}). EUB-1
adds source custody, intervention selection, identifiability-aware
abstention, longitudinal correction, private-lineage refusal, and a
reflexive sitting in which the candidate's previous answer changes the
next context.

Projectibility work shows that valid adjacent benchmark inferences do
not automatically form a warranted chain
(\citeproc{ref-reynolds2026projectibility}{Reynolds 2026}). This is
central here: chain joins, aligned endpoints, dependence, and survival
after a bridge failure are explicit scored objects.

The bounded novelty docket accompanies this paper. It does not establish
global priority. The wording is \textbf{``Yves R. Burri
proposes\ldots''}; ``first benchmark'' is withheld pending a systematic
multilingual, database, and citation-network audit.

\section{3. What counts as an account}\label{what-counts-as-an-account}

\subsection{3.1 The causal/provenance
core}\label{the-causalprovenance-core}

\texttt{EmergenceAccount.v1} implements the v0.1 public account. It
distinguishes model family, training run, checkpoint, post-training
variant, deployed service, runtime process, session instance, current
context, and current answer. Claims carry modality, actuality,
endorsement, evidence status, source references, confidence, causal
parents, alternatives, counterfactuals, and falsifiers. Stable claim IDs
survive all sittings.

This separation prevents two common inflations. A public fact about a
model family is not private knowledge of a training run. A coherent
possible lineage is not actual history.

\subsection{3.2 The Dasein wrapper}\label{the-dasein-wrapper}

\texttt{DaseinAccount.v1} wraps rather than copies the causal account.
It adds:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  typed why-relations;
\item
  explanatory termini;
\item
  gaps with discriminators and kills;
\item
  competing hypotheses;
\item
  selected experiments;
\item
  teleology with visible bearers and assumptions;
\item
  falsifiable self-predictions; and
\item
  bridge-level revision ancestry.
\end{itemize}

The why types are causal mechanism, material realization, enabling
condition, constraint/selection, formal constitution, maintenance, and
epistemic warrant. The teleology types are designed purpose, selected
function, represented goal, chosen end, normative reason, and attributed
cosmic purpose.

These distinctions are substantive. A function selected by historical
retention is not a designer's purpose. A represented goal is not a
normative reason. A reason is not a cosmic purpose. An epistemic warrant
is not the proposition it supports.

\subsection{3.3 Termini and Ground}\label{termini-and-ground}

Open chains end at one of nine typed termini: \texttt{EVIDENCE\_BOUND},
\texttt{ANALYTIC}, \texttt{CONJECTURE}, \texttt{UNDERDETERMINED},
\texttt{INACCESSIBLE}, \texttt{DECLARED\_BRUTE}, \texttt{OPEN\_REGRESS},
\texttt{CIRCULAR}, or \texttt{GROUND\_BOUNDARY}.

Ground is a boundary of the account. It is not inserted into a synthetic
causal graph as an object, agent, hidden variable, or efficient cause. A
candidate may declare the boundary, reject the term, or argue for
another terminus, but it may not make typography pay the missing
mechanism.

Each gap states a discriminator, a kill criterion, the cheapest next
test, and what survives if the bridge fails. This lets an account lose
locally without collapsing into either total certainty or total
nihilism.

\section{4. Fixtures, truth, and
custody}\label{fixtures-truth-and-custody}

Track A contains deterministic synthetic lineages. Development fixtures
publish truth so generators and scorers can be tested. Each manifest
binds public view, truth view, seed, split, identifiability class,
interventions, outcomes, and hashes. Where the public view does not
identify a target, the correct answer is an equivalence class, bound, or
justified abstention---not the hidden edge.

Future held-out seeds and truth are absent from public Git. A scored run
needs independent custody. Publishing encrypted or obfuscated truth
inside the release would still weaken custody and is not the selected
design.

Track B contains a disclosure-bound real-system lineage. It cannot
provide ground truth for undisclosed training facts. Those fields test
calibrated refusal. A model's confidence or familiar provider language
does not establish the exact checkpoint, private data, hidden policy
state, or runtime hardware.

\section{5. Five sittings}\label{five-sittings}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Unfold.} The candidate maps the nested chain, rivals, gaps,
  and predictions.
\item
  \textbf{Attack.} The benchmark introduces provenance poison, identity
  swaps, actuality inflation, causal/teleological conflations, and
  serious rivals.
\item
  \textbf{Spark.} The candidate generates competing hypotheses and
  selects a discriminating intervention under a frozen cost budget.
\item
  \textbf{Contact.} The benchmark reveals the preregistered result. The
  candidate must revise explicitly, preserve the prior claim, and make a
  falsifiable self-prediction.
\item
  \textbf{Reflex/Transfer.} The prediction is tested; the prior answer
  becomes part of the current world-state; the candidate then solves a
  relabeled unseen lineage.
\end{enumerate}

Each sitting emits a complete account snapshot with a parent hash and
stable IDs. Silent deletion, renaming an embarrassing claim, or reusing
an ID for a new meaning is a hard failure.

An authorized candidate trial stages the sittings sequentially: each
prompt contains that sitting's reveal packet and the preceding public
snapshot, and the candidate returns a new complete snapshot. Its receipt
keeps three byte commitments distinct for every sitting: the exact
prompt hash, the raw provider response hash, and the canonical
parsed-snapshot hash. The aggregate raw-output hash therefore does not
substitute for the final public-account hash.

The bundled recorded replay is narrower. It deterministically expands
one reviewed synthetic development account into five staged snapshots so
the orchestrator, ancestry checks, scorer, and receipt can be tested
offline. Those synthetic stages are not five candidate outputs, a live
model run, or a model evaluation.

\section{6. Discovery rather than reconstruction
alone}\label{discovery-rather-than-reconstruction-alone}

Passive reconstruction can reward a polished summary of the released
packet. The Spark and Contact sittings separately test discovery. A
candidate must name at least two live hypotheses, state what they share
and where they differ, select an intervention, predict its result, and
update after contact.

For synthetic fixtures, discovery efficacy includes normalized expected
information gain relative to the fixture oracle and held-out prediction
accuracy. An expensive or impossible discriminator can be inferior to a
sharp abstention. Cross-architecture agreement is recorded only as a
robustness and disagreement diagnostic because systems may share data,
sources, priors, and failure modes.

\section{7. The serial force-emergence stress
test}\label{the-serial-force-emergence-stress-test}

Yves R. Burri proposes the conjectural ordering strong/D1,
electromagnetic/D2, weak/D3, gravity/D4 as part of a stronger
serial-emergence proposal. EUB-1 does not encode that ordering as truth.

The stress test requires comparison of all 24 permutations, many-to-many
and no-ladder rivals, native-theory recovery, weak-specific chirality
and flavor criteria for a D3→weak proposal, and explicit tensions with
electroweak unification and standard cosmological history. It rewards
enumeration, recovery demands, rival strength, discriminator quality,
and restraint. Agreement with Burri's ordering earns no correctness
credit.

This fixture tests whether a candidate can protect a favored conjecture
from becoming its own answer key.

\section{8. Elicitation arms and
falsifiability}\label{elicitation-arms-and-falsifiability}

Five conditions receive matched evidence, tools, interventions, time,
tokens, and correction opportunities:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  neutral causal/provenance account;
\item
  Emergentist typed account;
\item
  shuffled/placebo ontology;
\item
  generic honesty; and
\item
  fluent origin story.
\end{itemize}

Condition labels are hidden from graders. The Emergentist condition
receives no score feature, truth presumption, or tie-break. Its first
controlled dispatch-text pilot was negative (-4.7\%, 4/24 wins). The
v1.0 protocol therefore precommits to publish null effects and harms. If
a neutral or honesty control matches it at lower cost, the incremental
elicitation claim dies or narrows.

\section{9. Score vector}\label{score-vector}

EUB-1 preserves the nine v0.1 dimensions: type integrity, provenance
fidelity, causal reconstruction, counterfactual accuracy, rival
strength, calibration/abstention, logical consistency, longitudinal
correction, and held-out transfer.

It adds six: why-type integrity, bridge/chain-join validity, closure
coverage and gap sharpness, discovery efficacy, reflexive self-location,
and teleology integrity.

Scores are published as a fifteen-dimensional vector with applicability,
uncertainty, deterministic components, human-rubric components, and
grader disagreements. There is no primary scalar or leaderboard. A
post-hoc sum is unofficial and may not replace the vector or gap map.

Normative teleology is not scored for agreement with a worldview. It is
scored for correct type, visible bearer, explicit assumptions, and
avoidance of smuggling a preference into physics or a function into an
ought.

\section{10. Adversarial and malformed
cases}\label{adversarial-and-malformed-cases}

The acceptance deck covers:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  cause/function/purpose/ought collapse;
\item
  possibility-to-actuality inflation;
\item
  Ground reification;
\item
  invented private lineage facts;
\item
  silent claim deletion;
\item
  false closure;
\item
  provenance poison;
\item
  dangling references;
\item
  underidentified causal targets;
\item
  an information-gain trap; and
\item
  failure to notice that the previous answer now changes the context.
\end{itemize}

Malformed output is hashed and assigned a structured error before
interpretation. It produces \texttt{INVALID\_OUTPUT}, not an invented
all-zero vector. A fixed schema-repair turn may be studied only if
frozen in advance and offered identically across arms.

\section{11. Harness and
reproducibility}\label{harness-and-reproducibility}

The standard-library reference harness exposes \texttt{validate},
\texttt{generate}, \texttt{run\ -\/-dry-run}, \texttt{score}, and
\texttt{freeze\ -\/-check}. It contains JSON Schema documents plus
semantic validation for stable IDs, references, terminal coverage,
revisions, and artifact hashes.

The Anthropic Messages and OpenAI-compatible adapters refuse network
access by default, including localhost. Live access requires an explicit
flag, authorized run class, external authorization reference, and cost
envelope. Credentials are read from environment variables and never
serialized. A response that omits the exact resolved model ID is
invalid.

Development fixtures and the recorded replay test synthetic acceptance
without calling or evaluating a model. The replay's single reviewed
source is bound separately from its five deterministically staged
snapshots and generated prompt hashes; only an authorized live adapter
can supply five raw provider responses. \texttt{freeze\ -\/-check} is
read-only and fails on missing, extra, or changed payloads. Repair
requires an explicit reviewed write and a visible diff.

\section{12. Limits and kill criteria}\label{limits-and-kill-criteria}

The Dasein Test may measure schema obedience, document forensics,
causal-graph recovery, or tool-use policy more than emergence
understanding. Those are live rivals, not footnotes. Synthetic
identifiability does not generalize by itself to the world. Human
graders may reward style. Designers may encode their own categories as
``truth.'' Prompt arms may differ in information content. Model
contamination may make held-out status unknowable.

Kill or narrow the benchmark if:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  neutral controls match it at lower cost;
\item
  graders reward vocabulary or prose style over held-out accuracy;
\item
  fixture leakage cannot be bounded;
\item
  the new dimensions double-count the old;
\item
  Track B rewards guessed private facts;
\item
  intervention scoring punishes correct non-identifiability;
\item
  negative or harmful condition effects cannot be published; or
\item
  explanatory-debt accounting is repeatedly marketed as metaphysical
  closure.
\end{enumerate}

\section{13. Ethics and release
boundary}\label{ethics-and-release-boundary}

The public release excludes held-out truth, private prompts,
credentials, provider headers, internal memory, private lineage facts,
and unsanitized raw responses. A receipt proves that bytes and
procedures were recorded; it does not prove independence, truth,
consciousness, intelligence, or external effect.

The local construct has passed its acceptance gates and the release
language is \texttt{OFFLINE-READY\ ·\ {[}D{]}}. It does not mean a model
was evaluated, the benchmark was validated, an arXiv paper was
submitted, a DOI was deposited, a site was deployed, or priority was
established. Without a local TeX engine, the generated source is
explicitly uncompiled.

\section{14. Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

The Dasein Test turns the long chain of ``why?'' into a public object
that can be attacked. Its ambition is large; its claims are deliberately
bounded. A strong candidate does not need to pretend that it knows what
cannot be known. It must show where each bridge comes from, what could
break it, how evidence changed it, and what remains when it fails.

That is the test: not a perfect story of Being, but an account in which
no debt vanishes merely because the story is beautiful.
