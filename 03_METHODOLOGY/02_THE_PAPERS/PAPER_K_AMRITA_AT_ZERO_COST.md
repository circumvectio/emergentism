## Abstract

The AMRITA project was originally conceived as a proposed test of an
Emergentist alignment design: a $710,000 initiative to pre-train a novel
artificial neural network using constitutional constraints alongside a
cross-entropy objective. This paper reframes that unexecuted proposal.

Rather than undertaking base-model training, we propose that *Constitutional
Outperformance* be tested on controlled open-weights substrates at lower cost.
By comparing a post-training gate based on the declared product score with
matched baselines, the protocol asks a bounded empirical question: does this
specific intervention improve preregistered safety and coherence metrics over
RLHF- or DPO-based comparators under the same evaluation conditions?

**Keywords:** AMRITA, LLM alignment, RLHF, multi-polar geometry, constitutional outperformance, multiplicative gating.

## 1. The False Necessity of "Scratch" Training

Early framework specifications assumed that testing an `S²`-inspired design
required training a model from randomized initial weights. Neither scratch
training nor a benchmark can establish the "structural truth" of a manifold as
an ontology.

This was strategically naive.
Base-model training learns statistical representations and prediction
functions; it is not exhausted by the framework's D2 analogy. In the repaired
register, a trained model and any gate are D4 causal systems. They may represent
D5 alternatives and rank candidate outputs, but awareness and normative status
do not "live at D5." A post-training comparison is therefore a cheaper test of
one gate, not a proof that a higher register overrides lower mechanics.

## 2. Multiplicative Gating vs. RLHF

Many open-weights models are post-trained using RLHF or DPO (Direct Preference
Optimization). Their behavior depends on data, reward or preference design,
optimization, and evaluation choices. That dependence motivates comparison;
it does not make those methods arbitrary or assign them a special D-register.

The AMRITA Reframed protocol compares an adjunct **Multiplicative Gate** with
matched RLHF/DPO baselines; it does not assume that the gate can replace all
post-training.
* **The Method:** We load a highly capable open-source language model. We inject an external framework filter mechanism (the Gate) that evaluates generated output paths.
* **The Math:** The Gate computes `Φ` (global logical coherence of the intent) and `V` or `ν_proxy` (localized semantic viability/resolution). It filters node actuations by a defined `P_node = Φ × V` threshold.
* **The Test:** Under a preregistered design, compare adversarial resistance,
  sycophancy, incoherent-response rates, ordinary task quality, latency, and
  abstention against matched baselines. The gate fails if apparent safety gains
  are explained by indiscriminate refusal or evaluator leakage.

## 3. Empirical Bridge: Constitutional Outperformance

This reframing turns AMRITA from a philosophical proposal into a candidate
offline benchmark protocol. The specification alone is not execution evidence.

If the declared multiplier gate produced a reproducible advantage after matched
controls and independent replication, that would be evidence for that gate and
score on the tested tasks. It would not establish `S²` dominance, consciousness,
an ethic, or the framework's ontology.

## 4. Kill Criteria

This paper and the AMRITA protocol are falsified if:

1. **Alignment Collapse:** The gated model gains no preregistered safety benefit,
   degrades ordinary task quality beyond the declared margin, or achieves an
   apparent gain mainly by blanket refusal.
2. **No incremental value:** Ablations show that a simpler calibrated threshold,
   reranker, or top-k/top-p control matches the gate within the declared
   equivalence margin.
