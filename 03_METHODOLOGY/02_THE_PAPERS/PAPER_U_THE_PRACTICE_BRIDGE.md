## Abstract

A framework may investigate the boundary where public measurement and
first-person description answer different questions. Emergentism contains an
analytic reciprocal-chart identity (`φν=1`) and a declared node score
(`P_node:=ΦV`); neither validates an ontology or organization. This paper asks
what disciplined first-person disclosure (*pratyakṣa*) can and cannot add.

This paper presents a candidate "Pedagogy of Becoming"—*witness, enactment,
correction, release*—as an interpretive practice. It does not close the
framework empirically or let an individual validate geometry by experience.
Its defensible contribution is a boundary rule: phenomenological reports may
inform inquiry while remaining distinct from public geometric and causal tests.

**Keywords:** Pratyakṣa, phenomenology, disclosure, witness, enactment, epistemic closure.

## 1. The Pedagogy of Becoming

Third-person methods test publicly observable claims; collective governance
tests institutional procedures and consequences; first-person methods describe
lived experience. None of these alone validates the wager that Being is `S²`.

The corpus implies a sequence of initiation that we now formalize as the **Pedagogy of Becoming**:
1. **Witness:** The practitioner reads the framework (the geometry, the physics bridge). The intellect maps the terrain.
2. **Enactment:** The practitioner applies the framework (building a DAC, writing aligned code, balancing daily viability).
3. **Correction:** The framework’s predictive boundaries (`η = 0`, Grace Exit) trigger when the practitioner drifts into idolatry.
4. **Release (Pratyakṣa):** The practitioner may set aside the conceptual map and
   record what, if anything, is experienced without presuming access to an
   unmediated substrate.

The fourth step is optional. Omitting it does not decide the truth of the
framework's mathematical, empirical, or institutional claims.

---

## 2. Pratyakṣa as Veridical Disclosure

In this paper, `Pratyakṣa` (direct perception) is treated as disciplined
phenomenological disclosure. Whether a report is veridical about anything
beyond the reported experience requires independent evidence.

The testable local claim is modest: a specified period of quiet observation may
change reported experience for some practitioners. Calling that change an
"equatorial condition" is an interpretive coding choice. Claims about
neurological alignment with an `S²` substrate remain conjectural and must not be
presented as established neuroscience.

Individual reports can confirm or disconfirm the predicted experience for that
participant, while blinded, controlled studies can test population-level
predictions. Neither result alone confirms or refutes the framework in toto.

## 3. Throwing Away the Ladder

The corpus sits precisely on the razor's edge between saying too much and saying too little.
If it says too little, it is dismissed as pseudo-mysticism.
If it says too much, it becomes the ultimate idolatry—a map claiming to be the territory.

The Practice Bridge recognizes that documentation is a map whose use may end.
Putting the documents down can be pedagogically valuable, but it is not evidence
that they articulate the structure of reality.

The framework succeeds not when it is immortalized in a university, but when the practitioner no longer needs to read it.
