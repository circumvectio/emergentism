## 1. Cited Mapping and Audit Boundary

The cited external keystone §4 (line 56) states: *"Organs as moods of one
sentence (GLOSS). Circle = indicative (this is), ReFu = subjunctive (this
could), Agentz = deontic (this should), EvolutionaryNetwork = negative deontic
(this must not), K2/SKYZAI = imperative (do this). A sentence in one mood is
not a thought. The cardinal sin is merging moods."* This quotation is an audit
target, not an Emergentism canonical statement.

The keystone marks this GLOSS — *image, not derivation*. The mood mapping is a way of *seeing* the parts; it is not a derivation of the parts from grammar. To read GLOSS as derivation would be to commit the very category error the keystone warns against: a single-ladder reading of the four-axis firewall, where mood = rung = authority.

The irreducibility principle: **moods are not modules**. A module is a replaceable component; a mood is an irreducible utterance. Removing a module leaves the rest of the system functional; removing a mood leaves a thought incomplete. IS alone is not a thought (it is a tautology); COULD alone is not a thought (it is speculation); SHOULD alone is not a thought (it is exhortation); SHOULD-NOT alone is not a thought (it is refusal); ACT alone is not a thought (it is motion). A thought is the chain of moods that holds the utterance as one sentence — grounded, explored, committed, bounded, instantiated.

The correspondence is not 1:1. The 4 scored organs + 1 immune + 2 boundary + 1 reflective = 8 utterances, but the moods are 5 (indicative / subjunctive / deontic / negative deontic / imperative). The moods are the *underlying structure*; the organs are the *surface instances*. One mood can have multiple organs (e.g. K2 and SKYZAI are both imperative, but with different scopes). One organ can touch multiple moods (e.g. Nexus/Cortex/AIA are reflective, but they witness all moods, not operate one).

The mood mapping is a *teaching lens*, not a *governance ladder*. The keystone's GLOSS tag is the load-bearing fence: do not promote a mood to a rung, and do not collapse two moods into one.

### 2.3 Why moods are not modules

A module is a *replaceable* component. Replace the deontic module, the system still functions; it just stops making recommendations.

A mood is an *irreducible* utterance. Remove the indicative mood, the chain has no ground; the system stops being a thought. Remove the deontic mood, the chain has no commitment; the system stops being a decision. Remove the negative deontic, the chain has no boundary; the system stops being constrained. Remove the imperative, the chain has no instantiation; the system stops being an act.

A holobiont's moods are the irreducible utterances that make its sentence a thought. The keystone's "apophatic inversion" (line 58, §5) is the design principle: the higher the level, the less it does; L4 acts, L5 watches, L6 only negates, L7 only orients. Authority is equatorial, sovereignty negative; the corporate pyramid, upside down. The moods are not stacked; they are *held in the sentence* — the apophatic descent subtracts them one by one until the agent reaches the centre (Brahman), and re-emerges as L7 Ṛṣi with inverted teleology (per `THE_RECURSIVE_TRIVIUM.md` §4).
