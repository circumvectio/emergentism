Capital is the force that commits accumulated means to a drawn world line so that line becomes more travelable inside its **option cone** — lowering the opportunity cost of *that* end without deleting the cone.

## Cone and cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the world lines you do not travel — the cone reminding you that you are finite.

Capital makes *one* potential world line — the end D5 has drawn — cheaper to travel relative to the rest of the option cone, by accumulating and committing means so more of the chosen line can be actualized without re-paying the whole cone at every cut. Roundabout production, tools, title, credit, skill: ways of not re-paying the whole cone at every step.

## Fence (or it becomes the pole)

| Interior | Titan |
|---|---|
| Thickens the chosen world line; neighbors remain, only relatively dearer | Drives opportunity cost to zero by deleting neighbors |
| Exit stays open | Enclosure of the cone; no outside left |
| Force in the interior | Settlement / record / ○ wearing a ledger |

Capital is **not** “more options” (fatter cone, no mission = D5 without a mission).  
Capital is **not** “zero options” (cone collapsed = closed totality).  
Reducing opportunity cost *toward* the chosen end is interior. *Eliminating* the cone is the Titan.

Capital does not maximize potential as such. Potential as such is cheap. Capital maximizes the *travelable* world line *inside* an already-drawn option cone.

Only the force has a world line. Most arguments about capital confuse the two.
