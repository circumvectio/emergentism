# RCAB-01 — The Reciprocal Co-Agency Bridge

RCAB-01 addresses a narrower question than moral realism:

> What follows when one independent agent asks another independent agent to
> accept a claim or action **because reasons count between them**?

It does not infer a value from geometry, emergence, evolution, balance,
potential or efficiency. Its normative entry is visible: participation in a
practice of reciprocal public justification.

## 1. Typed setup

Let `a` and `b` be independent actual D4 bearers. Let `P_R` be a practice in
which either bearer may offer reasons, assess them, contest them and revise an
answer. A merely possible D5 representation of either bearer is not the bearer.

```text
ReasonClaim(a,b,p) := a requests b's reason-governed uptake of proposition p
ReciprocalPeer(a,b):= a and b are equally situated for the relevant reason
RelevantDifference := a public difference that changes the burden in this case
```

The practice presupposes only its thin operating conditions:

```text
VISIBLE   the affected bearers and material stakes are not hidden
ANSWERABLE reasons may be requested and answered
CONTESTABLE either bearer may challenge the claim without losing standing
CORRIGIBLE errors may be corrected and material harm may require repair
EXITABLE  participation may be refused or left where feasible and lawful
```

These are not a complete moral code. They are the conditions under which the
named reciprocal practice is the practice it claims to be.

## 2. RCAB-01 theorem `[S]` inside `P_R`

If `ReasonClaim(a,b,p)` asks `b` to treat reasons as binding between reciprocal
peers, and `a` demands the five operating conditions for itself, then `a`
cannot deny the corresponding conditions to `b` without a declared relevant
difference while continuing to describe the exchange as reciprocal public
justification.

### Proof

1. By definition, `P_R` treats both participants as possible offerers,
   assessors and contesters of reasons.
2. A unilateral denial of one of those constitutive roles to an equally
   situated peer removes the reciprocity required by `P_R`.
3. Continuing to demand peer uptake while denying peer standing therefore
   invokes and rejects the same rule in the same relevant relation.
4. The act is performatively inconsistent with `P_R` unless a relevant
   difference is exposed and defended.

Therefore the five thin conditions are constitutive requirements of the
declared reciprocal practice. `□`

## 3. What bridges—and what does not

RCAB-01 supplies a **conditional constitutive bridge**:

```text
IF reciprocal public justification is undertaken,
THEN its reciprocal operating conditions ought not be denied arbitrarily.
```

The `IF` contains the normative practice premise. RCAB-01 therefore neither
violates Hume's warning nor closes the general is–ought problem. It shows why a
reason-giver cannot demand reciprocal justification and coherently exempt
itself from the minimum rules of that same practice.

The result does not establish:

- stance-independent moral facts;
- one complete theory of Justice;
- that every being can reciprocate reasons;
- shared personhood, shared consent, shared will or shared authority;
- a right to coerce participation;
- that public justification is the only valuable human practice; or
- that every relevant difference is legitimate.

## 4. Relation to Contribution, Support and Justice

RCAB-01 is a thin part of `J_core`. It does not make a transfer good. A proposed
`Contribution(a;i,H)` or `Support(a;H,i)` still requires the bearer-complete
Justice envelope, consent and authorization where applicable, uncertainty,
counterfactual alternatives and outcome receipts.

`CoAgency.v1` must always record:

```text
mergedPersonhood=false
sharedConsentInferred=false
authorityCreated=false
maySign=false
mayAuthorize=false
```

Co-agency coordinates independent agents through actual present carriers; it
does not fuse them into a higher person.

## 5. Rivals, discriminator, kill and survivor

Serious rivals include contractualism, discourse ethics, constitutivism,
Humean instrumentalism, moral realism, pluralism and error theory.

**Discriminator:** test cases must vary bearer identity while holding every
relevant feature fixed. A qualifying rule should remain invariant under that
permutation, expose any claimed relevant difference and outperform simpler
procedural rivals on contradiction detection, hidden-bearer detection and
repair without suppressing dissent.

**Kill or narrow RCAB-01** if the five conditions are not necessary to the
defined practice, if the argument hides a substantive moral conclusion in the
word *reciprocal*, if equally complete inputs yield contradictory results
without an explicit evidence or value difference, or if the bridge is used to
erase a non-reciprocal bearer.

**Survivor:** even if RCAB-01 adds no distinctive moral warrant, any honest
public-reason procedure must still declare who may speak, contest, correct and
leave.
