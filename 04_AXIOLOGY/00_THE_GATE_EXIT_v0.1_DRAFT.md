## §2 · Read the gate properly: seven formal versions, five strengths `[A]`

The docket's five prose versions are confirmed on disk and are not re-litigated
here. The re-run found a second, unexamined layer: **seven active documents
define the Justice-admissible set `A_J` formally, and they place five different
demands on a bearer who is neither `i` nor `H`.** All seven verified on disk
2026-08-12. All are ACTIVE; none is in `90_ARCHIVE`.

| # | Path | What it demands of an arbitrary `b ∈ B(a)` | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | `04_AXIOLOGY/00_THE_EXTRACTION_LAW.md` | `∀b∈B(a), Δ_T^R W_b(a) ≥ 0` | **S1 non-negativity** |
| F2 | `05_COSMOLOGY/00_THE_BURRI_RULES.md` | `∀b∈𝓑(a), Δ̂_T W_b ≥ 0`; and *"a hidden or harmed third bearer defeats Justice"* | **S1 non-negativity** |
| F3 | `07_THEOLOGY/00_RECONCILIATION_THEOREM_PACKET.md` | *"declared rights and minimum-viability conditions at every horizon"* | **S2 floor** |
| F4 | `04_AXIOLOGY/02_VALUE_THEORY/00_OBJECTIVE_MORALS_AND_ETHICS.md` | *"every declared bearer-horizon minimum holds"* | **S2 floor** |
| F5 | `01_TELEOLOGY/00_THE_GENERATIVE_LAGRANGIAN.md` | *"no predicted destruction of `i`, `H`, or an affected third bearer"* | **S3 non-destruction** |
| F6 | `05_COSMOLOGY/03_FORMAL_SYSTEM/08_EFR_POWER_MAX_LEMMA.md` | *"no bearer in `B(a)` is hidden or destroyed by aggregation"* | **S4 non-concealment** |
| F7 | `04_AXIOLOGY/README.md` | **nothing — `B(a)` does not appear in the formula** | **S5 silence** |

Strength order, strict: **S1 ⊐ S2 ⊐ S3 ⊐ S4 ⊐ S5.** Non-negativity forbids any
loss; a floor permits loss down to a declared minimum; non-destruction permits
loss short of destruction; non-concealment permits destruction that is visible
and not laundered; silence permits everything.

**These are not paraphrases of one rule.** F1 and F6 give different verdicts on
the same act: a visible, receipted, proportionate act that costs one bearer is
**inadmissible** under F1 and **admissible** under F6. F7 omits `B(a)` entirely
and therefore licenses exactly the aggregate laundering the gate exists to
forbid — inside `04_AXIOLOGY/README.md`, the README of the folder that owns the
gate.

> **Attack this first, hostile reader:** the corpus's own docket says the gate
> exists in five versions and frames the choice as A/B/C over *clauses*. The
> real fork is over *bearer roles and strengths*, it is seven-way not five-way,
> and none of the docket's three options resolves it.

## §3 · The type conflict bites at boycott, not at war `[A]`

The brief argues: warfare is definitionally the imposition of cost on a bearer,
therefore warfare conflicts with the gate. True, and too weak. The same is true
of a boycott, a strike, a lawsuit, a dismissal, a refusal to trade, a competitive
price cut, and a published criticism that costs someone a client.

**The reductio, and it needs no war:** `15_DHARMA_YUDDHA.md` §2.2 expressly
permits *"Refusal, witness, association, boycott, strike, public record, lawful
protest, mutual aid and voluntary exit"*. A boycott imposes `ΔW < 0` on the
boycotted party. The boycotted party is materially affected, and
`04_AXIOLOGY/00_THE_EXTRACTION_LAW.md` defines the set as *"the complete
affected-bearer set, including `i`, `H`, payers, beneficiaries, and exposed
third parties"* — with **no exclusion clause anywhere in the active corpus**
(grep for forfeiture/exclusion language over bearers, run 2026-08-12: zero
hits). So the boycotted party is in `B(a)`. Under **F1/F2 (S1)** the boycott is
inadmissible.

**Therefore:** the gate at strength S1 forbids an act the corpus explicitly
permits, in a document whose whole purpose is to say which resistance is
permitted. This is a live contradiction between two ACTIVE files, and it does
not depend on any view about war, violence, or defense. It is the cheapest
possible demonstration that S1 cannot be the gate.

A second contradiction sits **inside a single file**. `00_THE_EXTRACTION_LAW.md`
states `A_J^R = {a : J^R(a) ∧ ∀b∈B(a), Δ_T^R W_b(a) ≥ 0}` and, some thirty
lines later, *"**Immune response:** constrains or destroys a threat to protect a
bearer."* If the threat is in `B(a)`, the second sentence describes an action
the first sentence has already excluded. The document both forbids and permits
defense. `[A]`

## §4 · The three exits, worked, with prices

### (a) SCOPE IT — the gate governs within a polity, not between adversaries

**What licenses the boundary?** Nothing currently in the corpus. This is the
exit's first cost: it needs a membership criterion the corpus has never written,
and membership criteria are the most abusable objects in political philosophy.

**Does it collapse into combatant/non-combatant?** **No — and that is the
finding, not a reassurance.** Combatant/non-combatant (jus in bello
discrimination) partitions *the adversary's own side* and protects enemy
civilians. Polity-scoping partitions *by membership* and protects nobody on the
far side of the boundary. Scoping is therefore **strictly weaker than the laws
of armed conflict it superficially resembles**: LOAC protects the enemy's
children; a polity-scoped gate does not. `[A]`

**Whose position is this?** Hobbes and Schmitt in the pure form. Rawls in the
corrected form — *The Law of Peoples* scopes reciprocity to peoples but holds a
human-rights floor across the boundary, which is exactly the repair the pure
version needs.

**Price:** the corpus's universalism is gone. `00_OBJECTIVE_MORALS_AND_ETHICS.md`
would have to retract *"every affected bearer remains visible, contestable, and
leaveable"* as a universal and reissue it as a members-only claim. It also
imports the friend/enemy distinction as a primitive, and Schmitt is not a
neighbour this corpus can cite without a fence.

**Verdict:** available, honest, and expensive. Do not adopt the pure form; if
adopted at all it must be adopted in the Rawlsian form with the floor, at which
point it has converged on exit (d) plus a floor and the scope language is doing
no work.

### (b) DEFENSE IS THE GATE'S ENFORCEMENT ARM

**The attractive part:** defense becomes *downstream* of the ethic rather than
exempt from it — you defend the bearer so the gate can keep holding. It matches
`15_DHARMA_YUDDHA.md` §2.3, *"Defence is accountable to the same rules it
invokes against abuse."*

**What does it actually forbid?** On its own, **nothing.** This is the exit's
defect and it is well documented. Every belligerent in recorded history has
described itself as defending something. Schmitt's line against liberal
universalism is precisely this: an ethic that licenses force under a protective
description licenses all force, because the description is free.

**Does it forbid first strike?** Not by itself. The corpus's only no-first-strike
rule is in `90_ARCHIVE/pure_emergentism_boundary_2026_07_20/08_FRAMEWORK_SUPPORT/
02_OPERATORS/SPHERE_DERIVATIONS/MF_70_Kali_Closes_Sphere.md` — *"K\* = 0 for Q3
initiation. K\* ≠ 0 for Q3 retaliation."* That is archived, `[I]`-tiered, and
`K*` is a **retired token** with no conversion to any live quantity. It cannot
be cited as live doctrine. `[B]`

**Does it forbid deterrence-by-threat?** No, and this is worse than it looks. A
threat's efficacy requires conditional intent to do the thing threatened. Under
S1 the threatened act is inadmissible, so the corpus would be committed to
intending an act it forbids — the standard deterrence paradox, unaddressed
anywhere in the corpus.

**What would make it forbid something:** pairing it with necessity,
proportionality, and discrimination. The corpus already has all three, in
`15_DHARMA_YUDDHA.md` §5's nine-question action test. So exit (b) is not empty
*in this corpus* — but the work is being done entirely by the Dharma Yuddha
apparatus, not by the "enforcement arm" idea. The idea is a label on machinery
that already exists.

**Price:** low in new doctrine, high in abuse surface. Adopting (b) without
naming the protective-description failure mode in the same document would be the
corpus committing its own documented warrant-substitution pattern.

### (c) AMEND THE GATE

**The amended gate would say,** minimally: *a bearer's durable potential may
fall only when the fall is (i) visible and receipted, (ii) necessary and
proportionate to a proved threat, (iii) authorized under a complete envelope,
(iv) above a declared minimum-viability floor, and (v) attributable to that
bearer's own liable conduct — and never justified by a positive group sum.*

**What breaks if it changes** (each verified on disk 2026-08-12):

- `00_OBJECTIVE_MORALS_AND_ETHICS.md` closing line — *"The individual is never
  fuel for the whole. The whole is never fuel for the individual."* Survives:
  it is about `i` and `H`, not third bearers.
- `04_AXIOLOGY/README.md`, `05_COSMOLOGY/00_THE_BURRI_RULES.md`,
  `00_THE_EXTRACTION_LAW.md` — the three `A_J` formulas at S1 and S5 all need
  editing. Three files, three formulas.
- `12_PUBLIC_SITE/00_THE_RUNGS_2026_08_05.md` and
  `14_THE_DISTILLATION/00_THE_RUNGS_2026_08_05.md` both publish the both-rise
  form to readers. Public-facing propagation, two files.
- `00_META/00_THE_COMPASS.md`'s bias row and
  `00_META/00_THE_GRAND_PUZZLE_ASSEMBLY_LEDGER.md` carry compressed forms.
- **Count of files containing the string `aggregate launder`, re-run
  2026-08-12: 16, of which 0 are in `90_ARCHIVE`.** Not all 16 need editing;
  that is the upper bound on the propagation surface, not the estimate.

**Price:** the corpus loses its most quotable ethical sentence in its strongest
reading. It gains a gate that does not forbid boycotts.

## §5 · The fourth possibility, worked: is an adversary a bearer? `[A]`

**The brief's formulation is refuted by the corpus's own text.** The claim would
be that an adversary is not a "bearer" in the gate's technical sense, so `B(a)`
never contains him and the question dissolves. Three findings kill it:

1. **`B(a)` is defined by material effect, not by standing.**
   `00_THE_EXTRACTION_LAW.md`: *"the complete affected-bearer set, including
   `i`, `H`, payers, beneficiaries, and exposed third parties."*
   `05_COSMOLOGY/03_FORMAL_SYSTEM/29_PRIMITIVES_AND_TYPE_SIGNATURES.md`:
   *"every materially exposed third party. … Justice fails closed if the
   coverage is incomplete."* An adversary you are acting upon is materially
   exposed by definition. He is in.
2. **There is no forfeiture doctrine.** A sweep for exclusion/forfeiture
   language over bearers across the active corpus, run 2026-08-12, returns
   **zero** hits. Nothing anywhere removes anyone from `B(a)`.
3. **The corpus has already ruled on exactly this, against the exit.** The
   archived Kālī derivation: *"the affected-bearer set includes the target,
   defender, bystanders, and ecological substrates."* Archived and `[I]`, so it
   is not binding — but it is the only place the question was previously asked,
   and it was answered by **inclusion**.

**But the brief is right that `B(a)` is chosen, and right that this is where the
answer lives — it just picked the wrong lever.** The correct lever is not
*membership* but *what the gate demands of a member*. Restated:

> **The adversary is a bearer. The gate binds. What the gate demands of him is
> discharged by his own liable conduct, not waived by his exclusion.** `[I]`

This is McMahan's and Thomson's liability structure, imported honestly and cited
by name. Its virtue over exclusion: the adversary keeps standing, keeps the
right to contest, keeps a minimum-viability floor, keeps repair — and the moment
his conduct ceases (surrender, incapacity), liability lapses and the full gate
snaps back. Exclusion cannot produce that behaviour; liability does it
automatically.

**The corpus already has this criterion latently, and does not know it.**
`05_COSMOLOGY/03_FORMAL_SYSTEM/20_EFR_OPERATORS_ON_COMPLEX_PLANE.md`: Kali
*"fires only where η > 0 (defectors). Strategically excluded at η = 0 under the
enforced game (cooperators)."* A conduct-indexed permission that fires only
against those whose own conduct meets a threshold **is** a liability criterion.
It has never been named as one, never been connected to `B(a)`, and never been
given the discrimination and proportionality constraints that the literature
attaches to it. `[A]`

**What this does not dissolve.** Liability handles the attacker. It does not
handle the bystander — the enemy's civilians, the third party in the blast
radius. They are non-liable bearers, fully inside `B(a)`, and the gate binds on
them at full strength. That is the doctrine of double effect's territory, and
the corpus has nothing there at all. **Adopting liability without also adopting
a discrimination rule would be the most dangerous single edit available to this
corpus**, because it licenses force against the liable while leaving the
protection of the non-liable unstated. `[I]`

## §6 · Recommendation

**This is a recommendation. It is `[C]`, it is not a ruling, and the chair
decides. Nothing in this file is signed or signable by its author.**

**Recommend: exit (d) reformulated as liability, carried on the F6 gate
strength, with a mandatory discrimination clause.** Concretely, three moves:

1. **Adopt F6 (`08_EFR_POWER_MAX_LEMMA.md`) as the canonical strength for
   bearers other than `i` and `H`** — *"no bearer in `B(a)` is hidden or
   destroyed by aggregation"* — and keep S1 non-negativity for `i` and `H`
   themselves. This makes the gate **genuinely dyadic**, which is what its name
   has always claimed: two protected poles, everyone else protected by
   visibility, a floor, and non-laundering. Repair F1, F2 and F7 to match.
2. **Add a liability clause**, citing McMahan and Thomson by name: a bearer's
   own liable conduct discharges (never voids) the gate's demand on him, in
   proportion, and only while the conduct continues.
3. **Add a discrimination clause in the same edit, or make no edit at all.**
   Non-liable bearers retain the full gate. This must not be a follow-up item.

**Why.** It is the only exit that (i) fixes the boycott contradiction in §3,
(ii) requires no new membership criterion, (iii) uses a formula **already on
disk in an active document** rather than inventing doctrine, (iv) names a real
owner in the literature instead of re-deriving it, and (v) degrades safely —
when liability lapses, protection returns automatically.

**What it costs, stated plainly:**

- **The corpus loses "no bearer ever falls" as a universal.** That sentence, in
  its strongest reading, is the corpus's most distinctive ethical content and
  the recommendation demotes it to a claim about `i` and `H`. Anyone who values
  the gate *because* it is absolute will read this as a capitulation. They are
  not wrong about what is being given up; the reply is that the absolute version
  forbids boycotts and contradicts four other active documents, so it was never
  actually held.
- **It imports an unresolved external debate.** McMahan's revisionism is
  contested — Walzer's moral equality of combatants is the standing rival, and
  the corpus would be taking a side in a live dispute it has not studied.
- **It creates abuse surface.** "He is liable" is as free a description as "I am
  defending". Mitigated only by the `15_DHARMA_YUDDHA.md` §5 action test and the
  discrimination clause, which is why move 3 is not optional.
- **Anscombe is the cost's clearest voice.** If discrimination is absolute and
  the corpus adopts liability with a weak discrimination clause, Anscombe's
  objection lands and this document will have helped it land.

**What is explicitly not recommended:** exit (a) in its pure Hobbes/Schmitt form
(under-protects relative to LOAC; imports friend/enemy as a primitive); exit (b)
as a standalone (forbids nothing; the corpus's existing Dharma Yuddha apparatus
is doing all the work and should be credited rather than relabelled).

**What is out of scope and must not be inferred:** this file takes no position
on physical force. `15_DHARMA_YUDDHA.md` §2.4 places physical force *"outside
this framework's authority"* and that boundary is untouched here. A liability
clause in an axiology document is not a use-of-force authorization, and if it is
ever read as one, this document has failed and should be withdrawn.
