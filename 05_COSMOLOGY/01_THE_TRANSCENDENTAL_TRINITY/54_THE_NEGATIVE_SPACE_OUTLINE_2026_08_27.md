# The Negative-Space Outline

## 0 · The method, and why it is the only lawful one here

The corpus cannot display its object. `09 §5`, the two sterilities, Tarski, and
the witness doctrine all forbid it from four directions: no seat sees the whole
and remains a seat. What *can* be drawn is the **negative space** — and the
graves file already supplies the scythe. `DF-15`: *"fifteen renderings of one
shape are one datum shown fifteen ways."*

> **Renderings are chaff. What outlines a shape is what cannot be otherwise.**
> A point is admissible only if it is **theorem-grade** (proved, not modelled),
> **register-typed**, and **forbidding** (it says what cannot happen, with
> stated scope).

Every coherent world obeys Gödel, Bell, Carnot, Arrow. The object is the
intersection of everything the theorems permit — apophatic method with
citations, which is what this corpus's own `D6` doctrine demanded all along.

**The placement rule `[S]`** (what killed the register inflation): a theorem
sits in the register where the *forbidden thing* lives. It is a **crossing**
only when the prohibition is a **conjunction** — forbidding property-at-A
*together with* property-at-B, neither half forbidden alone. An imported
premise (AC in Banach–Tarski, a degree fact in Wantzel) is a **fence note, not
a crossing**.

**Fences are the deliverable, not the theorem names.** A sample of what the
cross-check caught, each of which would have been an overclaim:

- **Rice's theorem is 1953**, not 1951 — 1951 is the thesis year. (The
  escorted-number defect, `DF-22`, caught live in a citation.)
- **Church–Turing is a THESIS, not a theorem** — never present it as proved.
- **Tarski does not say a system can know nothing about its own truth** —
  partial truth predicates *are* definable (`Σₙ`-truth is `Σₙ`-definable).
  Only the *total* semantic mirror is forbidden.
- **Gödel II forbids self-certification, not certification** — Gentzen proved
  `Con(PA)` in 1936. And it is *intensional* (Feferman): it depends on the
  predicate actually expressing consistency.
- **Hilbert 10 is undecidable over ℤ; over ℚ it is STILL OPEN** (2026).
- **Löwenheim–Skolem's "paradox" reading is chaff**; **Tennenbaum** is the
  sharp fence — the deviant models exist but are not computably presentable.

## 2 · The three strongest new points `[B]`

**Lindström 1969 — and it changes the method's status.** Every other D1 entry
names a *hole*. Lindström names the **price list**: any logic extending
first-order with countable compactness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem has
exactly first-order expressive power. Gödel and LS stop being unfortunate
accidents and become a **trade fixed in advance** — you may buy power to pin
down a structure, or compactness and tractability, and the exchange rate is
not negotiable. For a program outlining by negative space this is the
difference between *a list of things that happen to be absent* and **a proof
that the negative space has a shape.** Fence: "strongest" = maximal expressive
power, not proof-theoretic strength; no normative claim that FOL is right.

**Hilbert 1901 / Efimov 1964, fenced by Nash–Kuiper 1954/55 — D2.** The
complete hyperbolic plane exists as an intrinsic object, has no complete `C²`
isometric immersion in `ℝ³`, and *has* a `C¹` one. **The forbidden object is
unrepresentable in exactly one ambient register at exactly one smoothness.**
The program's own method, occurring as a proved theorem — and it imposes a
discipline the corpus lacks: *an impossibility claim that does not name its
ambient register and its regularity class is not merely unfenced, it is
false.* Live warning attached: the corrected fence is flexibility for
`α < 1/5`, rigidity for `α > 2/3`, **the gap open**, and `C^{1,1/2}` is
Gromov's *conjecture*, not a located bound.

**Sonnenschein–Mantel–Debreu 1972–74, stated negatively — D5, and it points
at us.** Individual rationality imposes **no** restriction on aggregate excess
demand beyond Walras' law and homogeneity; therefore **no representative-agent
reduction of a collective is licensed.** This is the sharpest theorem-grade
form of *the collective is not a magnified individual* — and it bears directly
on this estate's largest standing affirmative structure: reading an organism,
holobiont, or DAV **as an agent with coherent preferences.** With Arrow,
Nakamura, List–Pettit and Holmström it makes one prohibition: **a collective
may have a rule, and members with preferences, and does not thereby have a
mind, a preference ordering, or a demand function.** Fence: exchange
economies, `l ≥ 2` goods, consumers ≥ goods; restrictions **do** reappear
under assumptions on the *distribution* of preferences (Hildenbrand,
Grandmont).

## 3 · Where to dig — and the corpus's exposure `[B]`

**D5 is densest (30 + 8)** and its fences are the best-developed in the whole
outline, because economists and distributed-systems theorists have been
fighting their own overclaimers for seventy years. Density there is
*protective*: it is also where this estate has the most exposure.

**D2 is sparsest (14) and that sparseness is the dangerous one** — thinner
than D1 or D4, and thin **exactly where the program's load-bearing objects
live**: sphere primacy, the stereographic chart, `ℂP¹`, the equator/`L4`
construction. *A sparse D2 means the geometric claims are the least fenced
claims in the corpus.*

**Next dig, named:** smooth-structure obstructions in dimension four —
**Donaldson 1983** (diagonalizability; non-smoothable topological
4-manifolds) read against **Freedman**, with **Milnor's exotic 7-spheres** as
the affirmative sibling whose forbidding content is the failure of smooth
uniqueness. *"Genuinely forbidding, genuinely theorem-grade, and
dimension-4-specific in a way the program should want to know about before it
builds further on a sphere."* Second dig: the h-principle boundary — where
flexibility stops.

**A structural finding, recorded not repaired:** D3 has only 11 pure entries
because **the quantum register is almost entirely made of crossings** (14 of
its 25). That is not a gap — it is the register's shape. Quantum theory
forbids little alone and a great deal **in conjunction** with causal structure
(12 crossings into D4) and with strategy (6 into D5). *Any account of D3 that
reports it as thin has mistaken a topology for a shortage.*

## 4 · The `L_D5` verdict — the proposed assembly failed, and the survivor is better

The session AI proposed a six-legged instrument for the verification register
(Ashby + Conant–Ashby + Tarski + Rice + Beer + Landauer). **Verdict: not a
coherent instrument as specified. Two legs removed, two narrowed.**

- **Beer's VSM is chaff outright** — an affirmative organizational framework
  with no forbidding theorem attached. It lent the assembly a false sixth leg.
- **Conant–Ashby cannot bear the weight.** "Model" there means a
  *deterministic homomorphic image* that may discard almost all information;
  the regulator is *handed* the system state, so there is no sensing and no
  inference. **Baez's criticism — that the formal proof does not support the
  paper's title — is in the standard reference.** The theorem structurally
  cannot say a verifier must *build* a model of what it verifies.
- **Ashby is theorem-grade but conditional on column-distinctness** — a
  hypothesis verification systems violate *by design*: buffering, defaults and
  canonicalization are exactly constant columns. **For a gate that normalizes
  its input before checking, requisite variety is vacuous.**
- **Landauer is wheat but mis-fitted**: it prices **erasure**. Using it as the
  thermodynamic floor of a *check* is a category slide unless the check is
  separately shown to erase.

**What survives is tighter and three-legged:**

1. **Tarski** — the evaluator must sit in a strictly stronger metalanguage, so
   closed self-certification is impossible, while **stratified, partial
   internal audit is entirely lawful.**
2. **Rice** — no total decision procedure for any non-trivial semantic
   property: syntactic gates are cheap, property gates are not, and the only
   lawful repair is a **sound-but-incomplete checker with an explicit UNKNOWN
   verdict.**
3. **Touchette–Lloyd, with the DPI equality condition as its instrument** — a
   gate's discriminating power is bounded by the information it actually
   acquires (at most one bit of order per bit measured), and **a gate that
   coarse-grains is informationally free exactly when its checked variable is
   a sufficient statistic for the property, strictly lossy otherwise — and
   that loss IS the false-pass rate.**

Landauer re-enters only where a step is shown to erase; Ashby only for gates
whose payoff table has no constant column.

### The prediction that tests it `[C]` — one day, binary outcome

> For **every** gate in this estate that (a) reads only syntactic features of
> an artifact and (b) issues a binary PASS/FAIL with no UNKNOWN verdict, **an
> artifact exists that passes the gate and violates the property the gate is
> claimed to certify — and it can be constructed in bounded time by inspecting
> the gate's own source alone.**

Pre-register the target list; attempt one adversarial artifact per gate. The
instrument predicts **success on every such gate**. It predicts failure only
where the checked variable is provably a sufficient statistic, or where the
artifact class is not Turing-complete — in which case Rice's fence is
*honoured*, not broken, and the gate is legitimately complete on a decidable
fragment. **A gate that resists construction and is neither of those two
things falsifies the Rice leg.**

This re-runs the corpus's own `D-06` finding — *"the gates certify formatting,
not properties"* — as a **test** rather than an assertion. Per the standing
rule: **secure and hash the evidence base before publishing the verdict, not
after.**
