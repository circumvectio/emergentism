# The Poles

## Distinct limits joined by a selected correspondence

> **[金] Kintsugi seam — relation is not identity.** The earlier paper said the

## 1. What the mathematics says

On the Riemann sphere, stereographic coordinates compactify the extended complex
plane by adding one point at infinity. The coordinate limits

```text
z = 0     and     z = ∞
```

are represented by two **distinct** points. A continuous path on the sphere may
connect them; connected points do not thereby become identical.

The reciprocal involution

```text
J(z) = 1/z
```

exchanges `0` and `∞` and fixes `+1` and `−1`. Thus the poles form a two-element
orbit under `J`; they do not collapse into a one-element set. This reciprocal
relation is the positive algebraic survivor.

For the selected open chart

```text
φ = cot(θ/2),  ν = tan(θ/2),  θ in (0,π),
```

the limits are

```text
θ -> 0:  φ -> ∞, ν -> 0
θ -> π:  φ -> 0, ν -> ∞.
```

The identity `φν=1` holds inside the open chart. At the excluded endpoints,
`0·∞` is not thereby assigned the ordinary arithmetic value `1`.

## 2. What Emergentism selects

The Rosetta uses the two poles as boundary symbols around seven selected
latitudes:

```text
north limit:  φ -> ∞, ν -> 0
              L7 ... L5
equator:      L4, φ = ν = 1
              L3 ... L1
south limit:  φ -> 0, ν -> ∞
```

This is a visualization and dispatch grammar. The sphere does not force seven
levels, these rows are not additional spacetime dimensions, and the pole tokens
are not causal agents.

The D-scaffold is typed separately:

```text
D4 = causal actuality, embodied means, performed action, record
D5 = counterfactual possibility, modeled futures, ranking, selection
D6 = apophatic boundary with no new positive freedom
r₆:D6 ↝ D0 = one non-μ interpretive return
```

There are exactly the candidate crossings `μ₀…μ₅`; there is no `μ₆`. The arrow
`r₆` records a return discipline, not equality of endpoints and not a theorem
derived from `S²`.

## 3. The mythic correspondence

Kāla may symbolize time, dissolution, or the taking boundary; Trimūrti may
symbolize generation, transformation, and preservation. Their placement at
opposite limits can illuminate a perennial insight: preservation and dissolution
are reciprocally implicated in living systems.

That statement is an interpretive crosswalk. It does not show that a deity is a
coordinate, that creation and destruction are numerically identical, or that
mythology anticipated a formal theorem. Rosetta translates a topology; it does
not transfer proof between domains.

## 4. The living use

The useful compass is not “the bottom is literally the top.” It is:

1. distinguish opposing limits clearly;
2. look for the transformation relating them;
3. preserve what is invariant under that transformation;
4. do not erase the difference that makes the relation informative;
5. return from abstraction to an accountable D4 action and its receipt.

This is the more beautiful closure: not an identity imposed on two different
points, but a visible seam joining difference, reciprocity, and return.
