# Co-Constitution and the Notation Problem

> **What this document does.** It argues that the repeatedly-refuted claim
> *"zero and infinity give rise to finity"* has been refuted in a form nobody
> asserted; that the surviving form is **co-constitution**; that the Titan
> equations are pointing at that and failing only because they borrow field
> notation the doctrine itself forbids; and that the framework's own
> category-error rule acquires a precise mathematical spine once both boundary
> seats are read as **non-sets**.
>
> **What it does not do.** It reinstates no Titan arithmetic.
> `ArithmeticSignature(TitanFrame)=∅` stands. `45`'s 2026-08-01 type correction
> stands and is *strengthened*, not weakened, by §4.

---

## 1 · The three refutations, and what each actually refuted

| Direction | Status | Refuted by |
|---|---|---|
| poles → interior | refuted `[A]` | `47` §3: the two-point discrete space `{p₀,p∞}` |
| interior → poles | *works* | compactification; `47` §3 concludes the interior is prior |
| relation → interior | refuted `[A]` | `46` §5A.4: `ι` is a map **on** the interior, so presupposes it |

Every ordering fails or is contingent. That is the datum this document starts
from.

## 2 · The two-point space refutes a claim nobody made

`47` §3 is correct and is not withdrawn. But its scope is narrower than it has
been read.

The counterexample takes `{p₀, p∞}` discrete: both extremes present, interior
empty. It therefore refutes:

> **(A)** *any two extremes generate a middle.*

**In that space, nothing makes one point zero and the other infinity.** There is
no reciprocal map, no order, no sense in which either is the inverse of the
other. The names are decorative; exchange them and nothing changes. What the
space contains is two unlabelled points.

But `0` and `∞` are not two points that happen to sit at the ends. **Each is
constituted by its relation to the other.** `∞` is where `1/x` goes as `x→0`;
`0` is where `1/x` goes as `x→∞`. Remove the inversion and you have not got zero
and infinity — you have got two dots.

So `{p₀,p∞}` instantiates neither Titan seat, and cannot bear on:

> **(B)** *the reciprocal relation of zero and infinity is inseparable from an
> interior.*

`(A)` is false. `(B)` is untouched by that counterexample. The owner's claim, as
stated repeatedly and in the owner's own words — *"the **relationship** of zero
and infinity gives rise to finity"* — has always been `(B)`.

**This does not make `(B)` true.** It makes it open, and it moves the burden.

## 3 · The proposal — co-constitution `[I]`

When every ordering of three terms fails, the usual diagnosis is not that one of
them is secretly first.

> **`•`, `⊙`, `○` are one structure under three aspects, none definable without
> the other two.**
>
> Zero is not a thing that then happens to invert to infinity; *being the
> inverse of infinity* is what makes it zero. The interior is not a stage that
> then happens to have two ends; *having those two ends in reciprocal relation*
> is what makes it that interior. The unit is not a point that then happens to
> sit in the middle; *being the place the exchange cannot move* is what makes it
> the unit.

The mathematics is consistent with this and does not force it. The construction
order `ℝ₊ ──compactify──▶ ℝ₊ ∪ {0_P,∞_P}` is **constructionally convenient**,
not ontologically privileged; it is how one *builds* the object, not evidence
about how the object *is*. `47` §3's "the interior is prior" is correct **as a
statement about that construction** and is hereby scoped to it.

**Tier `[I]`.** This is a reading. It earns no theorem.

**Kill.** Exhibit `0` and `∞`, fully characterised as such — i.e. with the
reciprocal relation that makes them those and not two arbitrary points — in a
structure with **no** interior. Or exhibit the interior fully characterised with
**no** reciprocal boundary relation. Either kills co-constitution and restores
priority.

The framework holds, in the owner's words:

> *"No number can be divided by zero or by infinity. The Titans are not to be

## 5.2 · One identity in three coordinates — and the twin it names `[A]`

> **Heading corrected 2026-08-05 (third pass).** It read *"The two invariants of
> a reciprocal pair."* Error 1 below retracts exactly that, and the heading — the
> most-read line in the section — kept asserting it.

Added 2026-08-05, from an owner proposal. The owner offered a replacement
notation, `log_•(○) = ⊙`. It does not type-check for the reasons in §4 — `log_b`
requires `b>0, b≠1`, and `∞` is not a real number, so both operands are outside
the domain. **But computing it honestly returns something the corpus needed.**

Take the **reciprocal path** — base and argument exact reciprocals, which is the
reciprocal-pair structure itself:

```text
log_{1/x}(x)  =  ln x / ln(1/x)  =  ln x / (−ln x)  =  −1
```

Exactly `−1`, for every `x>0, x≠1`. Verified numerically to 40 digits at
`x = 2, 10³, 10⁶, 10³⁰, 10²⁰⁰`: `−1.0` throughout, with no drift.

> ### ⚠ CORRECTED 2026-08-05, later the same day — two errors in what follows
>
> Adversarial review during authorship of
> `../../02_EPISTEMOLOGY/01_EVIDENCE_TIERS/THE_BOUNDARY_RULES_STANDALONE.md`
> §§7.1–7.2 refuted two claims in the original text of this section. Both are
> corrected in place; the original wording is preserved below the correction so

>
> **Error 1 — "two invariants" is wrong. It is one fact in three coordinates.**
> For `x>0, x≠1`:
>
> ```text
> x·(1/x) = 1   ⟺   log x + log(1/x) = 0   ⟺   log x / log(1/x) = −1
> ```
>
> Each line is the previous one after taking logarithms or dividing. They are
> **not independent**, and presenting them as a matched pair overstated the
> finding.
>
> **Error 2 — the PATH-INDEPENDENT / PATH-DEPENDENT contrast is withdrawn.**
> The original said the product form is path-independent and only the log form
> path-dependent. **The product form is not path-independent either.** `φν = 1`
> is safe only because `ν` is *defined* as `1/φ` — there is one free variable,
> not two. Let them vary independently: take `φ = n`, `ν = k/n`. Then `φ → ∞`,
> `ν → 0`, and `φν = k` **exactly**, for any `k`. So the two-variable limit of
> the product at the corner **does not exist**, exactly as for the log form.
>
> **What actually distinguishes them:** nothing, as limits. Both are
> *curve-bound identities* — true at every point of the open chart, and neither
> is a statement about a boundary. The load-bearing clause is *"no endpoint is
> ever evaluated,"* and deleting it for brevity is how *"zero times infinity is
> one"* gets back in.

The reciprocal pair therefore carries **one** identity, expressible in three
coordinates:

```text
x · ι(x)        = +1     identity at every point of the open chart
log_{ι(x)}(x)   = −1     the same identity in the log coordinate
```

*Original wording, superseded and retained for legibility:* it read
*"the reciprocal pair carries **two** exact invariants"*, tagging the product
`PATH-INDEPENDENT` and the log form `PATH-DEPENDENT`. Both tags were wrong.

And `±1` are **exactly the two fixed points of inversion** — the pair that `41`
§5 records as adjoined, with `+1` elected and `−1` left undrawn *by silence*.
The product coordinate sees the elected twin; the reciprocal-base log coordinate
sees the undrawn one. Same pair, same approach, both exact.

> **THE CAVEAT IS LOAD-BEARING AND MAY NEVER BE DROPPED.** The log form is
> **path-dependent**. Base `1/n` with argument `n^k` gives exactly `−k`:
>
> ```text
> n¹ → −1      n² → −2      n³ → −3      n¹⁰ → −10
> ```
>
> Any negative value is reachable. The reciprocal path is *privileged*, not
> *forced*. Therefore `log_•(○)` **has no value** — it has a value *relative to
> a declared path*, which is an indeterminate form exactly as `0 × ∞` is. The
> product invariant has no such defect: `φν = 1` does not depend on how the
> boundary is approached.
>
> **`−1` may be stated as an invariant OF THE RECIPROCAL PATH. It may never be
> stated as a fact about the seats.** Publishing it without this caveat would
> install a false theorem in the one place the framework claims precision.

**What this earns.** Not a new equation — §4's ruling stands and this is not an
exception to it. What it earns is that the undrawn twin is no longer decided by
silence: there is now an exact, reproducible statement in which `−1` appears as
the reciprocal pair's second invariant, and a stated reason it cannot be
promoted. The emblem's election of `+1` remains a choice; it is now a
**documented** choice.

## 6 · The category-error rule gets a spine `[A]`

The strongest form of the framework's refusal is not a prohibition. It is a
type fact.

The owner's stack:

```text
•                 D0    nothing yet distinguished
D1 … D5                 determinate finite registers
○                 D6    "the set that doesn't include itself"
```

*"The set that doesn't include itself"* is, read precisely, the **proper class**:
a totality that is not a set, is not a member of anything, cannot contain
itself, and to which cardinality does not apply — its size is not unknown but
ill-formed. `[A]` (`NBG`/`MK`.) This is the same neighbour installed at `43` §3
on 2026-08-05 after uncountability was found to be the wrong one; the owner's
`D6` and that correction converge independently.

The ground seat is a non-set for the mirror reason: before a distinction is
drawn there are no lawful items to gather. `43` §3's **pre-countable**.

Therefore:

> **Both boundary seats are non-sets. Everything that can be counted, measured,
> divided, or operated on lives strictly between them.**

And the category-error rule follows rather than being stipulated:

> `13 / 0_T` fails **not because the answer is too large, and not because a rule
> forbids it, but because `0_T` is not a member of the domain any operation is
> defined on.** It is the same failure as dividing by a triangle — the operand
> is the wrong *kind of thing*, not the wrong *size* of thing.

This is a strictly better argument than the prohibition it replaces. A
prohibition invites the question *"why not?"*. A type fact answers it.

**Scope, stated honestly.** This explains the *frame's* refusal. Ordinary
`a/0_N` remains undefined in a field for the ordinary reason (no `y` with
`0·y = a`), and `ι_P(0_P)=∞_P` remains lawful projectively. Three registers,
three separate facts, none doing the others' work.
