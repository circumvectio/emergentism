## 1. The invariant distinction

| | `D4` | `D5` |
|---|---|---|
| **Modality** | **actual** | **possible** |
| **Core meaning** | causal actuality | counterfactual possibility |
| **Contains** | embodied means, performed action, event, factual record | modeled futures, ranking, intention, selection among represented options |
| **Question** | What happened, with what means, and what was recorded? | What could happen, which options are represented, and how are they ranked? |
| **Failure mode** | means or action without adequate foresight | foresight or intention without usable means or enactment |

The words “witness,” “present,” “torus hole,” and “torus surface” may survive as
interpretive imagery `[I]`. They do not change the modality and they are not
physical definitions of dimensions.

## 2. Emergence is not enactment

Two arrows meet at the D4/D5 boundary and must never be fused.

### 2.1 Candidate emergence `[C]`

```text
D4 actual --μ4⇢ D5 possible
```

`μ₄` proposes that an actual causal system acquires a newly effective capacity
to model, compare, and select counterfactual histories. The new capacity is
implemented by an actual system; its contents remain possible. `μ₄` requires a
complete `MuCrossing` record and does not prove strong emergence.

### 2.2 Commitment `[S/I]`

```text
D5 possible --χ--> D4 actual
```

`χ` combines a selected option with actual means and accountable authorization,
then commits a D4 action. The option does not become history because it was
imagined or selected; it becomes enacted history only through an actual event.
This downward arrow is not `μ`, measurement, or collapse.

## 3. The finite-agent interface

At time `t`, let:

- `X_t` be the actual state available to the agent;
- `Ω_t` be its represented option field;
- `M_t` be its fallible model;
- `V_t` be its usable embodied means;
- `U_t` be its authorization envelope;
- `G_t` be its ranking/selection policy.

Unless independently manipulated by design, `Ω_t` is generated by an explicit
map `Options_t(X_t,M_t)` rather than treated as a stale field independent of the
model.

Commitment is

```text
χ_t : (X_t, Ω_t, M_t, V_t, U_t, G_t) → (a_t, q_t)
```

where `G_t` is an explicit selector input, `a_t` is the performed or initiated
action, and `q_t` is the immediate
**commitment/action receipt**. If no feasible, authorized action exists, `χ_t`
must return a null commitment rather than pretending that selection enacted an
outcome.

The environment separately returns

```text
(X_(t+1), r_(t+1)) ~ K_t(· | X_t, a_t, E_t),
```

where `E_t` includes other agents and unmodeled conditions and `r_(t+1)` is the
**outcome receipt**. The world may veto, redirect, amplify, or frustrate the
committed action.

The Soul Loop is

```text
(M_(t+1), G_(t+1))
  = Loop(M_t, G_t, q_t, r_(t+1)).
```

A null update is allowed when the receipts contain no information relevant to
the declared model or selector.

## 4. Three gaps made inspectable

| Gap | Comparison | Typical diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | actual territory `X_t` vs model `M_t` | calibration error, omitted variable, false analogy |
| **Execution** | selected intention vs `q_t` | no means, no authorization, drift between plan and act |
| **Outcome** | expected result vs `r_(t+1)` | environmental veto, side effect, other-agent response |

Corrective feedback decreases a declared error measure; reinforcing feedback
increases divergence or self-confirmation. Those signs are dynamical, not
moral.

## 5. Means, authorization, and consequence

`V_t` may include body, tools, energy, access, timing, and control. It is actual
only when available at the relevant boundary. `U_t` must identify:

```text
principal, mandate, scope, consent, custody,
expiry_or_revocation, contest_path,
actor, consequence_bearer.
```

A selected option without means remains possible. Means without a selected and
authorized use remain available actuality but do not constitute agency toward
that option. A receipt that hides who bears the result is incomplete.

## 7. Model-mediated future influence

Anticipated futures influence the present through their present
representations. Let `π_t` be the action distribution induced by `χ_t`, `G_t`,
and any declared selector randomization, and set
`Ω_t^(j)=Options_t(X_t,M_t^(j))`:

```text
π_t(a | do(M_t=M_t^(1)), X_t,Ω_t^(1),V_t,U_t,G_t)
  may differ from
π_t(a | do(M_t=M_t^(2)), X_t,Ω_t^(2),V_t,U_t,G_t).
```

This provides a clean intervention test: hold the actual present, available
means, authorization, policy state, and intervention protocol fixed; alter the
represented future; recompute its option field; and observe whether the present
action distribution changes. A positive result supports
model-mediated influence. It does not show that a future physical event sent a
signal backward in time.

## 8. Physical cone and option cone

- A **physical light cone** is the spacetime-causal region allowed by the
  relevant physics. It is not widened by symbolic cognition and remains subject
  to `c` where relativity applies.
- An **option cone** is the subset of admissible histories an agent can model,
  rank, coordinate, and reach with its actual means.

Two agents can have the same physical cone and different option cones. Humans
may have unusually wide symbolic, social, institutional, and intergenerational
option cones without exceeding the physical light cone or possessing greater
intrinsic worth.

“Agents maximize the cone” is an Emergentist objective/hypothesis `[I/C]`, not
a universal law. Its defensible normative form is durable mutual option-cone
widening under physical cost, consent, custody, contest, reversibility, and
exit.
