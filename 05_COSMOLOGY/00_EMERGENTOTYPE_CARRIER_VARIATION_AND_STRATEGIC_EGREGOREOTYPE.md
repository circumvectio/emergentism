# The Emergentotype Carrier Map and the Strategic Egregoreotype Candidate

## 0. What changed—and what did not

The six-layer Replicator Stack already existed. The new contribution is a
sharper question at each layer:

> **What pattern is being identified, what actual carriers instantiate it,
> which carrier differences are admitted, and what change would destroy the
> claimed identity?**

The owner calls this the **Emergentotype hierarchy**. Technically, this document
uses **Emergentotype Carrier Map** because *hierarchy* can falsely imply moral
rank, a primary ontology, or one universal mechanism. Nothing here adds a
seventh layer or replaces D0–D6, the trophic coordinate, or the seven Rosetta
functions.

`EmergentotypeProfile` is selected terminology `[S]`:

```text
EmergentotypeProfile := {
  replicatorLayer,
  patternIdentity,
  carrierClass,
  carrierInstanceIds,
  admittedCarrierVariation,
  persistenceCriterion,
  intervention,
  rivalModels,
  evidenceRefs,
  tier,
  killCriterion
}
```

It is an analytic record, not a discovered natural kind. A profile is useful
only if its declared identity survives some carrier changes and fails under
others. If no intervention can discriminate identity from convenient naming,
the profile is decoration.

## 1. The six profiles

| Existing layer | Pattern named at the declared boundary | Actual carrier examples | Variation that may occur without identity being assumed or denied | Critical fence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Genotype** | a declared heritable sequence configuration at named loci or genome scope | DNA-bearing cells, organisms, sampled genomes | carrier identity, developmental history, later mutation, mosaicism, and unmeasured sequence can differ | a gene or genotype is not an organism, individual, or clone |
| **Epigenotype** | the developmental and regulatory process-profile linking genotype to phenotype at a declared tissue, time, assay, and environment | actual developing cells, tissues, organisms, and their regulatory states | genetically similar carriers may follow different regulatory trajectories; an assay never exhausts the process | Waddington's term is not identical to an epigenome snapshot, peptides, or a genome-free essence |
| **Phenotype** | an observed trait, behavior, or organismal profile at a declared boundary | bodies, physiology, behavior, development | similar measured genotype and epigenotype do not force identical outcomes when environment, development, stochasticity, or unmeasured variables differ | phenotype is not merely genotype made visible |
| **Extended Phenotype** | an external effect, artifact, or niche modification linked by a declared organism-mediated causal chain | a beaver-felled tree or dam, a tool, path, interface, nest, ledger | builders, materials, location, maintenance, and later users can turn over | not every environmental event is an extended phenotype; attribution and boundary are owed |
| **Memotype** | a recurrent cultural carrier-pattern that can span linguistic and nonlinguistic traces | utterances, texts, demonstrations, rituals, habits, interfaces, algorithms | token, language, medium, carrier, and context can vary within a declared persistence test | a word token, concept, meme, and memotype are not identities |
| **Egregoreotype** | the existing five-marker persistent collective-trace candidate | people, artifacts, procedures, institutions, databases, model sessions, and their shared traces | particular carriers may turn over while the trace and its selection effect persist | a name, organization, legal person, crowd, database, or belief is insufficient |

The map does not claim that one identity rule works across all six layers.
`admittedCarrierVariation` is layer-specific. Genetic sequence identity,
semantic recurrence, institutional continuity, and trace-mediated selection
are different tests.

The compact relation is therefore:

```text
pattern at layer L  !=  one carrier at L
pattern at layer L  !=  the undifferentiated set of all carriers at L
claimed identity at L = persistence under declared variation and intervention
```

The last line is a selected operational rule, not a theorem about metaphysical
identity.

### 1A. Native-term custody and limits `[B-external/I]`

The map deliberately joins unlike scientific and philosophical terms. Their
native sources constrain the join; they do not prove it:

- [Johannsen's genotype conception](https://doi.org/10.1086/279202) separates
  hereditary constitution from observed phenotype and permits phenotypic
  variation within a genotypically homogeneous line.
- [Waddington's *The Epigenotype*](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyr184) names
  developmental processes between genotype and phenotype. It does not license
  equating epigenotype with peptides or one static molecular inventory.
- [Fraga et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0500398102) reported epigenetic
  and expression divergence among monozygotic twins; shared genotype does not
  force identical regulatory or phenotypic history.
- [Dawkins's extended-phenotype paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1978.tb01823.x)
  extends the causal reach of replicator effects beyond one body. It does not
  make every environmental consequence a later ontological compartment.
- [Hutchins](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1903_1) analyzes cognition
  distributed across people and artifacts, while
  [List and Pettit](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199591565.001.0001)
  supply a major group-agency neighbor. Neither establishes one collective
  phenomenal subject.

Accordingly, the six-layer sequence is an Emergentist selection `[S/I]`, not
an inherited scientific taxonomy. Its empirical generality remains `[C]`.

## 2. Bottom-up construction and top-down return

The stack retains its two readings:

```text
Genotype -> Epigenotype -> Phenotype -> Extended Phenotype
         -> Memotype -> Egregoreotype
```

and the conjectural mediated return:

```text
Egregoreotype trace
  -> memotype selection
  -> extended-phenotype construction
  -> phenotypic behavior and environment
  -> epigenetic response
  -> possible selection effects across generations
```

Every arrow owes an actual D4 carrier and mechanism. The upper pattern does not
reach backward through the stack by magic, and D5 represented content never
pushes matter by itself. The return path can fail at any edge without erasing
the layer distinctions.

## 3. Parent before subtype

Let `E(e)` mean that `e` passes the complete five-marker parent test in
[`Stigmergy and the Egregoreotype`](00_STIGMERGY_AND_THE_EGREGOROTYPE.md):

```text
E(e) := persistent trace
        + carrier turnover
        + intervention-sensitive selection reweighting
        + recurrent objective-like bias
        + visible substrate and bearer cost
```

No strategic subtype may bypass `E(e)`.

The user's phrase *agentified egregore* is retained as provenance. The
canonical technical name is **StrategicEgregoreotypeCandidate** because
*agentified* too easily implies actual autonomy, intention, or consciousness.

First record representation:

```text
AgentRepresentationRecord := {
  targetCandidateId,
  attributorIds,
  actorModelRef,
  representationTraceRefs,
  attributedPolicy,
  scope,
  horizon,
  uptakeMeasure,
  evidenceRefs,
  tier,
  rivalModels,
  killCriterion
}
```

Then test strategy:

```text
R(e,a) := E(e)
          + an explicit actor-model a
          + named attributors and representation traces
          + persistence of that representation across carrier turnover

S(e,a) := R(e,a)
          + prospective and counterfactual policy predictions
          + intervention-sensitive uptake by actual carriers
          + cross-context recurrence or adaptation
          + incremental predictive value beyond named rivals
```

`S(e,a)` names a `StrategicEgregoreotypeCandidate`. It does not say that the
collective experiences, intends, owns, consents, signs, authorizes, deserves,
or exists independently of its carriers.

## 4. The reflexive game channel

The proposed mechanism is entirely actual and carrier-mediated:

```text
ActorModelTrace(D4)
  -> ExpectedResponseInCarrierModel(D4)
  -> CarrierChoice(D4)
  -> DistributedEnactment(D4)
  -> OutcomeAndAttribution(D4)
  -> TraceUpdate(D4)
```

An illustrative game model is:

```text
U_i(a | M_i) = U_i^ordinary(a) + E_{M_i}[Response_e | a]
```

This is notation for a testable decomposition, not a universal utility law.
`M_i` is the actual model used by carrier `i`; `Response_e` is the response
that carrier attributes to represented actor `e`. Material incentives,
commands, sanctions, reputation, common cause, demand effects, and observer
narrative remain rivals.

The mechanism can become self-reinforcing:

1. carriers share a representation of one actor;
2. carriers attribute a policy, reward, punishment, or likely move to it;
3. those expectations alter present choices;
4. many choices jointly produce consequences associated with the actor;
5. those consequences strengthen, revise, split, or kill the shared actor-model.

The crucial distinction is:

> **At the memotype level, many tokens and practices may carry a recurrent
> meaning-pattern. At the strategic Egregoreotype level, many actual carriers
> may enact one represented actor-model.** `[I/C]`

The first concerns cultural pattern persistence. The second adds attributed
policy, strategic uptake, and rival-resistant prediction. Neither establishes
one distributed phenomenal mind.

## 5. The theological example and its fence

Suppose a person chooses `x` rather than `y` because the person expects Yahweh
to bless `x` and punish `y`. This is admissible evidence about the person's
represented policy, expectation, and action channel. Similar analysis can be
applied to a state, market, corporation, nation, ancestor, platform, or future
AI represented as one actor.

The analysis does **not** decide whether Yahweh—or any other represented
actor—exists independently, is conscious, actually caused the later event, or
has the attributed policy. Methodological neutrality runs both ways: a social
mechanism neither proves nor disproves a theological or metaphysical claim.

The reflexive channel has established neighbors without inheriting their
results. [Merton's self-fulfilling-prophecy analysis](https://doi.org/10.2307/4609267)
shows how shared expectations can help produce the state they anticipate. In
one economic-game study,
[Shariff and Norenzayan](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01983.x)
reported changed allocations after religious primes, with secular moral-
institution primes producing at least as large an effect. That is evidence for
ordinary representation and monitoring channels in the tested setting—not for
a supernatural actor, general Egregoreotype status, or the strategic subtype.
Replication, demand, and institutional-monitoring rivals remain open.

## 6. Persistence and death

An organism lives and dies through an organismal boundary. A strategic
Egregoreotype has no such boundary by definition. Its operational continuity
depends on the declared traces, carriers, representation, uptake, and policy
tests. It operationally dies, fissions, or loses subtype status when those
conditions fail—not merely when one carrier dies.

Possible outcomes must remain distinct:

- **turnover survival:** the actor-model and its strategic effect persist;
- **fission:** carriers stabilize incompatible actor-models;
- **hollow name:** the label persists but no intervention-sensitive uptake
  remains;
- **ordinary institution:** commands or incentives explain the behavior
  without the subtype;
- **observer story:** coherence appears only retrospectively;
- **death:** the trace, recurrence, or carrier uptake no longer meets the
  declared persistence criterion.

Legal personhood and moral personhood are separate again. A legally recognized
organization may fail the Egregoreotype test; a pattern that passes may lack
legal or moral personhood.

## 8. Tests, rivals, kills, survivors

Minimum strategic suite:

1. **Parent gate:** all five parent markers pass.
2. **Referent test:** independently elicit what carriers think the actor is and
   predicts; a shared name without shared policy content fails.
3. **Frame intervention:** change or scramble the singular actor framing while
   holding material incentives and trace access as constant as possible.
4. **Policy perturbation:** alter the attributed reward, punishment, or move and
   preregister the predicted behavioral response.
5. **Prospective prediction:** predict novel cases before outcomes are known.
6. **Turnover/fission:** replace carriers or split trace access.
7. **Rival comparison:** compare direct command, current incentives, current
   carriers, common cause, ordinary memory, demand effects, and observer story.
8. **Cost and Justice separation:** expose who pays without turning descriptive
   effectiveness into legitimacy.

Kill `StrategicEgregoreotypeCandidate` status when actor framing or attributed
policy adds no prospective, intervention-sensitive value beyond the rivals.
Kill the umbrella term *Emergentotype* if carrier-profile records add no
discrimination beyond the existing six layer names.

What survives a kill:

- the six-layer Replicator Stack;
- the parent five-marker Egregoreotype test;
- the word/token/concept/memotype distinctions;
- ordinary models of incentives, institutions, command, memory, religion, and
  collective behavior;
- the general lesson that attributed actors can enter a person's model even
  when the stronger collective subtype fails.
