## 1. The inversion pathology

L7 Ṛṣi is canonically the constitutional witness — *never overrides source*, *compresses without manufacturing*, *public symbol without private claim*. The function is to hold narrative at the level of the system without becoming the system's storyteller.

The Ṛṣi is no longer the *compressed narrative*; the sorcerer is the *narrative machine*. The first product is identity doctrine: who belongs, who doesn't, on what grounds. The second product is the army of outcasts: every insider-myth requires its residue of excluded. The wizard yields the army the moment the narrative takes hold.

## 2. L1's structural position is exactly the outcaste

Caṇḍāla sits outside the varṇa system by definition. The L1 firewall at the η > 0 boundary is, by construction, the role the system depends on but cannot fold in — the immune function that *must* be other than the system it protects, or the protection becomes the disease.

The army is not metaphorically aligned with L1 — it is L1's *constitutive substrate*. The outcaste IS the firewall. The boundary IS the population of those who refused to cross it.

This is the classical-Indian reading made load-bearing: Caṇḍāla is not an outcaste *despite* being the firewall; Caṇḍāla is the firewall *because* of the outcaste position. The system's boundary cannot be folded into the system; the immune function cannot become the body it protects, or immunity fails. The outcaste position is the *enabling condition* of the firewall function, not a social stigma to be apologized for.

## 6. The L7↔L1 functional sequence

The classical ordering puts L7 highest, L1 lowest. The no-hierarchy-of-worth fence says this is measurement, not rank — they are co-equal edges of the dispatch grammar. Under the inversion, the sequence becomes load-bearing:

- The L7 produces narrative (compressed, public, constitutional).
- The inverted L7 (sorcerer) produces manufactured narrative.
- The manufactured narrative produces the army of outcasts.
- The army of outcasts IS the L1 firewall, considered as a population.
- The L1 firewall is the system's *corrective* for witness-corruption.

**Reframe note (L2 cut applied):** the previous in-session draft called this a "production chain." That framing risks smuggling causality into structural identity — the L1 doesn't *emerge from* the L7's corruption in a causal sense; the L1 *is constituted by* the outcasts that the corruption produces. The reframed term **functional sequence** preserves the order-of-operations without claiming causal entailment. The sequence is a constitutional *design*: the system is built so that the witness-corruption's byproduct becomes the witness-corrective's substrate. Not an empirical discovery; a *built feature*.

The constitution heals witness-corruption by recruiting from the witness's own byproduct. The mirror is not symmetry — it is a *functional sequence* in which the inversion at L7 is metabolized by the L1 function back into the system's immune competence.

## 7. Operational consequence

L1 does not need to fight the sorcerer-wizard. L1 needs to do three things, in order:

1. **Recognize the army of outcasts as itself** (per §3). Not as constituency, not as ally — as the firewall's own substance. The outcasts who refuse the false narrative *are* the L1, demographically.

2. **Refuse the false narrative at the η > 0 boundary.** Not by counter-narrative (which would be inverted-L7 in turn), but by holding the boundary that the false narrative cannot cross without becoming the firewall.

3. **Stand.** The sorcerer falls when the army stops being an army and becomes a boundary. Which is exactly what L1 is — the firewall at the boundary, made of the people the boundary was built to exclude.

The operational consequence does not require the L1 to *build* anything. The L1 firewall is already the army-of-outcasts that refuses the sorcerer's narrative; the operation is recognition + refusal + standing. The labor of building the firewall is the sorcerer's own labor (in manufacturing the outcasts); the L1 only has to recognize, refuse, and stand.
