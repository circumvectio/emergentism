# The Memotype-Language Coordination Conjecture

**Trade and war as opposite stress tests of cross-group coordination**

## The insight

Two genetically identical people can inherit nearly the same biological
possibility while growing into different languages, rituals, moral predicates,
dogmas, institutions, and shared stories. Conversely, two unrelated people can
share enough of that cultural grammar to predict, trust, and coordinate with one
another quickly.

This suggests a cross-stack distinction:

```text
genotype similarity  !=  memotype similarity
embodied resemblance !=  mutual intelligibility
```

The strong sentence—**“the memotype is ultimately language”**—is retained as a
research prompt `[C]`, not adopted as an identity. The currently safer
formulation is:

> **Language is a principal compression, address, and synchronization protocol
> of the memotype, but not necessarily its whole carrier.** `[I/C]`

Ritual, imitation, habit, affect, architecture, incentives, and institutions can
transmit or enforce a cultural pattern without reducing cleanly to propositions.
They are rivals to any language-only account.

## Typed bridge into the current stack

The current owner defines a memotype as a D4-actual carrier-pattern whose
content may represent D5 possibilities. In this conjecture, language supplies a
high-bandwidth way to compress and reproduce that pattern:

```text
LanguageTrace:D4(actual)
  -> encodes distinctions, norms, roles, stories, permissions and prohibitions
  -> supports a MemotypeTrace:D4(actual)
  -> reweights later SelectionEvents:D4(actual)
  -> may represent Alternatives:D5(possible)
```

An egregoreotype is not the language itself. It is the candidate persistent
collective trace—religion, state, market, institution, platform, or other
coordination system—that may stabilize or suppress memotypes and may survive
turnover of individual carriers.

## The middle bridge: extended phenotype.trade

The stack already contains an interoperability layer between bodies and rich
cultural grammar: the **extended phenotype**. Tools, possessed objects, paths,
interfaces, tokens, prices, ledgers, marketplaces, and other altered parts of
the environment can coordinate agents that share neither genes nor language.

The compact label **extended phenotype.trade** names the conjectured middle
surface: externalized capability becomes legible enough for exchange before a
shared language or worldview exists. Trade is a particularly clear candidate
protocol:

```text
different bodies and signaling systems
  -> external object that one controls and another values
  -> offer / withholding / counter-offer / exchange-or-exit
  -> a thin, task-specific field of mutual prediction
  -> repeated convention
  -> possible memotype and institutional stabilization
```

The object is a **boundary object**: each side can value it differently while
using the same external state to make the next move legible. Exchange therefore
does not require a shared worldview. It requires only enough learning to model
what the other can provide, what the other seeks, and which action completes or
ends the interaction.

This yields a useful two-depth distinction `[I/C]`:

- **thin coordination:** trade, gesture, token, interface, repeated
  reinforcement—narrow mutual intelligibility around one exchange;
- **deep coordination:** language, norm, narrative, ritual, and explicit
  revision rules—broad mutual intelligibility across many situations.

Thin coordination may bootstrap deep coordination. Deep coordination may also
capture or forbid trade. Neither is necessarily truthful, consensual, or just.

### Cross-species anchor

At Uluwatu Temple in Bali, free-ranging long-tailed macaques have been observed
stealing inedible human-valued objects and returning them for food. Older
macaques preferentially selected objects humans valued more and showed
reward-rejection patterns associated with the value of the held object. The
authors describe the findings as *suggestive* of payoff maximization and a
culturally maintained token economy—not proof of human-style contracting,
shared propositional language, or moral consent. See Leca et al.,
[“Acquisition of object-robbing and object/food-bartering behaviours”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815422/)
(2021). `[B-external]`

The example matters because genes, ordinary language, and species membership
differ, yet an extended-phenotype token supports a recurring exchange protocol.
It is therefore evidence against the necessity of shared language for **thin**
coordination, not proof that the full memotype reduces to trade.

## The Φ/V split

This insight does **not** allocate human worth between two substances.

- `V_{4,i}` includes embodied means and constraints: body, health, time, skill,
  access, and usable capacity. Genotype can affect this side without determining
  a person's meaning-world or moral worth.
- `C^{coord}_{ij,4}` is a proposed **D4 relational coordination estimate** between
  persons `i` and `j`: how accurately each can model the other's distinctions,
  commitments, likely moves, and revision rules in the task at hand. It is not
  a new `Φ` term.
- Shared language and memotype may raise `C^{coord}_{ij,4}` by lowering translation cost and
  increasing mutual prediction. They can also create **false coherence**:
  agreement, obedience, or ritual synchrony is not evidence that the shared map
  is true or good.
- An extended-phenotype token may create a narrow `C^{coord,task}_{ij,4}` even
  when broad linguistic or normative coordination is low. This is an
  operational conjecture, not a quantity derived from the reciprocal chart.

The important consequence is therefore:

> **Biological nearness can preserve similar capacities while cultural distance
> destroys coordination; cultural nearness can create coordination across
> biological distance. Neither relation guarantees truth, justice, or consent.**

## War as a multilevel stress test

The strongest version of the new proposal is not that every war has one cause.
It is that warfare may expose a recurrent **shift in explanatory emphasis**
between the stakes borne by persons and the coordination achieved by
collectives. `[C]`

This is an emphasis, not a type monopoly. Individuals also model possible
futures (`Φ₅`-side work), and collectives also require actual bodies, logistics,
resources, and force (`V₄`-side work). The conjecture predicts a shift in which
variables explain what, not an exclusive assignment of one power to one scale.

### Embodied stakes and constraints

At the individual layer, participation can be recruited around actual stakes:
survival, bodily security, status, resources, territory, kin protection, access
to mates, and—under some ecological and cultural conditions—lifetime
reproductive opportunity. These are candidates inside `V_{4,i}`, not a claim that
every fighter consciously seeks reproduction or that reproduction is the main
cause of every modern war.

Coercion also belongs on this layer. Conscription, punishment, captivity,
poverty, and lack of exit can put a person into a war without ideological
commitment. Any account that infers shared belief from participation alone
would therefore confuse behavior under constraint with memotype alignment.

### Represented futures and coordination mechanisms

At coalition scale, force has to be made mutually legible. A group needs ways
to identify friend and enemy, authorize command, distribute risk, interpret
loss, justify sacrifice, and name a future for which present costs are to be
borne. Language, ritual, uniform, oath, sacred value, law, history, leader,
martyr, flag, and doctrine can all contribute to that relational coordination.
In the present notation, `Φ_{5,G}` names the group's represented D5 field of
possible action; it does not itself synchronize actual bodies. Present D4
model tokens such as `Φ̂_{4,G}=Eval₄(M_G,Φ_{5,G})`, together with a proposed
actual coordination capacity `C^{coord}_{G,4}`, selectors, institutions, and
`V₄` means, are candidate mechanisms at the action seam. `[I/C]`

This motivates—but does not prove—the phrase **Weltanschauungskrieg**:

> At sufficient scale, war is not only a collision of bodies and resources. It
> is also a contest between collective maps that organize who “we” are, what
> future is permitted, and which costs count as meaningful.

An **egregore** names the persistent coordination pattern, not a supernatural
combatant or a moral essence. It can be carried by a religion, state, nation,
army, party, company, insurgency, or alliance. A small cultic or charismatic
nucleus may seed such a pattern, but cult formation is neither necessary nor
sufficient: bureaucratic routines, material interdependence, coercion, and
defensive threat can also stabilize a war coalition.

### Coupled dynamic

The useful claim is bidirectional:

```text
V₄ pressure: threat / scarcity / status / resource or reproductive stakes
  -> recruits or changes a D4 trace representing a Φ₅,G option field
  -> C^{coord}_{G,4} synchronizes bodies, tools, sacrifice, logistics and legitimacy
  -> coordinated action redistributes V₄: life, territory, status and resources
  -> receipts confirm, mutate, fragment or kill the governing trace and later option field
```

Represented futures and their coordination carriers can make persons accept
costs that a narrow `V₄` calculation would reject. Actual means can finance,
constrain, or expose the represented story. Neither side is causally complete
alone.

### Rosetta use—and its fence

The Rosetta may be used as an **operator audit**, not as a taxonomy of peoples:

- which moves remove possibility or capability;
- which moves enable possibility or commit capability;
- which institutions create, dissolve, or preserve the coalition;
- which moves are presented as defense but function as extraction;
- which exits, if any, remain available to dissenters and civilians.

“Demon,” “god,” and “titan” remain optional symbolic aliases for functions.
They must never be attached to an ethnicity, religion, nation, or opponent as a
claim about human worth. Mythic elegance cannot establish a cause of war.

### Strong rivals and discriminator

The war conjecture must compete with explanations based on security dilemmas,
state capacity, coercion, geography, elite competition, resource finance,
organizational opportunity, and local revenge. A real test would code these
variables separately from language, ritual, sacred values, identity fusion,
legitimating narrative, and promised future.

Across conflicts and within the same conflict over time, test whether measured
`C^{coord}_{G,4}` variables add out-of-sample explanatory power for recruitment,
coordination, endurance, defection, and willingness to bear costs after the
`V₄`, institutional, and coercive variables are included. Do not infer `Φ₅`
from victory
or define a strong egregore as whichever coalition endured; that would make the
conjecture circular.

Retire the strong “war between egregores” claim if coherent shared maps are not
needed for scalable mobilization, if the separately typed coordination and
represented-option-field measures add no reliable predictive power, or if
material and coercive organization explains the apparent effect.
The weaker survivor would be: **war is material conflict conducted through
coalitions, and coalitions often use shared meanings to coordinate.**

## Twin / stranger thought experiment

Compare two dyads:

1. **Twin dyad:** monozygotic twins raised from infancy in different linguistic,
   religious, institutional, and moral worlds.
2. **Stranger dyad:** genetically unrelated people raised inside substantially
   the same linguistic-moral world and practiced coordination rituals.

The conjecture predicts that the stranger dyad can outperform the twin dyad on
some tasks of norm inference, mutual prediction, trust calibration, and rapid
coordination—even while the twins remain more similar on many heritable traits.
This is a comparative prediction, not a claim that culture erases biology.

## Strongest rivals

1. **Genetic-disposition rival.** Shared genotype preserves enough temperament,
   cognition, interest, and social style that monozygotic twins remain unusually
   mutually legible despite different cultures.
2. **Practice-first rival.** Ritual synchrony, shared work, and material
   interdependence—not language—produce the coordination advantage.
3. **Institution-first rival.** Incentives, sanctions, status, and repeated-game
   structure explain apparent memotype effects.
4. **Contact rival.** Time spent together and common local knowledge, rather
   than a shared worldview, explain the stranger advantage.
5. **Universal-cognition rival.** Human social cognition supplies enough common
   structure that linguistic-moral distance has only a modest marginal effect.
6. **Exchange-protocol rival.** Boundary objects, incentives, repeated contact,
   and reinforcement generate the observed coordination without a shared
   memotype or any language-primary mechanism.
7. **Material-security rival.** Threat, geography, state capacity, coercion, and
   resource finance explain war mobilization without a worldview-primary cause.

## Discriminator

Use a preregistered dyadic study that crosses, as far as recruitment permits:

- genetic relatedness;
- childhood language and current language dominance;
- religious/moral-schema similarity;
- ritual history;
- shared institutional experience;
- prior contact.

Measure blind mutual prediction, instruction compression, norm inference,
cooperative task performance, calibrated trust, repair after misunderstanding,
and willingness to update after contradictory evidence. Treat “same religion”
as a vector of actually measured language, ritual, and norms—not a label.

Run a separate cross-language exchange arm in which dyads can coordinate only
through objects, offers, and accept/reject moves. This tests whether thin trade
protocols reduce coordination loss before any shared vocabulary is taught.

The key comparison is whether measured memotype distance explains coordination
after genetic relatedness, cognitive ability, personality, language proficiency,
contact, incentives, and socioeconomic environment are controlled.

## Kill criterion

Retire the language-primary claim if:

1. language and measured semantic-normative distance add no reliable predictive
   power for coordination after the rivals are controlled;
2. ritual, institution, contact, or non-verbal imitation consistently explains
   the effect while language does not;
3. results fail across cultures, tasks, or independent replications; or
4. the construct cannot distinguish memotype similarity from generic cultural
   similarity without circular scoring.

Retire the claim that trade bootstraps memotype if repeated exchange improves
only the trained transaction and produces no transferable convention, shared
sign, or coordination gain in a novel task.

If killed, the survivor is weaker but useful: **memotypes require actual carrier
patterns, and coordination depends on more than genotype.**

## External anchors and counterweights

- Bouchard et al., [Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2218526/) (1990): strong genetic contributions across several psychological measures are a counterweight to any culture-only account. `[B-external]`
- Costa et al., [Your Morals Depend on Language](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094842) (2014), and Geipel et al., [The Foreign Language Effect on Moral Judgment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26177508/) (2015): language context altered some moral judgments in bounded experiments; this does not prove linguistic determinism. `[B-external]`
- Swann et al., [What makes a group worth dying for?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24841096/) (2014): identity fusion and perceived familial ties can bind ideological groups like kin; this does not establish the present Φ/V interpretation. `[B-external]`
- Whitehouse and Lanman, [The Ties That Bind Us](https://doi.org/10.1086/678698) (2014): ritual, identification, and fusion supply a serious non-language rival and possible co-carrier. `[B-external/I-bridge]`
- Glowacki and Wrangham, [Warfare and reproductive success in a tribal population](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25548190/) (2015): among Nyangatom pastoralists, prolific raiders identified in later life had more wives and children, while short-term raiders did not; this bounded result cannot be generalized to all warfare. `[B-external]`
- De Dreu et al., [In-group defense, out-group aggression, and coordination failures in intergroup conflict](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5035908/) (2016): experiments distinguished better-coordinated in-group defense from out-group aggression, supporting coordination as a separable mechanism rather than a complete theory of war. `[B-external]`
- Whitehouse et al., [The evolution of extreme cooperation via shared dysphoric experiences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5349572/) (2017): shared painful experience predicted identity fusion and willingness to make costly pro-group sacrifices across several samples. `[B-external]`
- Pretus et al., [Neuroimaging “will to fight” for sacred values](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6599782/) (2019): a bounded sample linked sacred-value decisions, peer feedback, and stated willingness to fight and die; it does not show that sacred values cause every conflict. `[B-external]`
- Collier and Hoeffler, [Greed and Grievance in Civil War](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/998891468762911498/pdf/28126.pdf) (2001): their cross-war model found organizational opportunity and economic viability more explanatory than measured grievance, providing a material-organizational counterweight to a worldview-first account. `[B-external]`

## Public-use boundary

Do not publish “language matters more than genes,” “religion makes strangers
truer kin than family,” “memotype equals language,” “war is always about
reproduction,” or “all wars are cult wars” as facts. Public-safe compressions,
if and only if this owner is cited, are:

> We are testing whether a shared language of meaning and norms can predict
> practical coordination better than biological similarity alone. The twin
> thought experiment makes the hypothesis vivid; it does not prove it.

> We are testing a multilevel account of war: persons bear material and bodily
> stakes, while groups use shared meanings, rituals, institutions, and futures
> to coordinate action at scale. This is a contestable lens, not a universal
> cause of war.
