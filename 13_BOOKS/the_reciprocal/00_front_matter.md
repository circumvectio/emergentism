# The Reciprocal

This book is not asking you to join anything.

That has to be said first, because the subject matter makes capture easy. A framework that
describes how institutions capture people is exactly the kind of thing that captures people.
It arrives with vocabulary, a diagnosis, a sense of being early, and a quiet promise that
whoever holds it sees more clearly than whoever does not. That promise is the failure mode,
not the feature.

So the first instruction is simple. **Do not believe this book too quickly.** Use it. Test
it. Put it down when direct seeing is available without it.

## Three ways to keep a book at a distance

**Agreement** feels generous, and it can become sleep. The mind says yes, yes, yes, and
turns the pages, and nothing is examined because nothing was resisted.

**Dismissal** feels intelligent, and it can become defence. The mind finds an overstatement,
a term it dislikes, one weak example, and uses that to avoid the part that would have cost
something.

**Conversion** feels like energy, and it can become capture with better vocabulary. The
reader stops testing and starts recruiting. This is the worst of the three, because it looks
like success.

Agreement, dismissal and conversion are all ways of not being touched. The better relation
is apprenticeship without submission: let the argument work on perception, let your own
examples test the claims, and keep the exit in view the whole time.

## What this book argues

It begins from a feeling many people already have — that something has gone wrong in the
relation between institutions and the functions they were built to perform. School still
names education. Medicine still names health. Democracy still names self-government.
Platforms still name connection. The forms remain; the functions feel thinner.

The book calls that condition the **cargo cult**: form without the living function that once
justified the form. It then asks the only question that matters about a cargo cult, which is
not whether the ritual looks right but whether the planes come.

## The small apparatus

The apparatus is deliberately small, because a large one becomes a language, and a language
becomes a membership.

```text
φ · ν = 1        an invariant inside the declared model
B = sin θ        balance, read as a coordinate on that model
```

And one law stated in words rather than symbols, for a reason given below:

> **Living potential is governed by a two-factor AND-class law.** Coherence and viability
> are **jointly necessary**. Neither substitutes for the other, and a conjunctive law
> returns nothing at all when either factor is absent.

The earlier edition of this book wrote that law as a product and used it to rank things.
Both moves have since been withdrawn by this corpus. The product form is retired as a
ranking, and the ranking use is the specific error: two factors that are jointly necessary
tell you *whether* something can flourish, not *how much* it flourishes relative to
something else. What survives is the conjunction, and the conjunction is enough. It is the
question the book keeps returning to:

*Are coherence and viability holding each other, or has one begun to devour the other?*

Coherence without viability becomes beautiful impotence — an immaculate structure about
nothing. Viability without coherence becomes efficient extraction — a machine that works and
should not.

## What the translations are for, and what they are not

The book reads its pattern through several traditions: institutional, economic,
psychological, symbolic, contemplative. That is a **translation lens**, and a translation
lens transfers no proof. One pattern rendered through many traditions is one pattern plus
several readings — never several independent confirmations of it. Where a resemblance is
striking, that is a reason to go and check the domain on its own terms, not a reason to
count it as evidence.

Myth can illuminate. It cannot coerce. Geometry can clarify. It cannot guarantee the world.

## How to check the book

Every substantive claim carries a tier, and the tiers are not decoration:

- **[A]** analytic or machine-checked — follows from stated premises, and says nothing about
  whether the premises describe anything real.
- **[B]** receipted — there is a dated artefact you can open.
- **[S]** selected — a considered choice, defensible as a choice, not a discovery.
- **[I]** interpretive — a reading, offered as one.
- **[C]** conjecture.
- **[D]** draft.

Canonical status, publication, beauty, adoption, survival and authority are **not** evidence
tiers. If a sentence in this book seems to be doing more work than its tier allows, that is a
defect in the book and you should treat it as one.

**No equation in this book derives an ought.** Where it moves from structure to conduct, that
move is a selection, marked as one, and you are entitled to decline it.

## What the book has not got

It has no outside result. This project keeps a public ledger of outcomes, including the
ones that went against it, and at the time of this edition **not one entry came from anyone
outside the project**. Every verdict was reached by the same people who wrote the framework,
using their own instruments.

That is the largest single gap in the case, it is measured rather than felt, and it is why
nothing here is described as validated. A framework can be internally immaculate and still be
about nothing.

The last chapter asks you to close that gap by one. That is the whole ask, and the exit
stays open whether or not you take it.
