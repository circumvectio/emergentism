# THE NEGATIVE-SPACE OUTLINE — 127 ADMISSIBLE IMPOSSIBILITY AND LIMIT THEOREMS

**Placement rule used.** A theorem sits in its **home register** = the register where the *forbidden thing* lives. It appears in **CROSSINGS** only when the prohibition is a **conjunction** across two registers — the theorem forbids property-at-A *together with* property-at-B, and neither half alone is forbidden. A premise imported from another register (AC in Banach–Tarski, a degree fact in Wantzel, Hilbert-space geometry in Kochen–Specker) is a *fence note*, not a crossing. This rule is what kills the register inflation the cross-check caught.

**Corrections applied.** All 16 cross-check corrections are in force. Additionally I extended the cross-check's anti-inflation ruling to two entries it did not reach, and flag them here as my own calls: **CH independence** retyped D1×D2 → **pure D1** (the cardinality of the continuum is set-theoretic, not geometric — same reasoning that retyped Löwenheim–Skolem); **Kuratowski** retyped D2×D5 → **pure D2** (an abstract graph is a combinatorial structure, not a D5 representation — the prohibition is a genus obstruction). On the D3 register-definition question the cross-check put to the chair, I ruled **(b): D3 is the quantum register**, so the six classical-Shannon entries are typed pure D5. Count reconciles to the cross-check's 127.

## 1. THE OUTLINE TABLE

### D1 — DISTINCTION / ARITHMETIC / LOGIC (21)

| Register | Theorem | Owner + year | What it forbids | The fence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Cantor's theorem | Georg Cantor, 1891 | Self-enumeration: no set surjects onto its own powerset — no system indexes its own space of possibilities from within | Holds in ZF; the no-surjection form is constructively valid (needs neither AC nor LEM). Makes no claim about physical infinities. Inside a model of set theory it is Skolem-relative: a countable model satisfies it while being countable from outside |
| D1 | First Incompleteness | Kurt Gödel 1931; Rosser 1936 | Completeness together with effective axiomatizability, above the arithmetical threshold Q | NOT "there are unprovable truths" absolutely — G_F is provable in stronger systems. Presburger, Skolem, and real closed fields are complete AND decidable; the trigger is +&× with enough induction to interpret Q. Needs an r.e. axiom set. Says nothing about minds exceeding machines |
| D1 | Second Incompleteness | Gödel 1931; first full proof Hilbert–Bernays 1939 | Self-grounding: no consistent sufficiently strong system proves its own consistency | INTENSIONAL (Feferman 1960) — depends on Con(F) actually *expressing* consistency; deviant provability predicates prove formal "consistency" sentences. Requires HBL conditions D1–D3. Does NOT forbid consistency proofs: Gentzen 1936 proved Con(PA) via ε₀. SELF-certification is forbidden, not certification |
| D1 | Undefinability of truth | Alfred Tarski, 1933/1936 | A total semantic mirror inside a system: "…is true" for L cannot live in L | Truth for L IS definable in a stronger metalanguage. Needs classical bivalence + full self-reference; Kripke 1975 fixed points buy a self-applicable truth predicate by surrendering bivalence. **PARTIAL truth predicates ARE definable** (Σₙ-truth is Σₙ-definable) — "a system can know nothing about its own truth" is an overclaim |
| D1 | Löb's theorem | Martin Hugo Löb, 1955 | Self-trust: "if I can prove it, it is true" is unassertible for anything not already proved outright | Requires HBL conditions and a natural provability predicate (same intensionality caveat as Gödel II). Reflection *instances* for already-proved sentences are fine; only nontrivial reflection is fenced. It constrains formal derivability, not informal justification — applying it to agents needs a defended mapping to a predicate satisfying D1–D3 |
| D1 | Entscheidungsproblem + Halting | Alonzo Church; Alan Turing, 1936 | A universal decision procedure: no algorithm decides arbitrary termination, nor arbitrary logical consequence | The Church–Turing **THESIS** is not a theorem — never present it as proved. Church's theorem needs an at-least-binary relation: monadic FOL, Bernays–Schönfinkel, Presburger, RCF, S1S/S2S are decidable. Halting is decidable for finite-state and total languages. Undecidability is worst-case and UNIFORM |
| D1 | Rice's theorem | H. G. Rice, **1953** (Trans. AMS 74, 358–366) | A general procedure reading a program and deciding what it means or does | ⚠️ **1951 is the thesis year — the theorem is 1953.** Property must be EXTENSIONAL: "under 100 lines", "halts in 500 steps" are decidable. Must be non-trivial. Sound-but-incomplete static analysis, total languages, and finite-state models all decide plenty — which is why model checking exists |
| D1 | Trakhtenbrot's theorem | Boris Trakhtenbrot, 1950 | A complete effective proof calculus for the finite — restricting to finite worlds costs completeness | Needs an at-least-binary relation. Exactly DUAL to the unrestricted case: over all structures validity is r.e.; over finite structures satisfiability is r.e. and validity is not. Does NOT say finite model *checking* is undecidable — quantification over ALL finite structures is what bites |
| D1 | Löwenheim–Skolem(–Tarski) | Löwenheim 1915; Skolem 1920/22 | Any first-order axiom set pinning down a unique infinite structure up to isomorphism | FAILS for full second-order semantics and infinitary logics; does not touch finite models. Skolem's "paradox" is not a contradiction — uncountability is not absolute between models; the paradox *reading* is chaff. Second-order categoricity restores determinacy only if the metatheory's powerset is taken at face value |
| D1 | Tennenbaum's theorem | Stanley Tennenbaum, 1959 | Computable ACCESS to the deviant models LS guarantees: no countable nonstandard model of PA is recursive | Applies to PA and fragments with enough induction; the boundary for very weak fragments (IΔ₀) is open. Th(ℕ,+) HAS computable nonstandard models — this is a fact about multiplication-plus-induction. Does not refute LS: the models exist, they cannot be computably presented. **The sharpest fence against reading LS as "arithmetic could secretly be about something else"** |
| D1 | Lindström's theorems I & II | Per Lindström, 1969 | Expressive strength together with compactness and Löwenheim–Skolem — the price list for any logic is fixed in advance | "Strongest" = maximal EXPRESSIVE POWER, not proof-theoretic strength. The minimal closure conditions do real work; logics violating them (positive, intuitionistic, many-valued) get separate characterizations. Carries no normative claim that FOL is "the right logic" — it fixes the trade-off, not the choice |
| D1 | Incompleteness of second-order logic (full semantics) | Corollary of Gödel 1931 + Church/Turing 1936 | Categoricity and effective proof at the same time | FULL semantics only. Under Henkin semantics SOL HAS a sound complete calculus — but categoricity is lost and LS reapplies. Sound-but-incomplete SOL calculi exist and are used. It makes categoricity non-effective, not useless |
| D1 | Essential + hereditary undecidability of Q | Tarski, Mostowski, R. M. Robinson, 1953 | Escaping incompleteness by ADDING AXIOMS — no effectively writable consistent completion exists | Needs consistency and interpretability of Q; theories not interpreting Q can be decidable (Presburger, RCF, DLO, abelian groups). Undecidability of a THEORY ≠ undecidability of each sentence. This is the fence against "just add the Gödel sentence" |
| D1 | MRDP / Hilbert's Tenth | Matiyasevich 1970, on Davis–Putnam–J. Robinson | A general method for whole-number solvability of polynomial equations | ⚠️ **SCOPE IS THE INTEGERS.** Hilbert 10 over ℚ is STILL OPEN (2026) — never say "Diophantine equations are undecidable" without naming the ring. Restricted classes are decidable; the (degree, variables) boundary is unmapped. About the general uniform problem, not any specific equation |
| D1 | Paris–Harrington | Jeff Paris & Leo Harrington, 1977 | The assumption that Gödel sentences are artificial — ordinary finite combinatorics already outruns PA | Unprovable IN PA, provable in ZFC and PA+TI(ε₀) — a relative limit. "First natural example" is contested (Goodstein/Kirby–Paris 1982; Kruskal/Friedman). The mechanism is growth rate at level ε₀ — the fence is about available induction, not about combinatorics being mysterious |
| D1 | Chaitin's incompleteness | Gregory Chaitin, 1971/1974 | Certification of individual randomness: T proves no "K(x) > c_T", though almost all x satisfy it | ⚠️ **THE POPULAR READING IS FALSE.** Raatikainen (*J. Phil. Logic* 27, 1998, 569–586): the constant is fixed by the chosen coding of universal machines, not by the theory's power — for ANY theory an acceptable indexing makes c_T = 0. **Never cite "a system cannot certify randomness beyond its own complexity."** Only LOWER bounds are fenced; upper bounds are r.e.-approximable |
| D1 | Strictness of the arithmetical hierarchy (with Post's theorem) | Kleene, Post, c. 1943–48 | Eliminating quantifier alternation — nested ∀/∃ depth is irreducible | About DEFINABILITY over ℕ with recursive matrices, not syntactic form: individual formulas collapse; the CLASSES do not. Do NOT transfer to complexity theory — strictness of the polynomial hierarchy is OPEN. Post's *problem* (intermediate r.e. degrees) is a separate affirmative result |
| D1 | Independence of CH from ZFC | Gödel 1938/40 (L); Cohen 1963 (forcing) | ZFC fixing the size of the continuum | RELATIVE consistency, conditional on Con(ZFC) — which by Gödel II ZFC cannot prove. Does NOT establish that CH lacks a truth value: realist programs (large cardinals, forcing axioms, Ultimate-L) aim to settle it, and MM/PFA give 2^ℵ⁰ = ℵ₂ |
| D1 | Baker–Gill–Solovay relativization barrier | Baker, Gill, Solovay, 1975 | An entire family of proof METHODS in advance: no relativizing technique settles P vs NP | A barrier to METHODS, not an independence result — P vs NP is NOT known independent of ZFC. Non-relativizing techniques succeeded (arithmetization → IP = PSPACE), after which the barrier was re-erected as natural proofs (1994) and algebrization (2008). "Relativizes" needs a precise definition and the sharpness is contested |
| D1 | Abel–Ruffini / Galois criterion | Ruffini 1799, Abel 1824; Galois 1830–32 | A universal radical FORMULA for the general quintic (S_n unsolvable for n ≥ 5) | *Routed here from D2 by cross-check.* Quintics CAN be solved — Bring radicals, elliptic/theta functions (Hermite, Kronecker 1858), numerically to any precision. Forbidden: a formula in four operations plus n-th roots, and only for the GENERAL quintic; x⁵−2 = 0 solves by radicals |
| D1 | Transcendence of π | Lindemann 1882 (Weierstrass 1885) | π being the root of any rational polynomial | *Routed here from D2 by cross-check.* Lindemann ALONE forbids nothing geometric — the quadrature prohibition is a composite with Wantzel's degree criterion and is carried in Wantzel's fence. Citing Lindemann as "the circle cannot be squared" launders a composition into a single result |

*Cross-references only (canonical entries above, no independent count):* Tarski → D5 corollary: **stratified/bounded internal audit is lawful; CLOSED self-certification is impossible.** Rice → D5 corollary: **syntactic properties are decidable (which is why formatting gates are cheap); the only lawful repair is a sound-incomplete checker with an explicit UNKNOWN verdict.** Halting, Chaitin → D5 pointers only.

### D2 — CONFIGURATION / GEOMETRY (14)

| Register | Theorem | Owner + year | What it forbids | The fence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D2 | Wantzel's constructibility criterion | P. L. Wantzel, 1837 | Reaching points of degree ≠ 2^k by unmarked straightedge and compass in finitely many exact steps — cube duplication, general trisection, and (with Lindemann) quadrature | "Trisection is impossible" is TOOL-RELATIVE: neusis, origami (Beloch 1936), a conic, or Archimedes' spiral all trisect; 90° trisects; approximation is unobstructed. Quadrature corollary: π transcendental + the degree criterion ⇒ no compass-and-straightedge squaring — but the quadratrix squares it, and by finite dissection with **translations alone** it IS squarable (Laczkovich 1990). The fence is on the move class, never on the shape |
| D2 | Gauss–Wantzel polygon criterion | Gauss 1796 (sufficiency); Wantzel 1837 (necessity) | Exact Euclidean equipartition of the circle for all n except 2^k × distinct Fermat primes | "Distinct" is load-bearing: 9 = 3×3 fails though 3 is Fermat. Only five Fermat primes are KNOWN and a sixth is OPEN — this is a criterion, not a finished list. Origami constructs the 7-gon and 9-gon |
| D2 | Dehn's theorem / Hilbert's Third Problem | Max Dehn 1901; converse Sydler 1965 | Finite polyhedral dissection between equal-volume solids with different Dehn invariants — volume alone is not a complete transfer invariant above dim 2 | **BORN AT DIMENSION 3** — Wallace–Bolyai–Gerwein makes any two equal-area polygons scissors-congruent. Finite dissection by planar cuts only. **Sydler CLOSES it**: the obstruction is exactly two invariants, not open-ended. Hyperbolic/spherical analogues remain open — do not export the completeness claim |
| D2 | Non-existence of a finitely-additive isometry-invariant measure on all subsets of ℝⁿ, n ≥ 3 | Hausdorff 1914; Banach–Tarski 1924; fences Banach 1923, von Neumann 1929 | Total, isometry-invariant, finitely-additive measurement of configuration space in three or more dimensions | ⚠️ Cite the **negative form** — the paradoxical decomposition is affirmative and is chaff. **DIMS 1 AND 2 ARE EXEMPT** (Banach 1923: such measures DO exist on all subsets of ℝ and ℝ²). The cause is not infinity but the ISOMETRY GROUP: the plane's is amenable, SO(3) contains a free rank-2 group. Requires AC (fails in Solovay's model). Pieces are non-measurable, licensing nothing physical. Used as a warning about "unfenced infinity" it is overclaimed by two whole dimensions |
| D2 | Crystallographic restriction | classical (Haüy/Bravais/Fedorov–Schoenflies) | Translational periodicity together with 5-fold or ≥7-fold rotational symmetry in 2 and 3 dimensions | **DIMENSION-BOUND**: n-fold is lattice-compatible in dim d iff φ(n) ≤ d, so φ(5) = 4 makes 5-fold perfectly legal in ℝ⁴. Constrains LATTICES, not long-range order — Shechtman's quasicrystals (1982, Nobel 2011) show sharp 5- and 10-fold diffraction by not being periodic. Citing it to forbid pentagonal order cites it against its own fence |
| D2 | Kepler conjecture (Hales's theorem) | Hales with Ferguson 1998/2005; **Flyspeck formal proof 2014/2017** | Any congruent-ball packing of ℝ³ exceeding density π/√18 ≈ 0.74048 | The optimum is NOT unique — infinitely many packings attain it, so density is bounded without a configuration being forced. Needs CONGRUENT balls in unbounded ℝ³ with density as a limit. Cite the **2014/2017 machine-checked warrant**, not the 1998 announcement refereed only to "99% certain" |
| D2 | Sphere packing optimality in dims 8 and 24 | Viazovska 2016; Cohn–Kumar–Miller–Radchenko–Viazovska 2017 | Exceeding E8 / Leech density in exactly those two dimensions | "Sphere packing is solved" is false — only dims 1, 2, 3, 8, 24 are solved; **dim 4 is OPEN**, as is everything above 24. Cohn–Elkies is provably not sharp in most dimensions. An exceptional-structure result, never a general law |
| D2 | Impossibility of a perfect cubed cube | corollary of Brooks–Smith–Stone–Tutte 1940; Littlewood descent | Dissection of a cube into finitely many all-distinct smaller cubes | The SQUARE case is possible (Duijvestijn's order-21 simple perfect squared square, 1978). Dimension 2 permits, dimension 3 forbids — the **reverse** of the Dehn pattern. Neither is a "higher dimensions are harder" law; each threshold has its own cause |
| D2 | Dubins–Hirsch–Karush scissor-congruence | Dubins, Hirsch, Karush 1963; fence Laczkovich 1990 | Reassembling a disk into a square by finite dissection into Jordan-bounded pieces | ⚠️ **Laczkovich 1990 proved Tarski's circle-squaring IS possible** with translations alone — so this is a REGULARITY impossibility, not an area or measure one. The regularity fence has since been pushed (Marks–Unger 2017: Borel pieces; later, Jordan-measurable with small boundary). Any use must name the piece class or it asserts something false. **The cleanest demonstration in the whole outline that an impossibility indexes an admissible-MOVE CLASS, not the objects** |
| D2 | Hilbert on complete constant-negative-curvature surfaces; Efimov extension | Hilbert 1901; Efimov 1964; fence Nash 1954 / Kuiper 1955 | A complete C² isometric immersion of the hyperbolic plane in ℝ³ (Efimov: any complete surface with K ≤ −c < 0) | ⚠️ **REGULARITY IS THE ENTIRE FENCE** — Nash–Kuiper gives a C¹ isometric embedding of every Riemannian surface, hyperbolic plane included. **Corrected:** flexibility is proved for α < 1/5 (De Lellis–Inauen–Székelyhidi), rigidity for α > 2/3 (Borisov; Conti–De Lellis–Székelyhidi), and **the gap between them is OPEN. C^{1,1/2} is GROMOV'S CONJECTURE — cite it as a conjecture, never as a located bound.** Specific to ℝ³: the hyperbolic plane embeds without difficulty as the hyperboloid in ℝ^{2,1}. COMPLETENESS is essential — local pieces are fine (the pseudosphere) |
| D2 | Theorema Egregium, negative corollary | C. F. Gauss, 1827 | Exact isometric flattening of any positively curved patch — the perfect map | Forbids ISOMETRY only. Conformal (Mercator) and equal-area (Lambert) maps exist and are exactly right about what they preserve. Developable surfaces — cylinder, cone — DO flatten exactly. The obstruction is nonzero Gaussian curvature, not curvature in the everyday sense |
| D2 | Hairy ball (Poincaré–Brouwer) with Poincaré–Hopf | Poincaré 1885; Brouwer 1912 | A globally nonvanishing continuous tangent field on a closed manifold of nonzero Euler characteristic | Only EVEN-dimensional spheres — S¹, S³, S⁷ comb without trouble, and so does the torus. The deciding invariant is χ, not sphere-ness. Only CONTINUITY is required, and only a GLOBAL field is forbidden — local ones always exist |
| D2 | Kuratowski's theorem, negative half | Kuratowski 1930; minor form Wagner 1937 | Planar realisation of K₅ and K₃,₃ — a relational obstruction to a geometric configuration | "K₅ cannot be drawn without crossings" IN THE PLANE OR ON THE SPHERE. Both embed on the TORUS; every finite graph embeds in ℝ³. This is a GENUS obstruction — the right statement names the surface. By Fáry (1948) planar graphs admit straight-line drawings, so straightness adds nothing. Finite graphs only |
| D2 | Schläfli's classification, negative form | Schläfli 1852 (Euclid XIII for dim 3) | Any further regular convex polytope beyond a finite list: 5 in ℝ³, 6 in ℝ⁴, exactly 3 in every d ≥ 5 | Only five CONVEX ones in EUCLIDEAN THREE-SPACE. Drop convexity → four Kepler–Poinsot solids; go to ℝ⁴ → six, including the 24-cell with no 3-D analogue; hyperbolic space and tessellations change the lists again. **The count is 5, 6, then 3 forever — this also forbids dimension-generic intuition.** It forbids nothing metaphysical: Platonic-solid completeness as a claim about reality is an enumeration read as a rendering |

### D3 — PROBABILITY-BEARING STATE / QUANTUM (11)

| Register | Theorem | Owner + year | What it forbids | The fence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D3 | Kochen–Specker | Kochen & Specker 1967 (indep. Bell 1966) | Non-contextual value-definiteness: quantum observables cannot all carry pre-existing measurement-independent values | **dim ≥ 3 strictly** — the qubit admits non-contextual assignments. Rules out NON-CONTEXTUAL deterministic hidden variables only; Bohmian mechanics is contextual and fully consistent with KS. A statement about the whole projector algebra, not any experiment; inequality versions get tested, and the strict colouring faces the finite-precision objection (Meyer 1999; Kent; Clifton–Kent) |
| D3 | Gleason's theorem (dispersion-free corollary) | Andrew Gleason 1957 | Any non-Born, non-contextual probability assignment over projectors — and in particular any deterministic state — in dim ≥ 3 | **dim ≥ 3 strictly** — dim 2 admits a continuum of non-Born frame functions. Assumes non-contextuality of the measure plus countable additivity over orthogonal projectors. It derives the FORM of the Born rule, not its interpretation, and says nothing about which ρ obtains. Routinely overclaimed as "deriving quantum mechanics" |
| D3 | Tsirelson bound | Boris Cirel'son 1980 | Quantum CHSH correlations above 2√2 — quantum theory is nonlocal but not maximally so | Scope is the CHSH scenario alone (2 parties, 2 settings, 2 outcomes) — other Bell scenarios have different maxima, so there is no single "Tsirelson bound". Bounds QUANTUM, not no-signalling: PR boxes reach 4 while no-signalling, so relativity alone does not yield 2√2. Post-MIP*=RE, "the quantum set" must be disambiguated — tensor-product and commuting-operator models provably differ |
| D3 | Robertson uncertainty relation (Schrödinger strengthening) | Kennard/Weyl 1927; Robertson 1929; Schrödinger 1930 | Joint sharp ENSEMBLE statistics for non-commuting observables in one prepared state | ⚠️ **AN ENSEMBLE/PREPARATION STATEMENT, NOT MEASUREMENT DISTURBANCE.** The naive ε(A)·η(B) ≥ ħ/2 is not a theorem: Ozawa 2003 corrected it and it was experimentally violated (neutron-spin 2012; photon 2013). Busch–Lahti–Werner 2013 recover a Heisenberg form only under calibration/worst-case definitions; the Ozawa–BLW dispute is unresolved. The bound is state-dependent and collapses to 0 when ⟨[A,B]⟩ = 0 — the state-independent statement is entropic (Deutsch 1983; Maassen–Uffink 1988) |
| D3 | No-cloning | **James L. Park 1970**; Wootters–Zurek, Dieks 1982 | Exact deterministic duplication of an unknown quantum state | Orthogonal states CAN be cloned — "unknown" must mean drawn from a non-orthogonal set. Approximate cloning is permitted with known optimal fidelity (Bužek–Hillery 5/6); probabilistic exact cloning of linearly independent states is permitted (Duan–Guo). Only exact AND deterministic is forbidden. **Priority: the proof is Park 1970, twelve years before the papers whose names it carries** |
| D3 | No-broadcasting | Barnum, Caves, Fuchs, Jozsa, Schumacher 1996 | Duplication of non-commuting mixed states even in the weakened marginal sense | It is an **IFF**: commuting (effectively classical) sets broadcast fine — which is why classical information copies freely. Scoped to 1→2: superbroadcasting (D'Ariano–Macchiavello–Perinotti 2005) takes N ≥ 4 to M > N copies with marginals MORE pure than the input. The correlational statement is the separate no-local-broadcasting theorem (Piani–Horodecki² 2008) |
| D3 | No-deleting | Pati & Braunstein 2000 | Linear destruction of redundantly held quantum information | Time-reverse dual of no-cloning and inherits its exemptions: orthogonal states deletable, KNOWN states trivially so. Forbids deletion-against-a-copy, not ordinary discarding or resetting. **A LINEARITY result, NOT a thermodynamic one** — it is not Landauer and bounds no heat or work |
| D3 | Holevo bound | Alexander Holevo 1973 | Extracting more than n classical bits of accessible information from n transmitted qubits | Bounds ACCESSIBLE information out of a transmitted system, not what the system "contains"; it is an upper bound only — achievability is the separate HSW theorem (asymptotic, product-state coding). Superdense coding does not violate it: two qubits are involved in total. **Not the thermodynamic extraction bound** — that is Landauer/Szilard, a different theorem |
| D3 | Monogamy of entanglement (CKW) | Coffman, Kundu, Wootters 2000; Osborne–Verstraete 2006 | Unlimited sharing of maximal entanglement across parties | Monogamy is a property of PARTICULAR measures: entanglement of formation is NOT monogamous in general; squashed entanglement is (Koashi–Winter). CKW is proved for **QUBITS** — the tangle inequality fails in higher dimensions. Classical correlations are freely shareable. "Entanglement is monogamous" without naming measure and dimension is an overclaim |
| D3 | Bound entanglement / non-distillability of PPT states | Horodecki³ 1998 | LOCC recovery of pure entanglement from PPT entangled states — entanglement is not a single fungible currency at the single-copy level | PPT ⇒ undistillable is proved; **the CONVERSE (are all NPT states distillable?) is a famous OPEN problem** — "bound entanglement = PPT entanglement" is an overclaim. Bound entangled states are not useless: they activate, aid metrology, and sometimes yield secure key. Scope is bipartite LOCC distillation to maximally entangled pairs |
| D3 | PBR theorem | Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph 2012 | ψ-epistemic ontological models that satisfy Preparation Independence | **Preparation Independence is the entire load-bearing assumption** and is contested — relax it to allow classical correlations, or to mere no-superluminal-influence, and ψ-epistemic models survive. Independently, Lewis–Jennings–Barrett–Rudolph 2012 built ψ-epistemic models in dim ≥ 3 reproducing quantum statistics, so "ψ-epistemic is impossible" is FALSE without PBR's extra premise. Presupposes the λ-mediated realist framework that QBism and relational readings reject; experiments face a detection loophole |

### D4 — CAUSAL ACTUALITY / THERMODYNAMICS / SPACETIME (22)

| Register | Theorem | Owner + year | What it forbids | The fence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D4 | Entropy Principle (axiomatic Second Law) | Lieb & Yngvason 1999 | Any adiabatic process from equilibrium to equilibrium of strictly lower entropy — perpetual motion of the second kind | Equilibrium-to-equilibrium ADIABATIC transitions only. Says nothing about non-equilibrium states, rates, fluctuations, or "disorder". The Comparison Hypothesis is not free — it must be earned from further axioms. The claim is biconditional (S(Y) ≤ S(X) iff accessible), not "entropy always increases" |
| D4 | Carnot bound | Sadi Carnot 1824 (rigorous within Lieb–Yngvason/Carathéodory) | Cyclic heat-to-work conversion exceeding 1 − Tc/Th, for any working substance | Two fixed-temperature reservoirs and a CYCLIC engine. Saturation requires quasi-static operation, i.e. **zero power**. Multi-reservoir, non-cyclic, information-fed, or finite-time engines are governed by different bounds |
| D4 | Universal power–efficiency trade-off | Shiraishi, Saito, Tasaki 2016 | Carnot efficiency at nonvanishing power — closes the gap Carnot leaves open | Scope is CLASSICAL Markov jump dynamics with local detailed balance and **finite dynamical activity**. Every known "Carnot at finite power" construction exploits diverging activity or relaxation time. Quantum-coherent and broken-time-reversal engines are outside the proved class |
| D4 | Third Law as unattainability | Nernst 1912; general derivation Masanes & Oppenheim 2017 | Reaching T = 0 in finite time, finite steps, or with finite resources | Rests on the bath having **POSITIVE HEAT CAPACITY** — the authors show finite-time perfect cooling becomes possible if that fails. Unattainability is logically **INDEPENDENT** of the Nernst heat theorem; neither implies the other, and conflating them is the standard third-law error |
| D4 | Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relation | Barato & Seifert 2015; proof Gingrich, Horowitz, Perunov, England 2016 | Precision on the cheap: relative precision of any steady-state current costs proportional minimum dissipation | Proved only for CLASSICAL Markov jump / overdamped Langevin dynamics with time-independent driving in a steady state. **Demonstrably VIOLATED** by quantum-coherent transport, underdamped dynamics, time-dependent driving, and discrete-time dynamics. Not a universal precision-dissipation law |
| D4 | Penrose singularity theorem | Roger Penrose 1965 | Null geodesic completeness once a closed trapped surface forms | Proves GEODESIC INCOMPLETENESS — not a curvature singularity, not infinite density, not "physics breaks down", and emphatically not "a beginning". The **NON-COMPACT Cauchy surface** hypothesis does real work and fails in spatially closed cosmologies. NEC is pointwise and quantum fields violate it. A theorem about the classical Einstein equation |
| D4 | Hawking singularity theorem (cosmological) | Stephen Hawking 1966–67 | Extending an everywhere-expanding, SEC-obeying cosmology arbitrarily far into the past | Requires the **STRONG energy condition, violated by inflation and by Λ > 0** — so it does not apply to our observed cosmology. Citing it as proof of a cosmic beginning ignores that its central hypothesis is believed false of our universe. This gap is exactly what BGV was written to fill |
| D4 | Borde–Guth–Vilenkin past-incompleteness | Borde, Guth, Vilenkin 2003 | A past-eternal geodesically complete history for any on-average-expanding model, including eternal inflation | Purely KINEMATIC — no energy condition, no field equations — and therefore yields **ONLY geodesic incompleteness**: not a singularity, not a first moment, not a creation event. The implied boundary may be perfectly non-singular. H_av is defined relative to a comoving congruence, so the hypothesis constrains that congruence, not "the universe" simpliciter |
| D4 | Hawking area theorem | Stephen Hawking 1971 | Any classical process decreasing total event-horizon area | Both hypotheses are load-bearing and contested. NEC is POINTWISE and quantum fields violate it — which is why Hawking radiation shrinks horizons and the classical theorem is false semiclassically. **Weak cosmic censorship is an open CONJECTURE** with counterexamples in higher dimensions and AdS, so the theorem is conditional on an unproved premise. The event horizon is teleologically defined, so this is not a local statement |
| D4 | Generalized Second Law (proved case) | Aron C. ## 3. THE THREE STRONGEST NEW POINTS

**1. Lindström's Theorem (Per Lindström, 1969) — D1, meta-register.**
Every other entry in D1 names a *hole*. Lindström names the *price list*. Any abstract logic satisfying minimal regularity that extends first-order logic and possesses both countable compactness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem has exactly first-order expressive power — and the second theorem gets the same conclusion from downward LS plus r.e. validity. This converts Gödel and Löwenheim–Skolem from a pair of unfortunate accidents into a **necessary trade fixed in advance**: you may buy the power to pin down a structure, or you may buy compactness and tractability, and the exchange rate is not negotiable. For a program that outlines its object by negative space, this is the difference between a list of things that happen not to be there and a **proof that the negative space has a shape**. It also supplies the outline's only honest meta-level fence: it constrains what a *fence* can be, so it disciplines the program's own instrument-building. Fence to carry: "strongest" means maximal expressive power, not proof-theoretic strength, and it carries no normative claim that first-order logic is the right logic.

**2. Hilbert 1901 / Efimov 1964, fenced by Nash–Kuiper 1954/55 — D2.**
The complete hyperbolic plane exists perfectly well as an intrinsic Riemannian object; it has no complete C² isometric immersion in ℝ³; and by Nash–Kuiper it has a C¹ one. **The forbidden object is unrepresentable in exactly one ambient register at exactly one smoothness, and nowhere else.** This is the program's own method — outline the object by where it cannot be drawn — occurring as a proved theorem rather than as a metaphor, and it hands the program a specific discipline it currently lacks: *an impossibility claim that does not name its ambient register and its regularity class is not merely unfenced, it is false.* It also carries a live warning about the program's habit of letting conjectures do fence work — the corrected fence is flexibility for α < 1/5, rigidity for α > 2/3, **the gap open, and C^{1,1/2} is Gromov's conjecture, not a located bound**. The theorem is strongest precisely where the corpus is weakest.

**3. Sonnenschein–Mantel–Debreu (1972–74), stated negatively — D5.**
Individual rationality imposes **no** restriction on aggregate excess demand beyond Walras' law and homogeneity; therefore uniqueness and stability of equilibrium are not derivable from rational microfoundations, and **no representative-agent reduction of a collective is licensed**. This is the sharpest theorem-grade statement available of *the collective is not a magnified individual*, and it is directly load-bearing against the corpus's largest standing affirmative structure — reading an organism, holobiont, or DAV as an agent with coherent preferences. It sharpens the outline because it forbids a whole *class of moves* rather than a single object, and because it pairs with the D5 aggregation cluster (Arrow, Nakamura, List–Pettit, Holmström) to make one coherent prohibition: a collective may have a rule, and may have members with preferences, but it does not thereby have a mind, a preference ordering, or a demand function. Fence to carry: exchange economies, l ≥ 2 goods, compact subset bounded away from the boundary, consumers ≥ goods, and restrictions **do** reappear under assumptions on the *distribution* of preferences and endowments (Hildenbrand, Grandmont).

*(Runners-up, recorded but not counted: Tennenbaum's theorem — the sharpest fence against the loose Löwenheim–Skolem reading, since the deviant models exist but are computably unreachable; and Touchette–Lloyd, the units-matched replacement for Ashby.)*

## 4. DENSEST AND SPARSEST — where the next hunt digs

**D5 is by a wide margin the densest**: 30 pure entries plus 8 crossings, drawn from four literatures — social choice, mechanism design, distributed systems, information theory — that have never before been read as one register, and whose fences are the best-developed in the whole outline because economists and distributed-systems theorists have been fighting their own overclaimers for seventy years. It is also the register where the corpus has the most exposure, and the density is therefore protective rather than decorative. **D2 is the sparsest and the most dangerous sparseness**: 14 pure entries after routing Abel–Ruffini and Lindemann out to D1, thinner than D1 (21) or D4 (22), and thinnest exactly where the program's own load-bearing objects live — sphere primacy, the stereographic chart, ℂP¹, the equator/L4 construction. A sparse D2 means the geometric claims are the least fenced claims in the corpus. **Dig there next, and dig at the place D2's own lane already flagged and then deferred: smooth-structure obstructions in dimension four — Donaldson's 1983 diagonalizability theorem and its consequences (non-smoothable topological 4-manifolds), read against Freedman's classification, with Milnor's exotic 7-spheres as the affirmative sibling whose forbidding content is the failure of smooth uniqueness.** That region is genuinely forbidding, genuinely theorem-grade, and dimension-4-specific in a way the program should want to know about before it builds further on a sphere. Second dig: the h-principle boundary — the general question of *where flexibility stops* — of which the corrected C^{1,1/5}–C^{1,2/3} gap is one instance and probably not the most important one. A separate structural finding worth recording rather than repairing: **D3 has only 11 pure entries because the quantum register is almost entirely made of crossings (14 of its 25 distinct theorems).** That is not a gap. It is the register's actual shape — quantum theory forbids very little on its own and forbids a great deal in conjunction with causal structure (12 crossings into D4) and with strategy (6 into D5) — and any account of D3 that reports it as thin has mistaken a topology for a shortage.

## 5. THE CANDIDATE L_R ASSEMBLY FOR THE VERIFICATION REGISTER

**Verdict: the proposed assembly (Ashby + Conant-Ashby + Tarski + Rice + Beer + Landauer) is NOT a coherent instrument as specified, and two of its six legs must be removed before it can be one.** Beer's VSM is chaff outright — an affirmative organizational framework with no forbidding theorem attached, and including it lends the assembly a false sixth leg. Conant–Ashby survives only in its narrow form, which cannot bear the weight the assembly wants: "model" there means a deterministic homomorphic image that may discard almost all information, the regulator is *handed* the system state so there is no sensing and no inference, and Baez's criticism — that the formal proof does not support the paper's title — is recorded in the standard reference; the theorem structurally cannot say a verifier must *build* a model of what it verifies. Ashby is theorem-grade but conditional on column-distinctness, a hypothesis that verification systems violate constantly and by design, since buffering, defaults, and canonicalization are exactly constant columns — for a gate that normalizes its input before checking it, requisite variety is *vacuous*. Landauer is wheat but mis-fitted: it prices **erasure**, bounding the entropy decrease of the system, and using it as the thermodynamic floor of a *check* is a category slide unless the check is separately shown to erase. What survives is a genuine and tighter three-legged instrument: **(i) Tarski** — the evaluator must sit in a strictly stronger metalanguage, so closed self-certification is impossible, while *stratified, partial* internal audit is entirely lawful (Σₙ-truth is Σₙ-definable, and the "a system can know nothing about its own truth" reading is an overclaim the assembly must not import); **(ii) Rice** — no total decision procedure for any non-trivial semantic property, so syntactic gates are cheap and property gates are not, and the only lawful repair is a sound-but-incomplete checker with an explicit UNKNOWN verdict; **(iii) Touchette–Lloyd, with the DPI equality condition as its instrument** — a gate's discriminating power is bounded by the information it actually acquires, at most one bit of order per bit measured, and a gate that coarse-grains is informationally free **exactly** when its checked variable is a sufficient statistic for the property, strictly lossy otherwise, with that loss being precisely the false-pass rate. Landauer re-enters only where a verification step is shown to erase; Ashby re-enters only for gates whose payoff table has no constant column. **The single prediction that tests it:** for every gate currently deployed in the estate that (a) reads only syntactic features of an artifact and (b) issues a binary PASS/FAIL with no UNKNOWN verdict, an artifact exists that passes the gate and violates the property the gate is claimed to certify — **and it can be constructed in bounded time by inspecting the gate's own source alone.** Pre-register the target list, then attempt one adversarial artifact per gate. The instrument predicts success on every such gate.
