# Owner direction — horizon balance

In the continuing v2.1 implementation sitting, Yves R. Burri added the
following intended meaning to the reciprocal spectrum:

- approaching `V` means a shorter time horizon and greater emphasis on present
  power, at the expense of future potential;
- approaching `Φ` means a longer time horizon and greater emphasis on future
  potential, at the expense of present power;
- the centre means optimal balance, least opportunity loss, flow, and Dharma.

## Typed adoption

The direction is adopted as `emergentism/HorizonBalanceOverlay.v1` with four
separate claim types:

1. `[A]` normalized reciprocal-chart weights have an equal-weight centre that
   uniquely maximizes the declared balance function.
2. `[I]` the ν-dominant side is read as Vward/short-horizon/present-facing and
   the φ-dominant side as Φward/long-horizon/future-option-facing.
3. `[I/C]` real opportunity regret is bearer-wise and depends on a declared
   feasible set, horizon, calibration, and loss or regret rule.
4. `[C]` the centre may support flow and Dharma only in domains where present
   enactment and future-option retention are complementary under the declared
   premises. The transfer is untested.

The lowercase coordinates remain distinct from uppercase powers. The
Burrisphere display path remains non-temporal. Possible content does not act
without a present D4 carrier.

## Language not adopted as theorem

The amendment does not adopt any universal claim that the centre removes real
tradeoffs, minimizes every bearer's opportunity cost, makes short and long
clocks equal, or derives flow, Dharma, Justice, or the Good from geometry.
Specialized or asymmetric domains may lawfully optimize away from the centre.

## Test boundary

A scored test must preregister the decision domain, short and long horizons,
native feasible actions, calibration, complement/substitute relation, prices
and storage, affected bearers, Justice, consent, Exit, held-out target, null,
and serious rivals. Centre-optimality dies in a domain when a tilted,
timescale-matched, or native rival robustly wins; Dharma integrity dies when a
bearer is hidden, harmed, or unable to exit; the flow conjecture dies when an
independently operationalized flow measure adds no discriminating signal.
