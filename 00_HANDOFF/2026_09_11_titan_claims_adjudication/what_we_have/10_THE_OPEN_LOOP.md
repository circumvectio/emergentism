# The open loop

`•  ⊙  ○`

## 0 · The problem this answers

`09_THE_PICTURE.md` §3 established that `D5` is representational, and therefore
that models drift. That leaves a specific and nasty problem:

**Your option cone always contains phantoms you cannot detect from inside it.**
`E4` is explicitly model-relative — *"Within a **specified** possibility space"* —
so consistency is only ever consistency relative to what you have specified. A
state that looks admissible may be unreachable, or incoherent at a depth your
specification does not reach.

**Checking coherence harder does not find them.** It finds the phantoms your
specification already reaches, and by construction it cannot find the others.
Phantoms are found by **contact**, not by inspection.

---

## 1 · The Soul Loop is the contact mechanism

`10_THE_SOUL_LOOP.md`:

```text
territory → model → alternatives → selection → commitment receipt q_t
          → outcome receipt r_(t+1) → update
```

```text
(M_(t+1), G_(t+1)) = Loop(M_t, G_t, q_t, r_(t+1))
```

The loop may update the model of the territory, the weights and habits used to
select, and the declared uncertainty and tier. **`r` is the only term that
carries the world.** Everything else in the loop is the modeller talking to
itself.

And the document separates the three places it can fail:

| Gap | Comparison | Primary record |
|---|---|---|
| cognitive | territory versus model | prediction/model audit |
| execution | intended selection versus performed attempt | `q_t` |
| outcome | expected versus observed consequence | `r_(t+1)` |

**The phantom problem lives in the third row, and only `r` reaches it.**

## 2 · A false egregore is an open Soul Loop at collective scale `[I]` — new

Take a sacrificial cult whose theology promises rain. The model draws a `D5`
worldline in which the rite produces the weather. The rite is performed — `q`.
The weather arrives, or does not — `r` exists in the world.

**And the model never updates.** Rain vindicates the rite; drought demands more
of it. Every possible `r` is absorbed as confirmation.

**A loop that cannot take a disconfirming receipt is not a loop. It is a
ratchet.** And the Soul Loop's own retirement criterion names exactly this,
without needing the example:

> Retire the loop for a scope if it cannot distinguish its own commitment from
> the world's consequence **or cannot update under adverse receipts.**

The Lived Compass names the same object one register up: *"A reinforcing loop may
amplify accuracy or delusion."* It cannot tell which, **because `r` is the only
term that discriminates.**

## 3 · What the case corrects about concealment `[S]`

`08_THE_STANDPOINT_BRIDGE.md` §8b argued that evil is structurally required to
hide. A mass public rite looks like a counterexample: nothing about it is hidden.

**It is not a counterexample. It is a correction about *what* hides.**

`J` requires *"named actors, payers, beneficiaries, and consequence bearers."*
The rite names them **falsely** — it says the beneficiary is the collective when
the beneficiary is the priesthood. The deed is in the open; **the ledger is not.**

| `J` clause | status under the rite |
|---|---|
| complete and valid `AuthorizationEnvelope` | absent |
| named actors, payers, beneficiaries, consequence bearers | **misnamed — this is the concealment** |
| consent, contest, revocation, reversibility, exit | absent |
| no hidden extraction or compensating aggregate erasing a harmed side | the aggregate *is* the erasure |
| physically admissible means and **honest uncertainty** | fails at the root — the causal claim is false |

**So: it is not the act that must hide. It is the accounting.** An act performed
in front of a city can be perfectly concealed in the only sense that matters —
and that concealment is *cheaper* than secrecy, because it needs no secret and it
recruits the payers into defending it.

## 4 · The deeper reason extraction needs a false model `[I]` — new

§8b gave one reason for concealment: a named payer is a priced transfer, so the
collective would stop it. That reason is real and it is not the deepest one.

**A false ledger disables the return leg.** You cannot compute `r_(t+1)` —
expected versus observed consequence — if the account of who paid and what
followed is false. The outcome gap cannot be measured against a fabricated
expectation.

So concealment does not merely evade the collective's judgement. **It disables
the error-correction that would expose the phantom.**

Which is why extraction and delusion travel together, and why neither is
sufficient to explain the other:

- **The phantom makes the extraction acceptable** — the payers consent to a
  transfer they believe benefits them.
- **The extraction makes the phantom durable** — the beneficiaries are selected
  for maintaining the belief, and a belief that pays its holders is not
  abandoned when `r` disappoints.

**Each protects the other.** That is the mechanism, and it needs no liar. This is
the Syntropic Imperative's territory: an architecture that lays down *"traces of
outrage, dependency, opacity, and capture"* rather than *"evidence, provenance,
reversibility, sovereignty, and direct practice."*

## 5 · Does evil require knowing? `[D]` — split, and the split is honest

If the priests **sincerely believed** the causal claim, the mechanism clause in
`Evil(a)` may not fire: the gain is not obtained *through* the loss **as its
purpose**; it is obtained through a false model that happens to pay.

Two things can be said cleanly, and a third cannot:

1. **`¬J` holds regardless of sincerity.** No consent, no exit, no contest, no
   revocability, no honest uncertainty. The act is impermissible whether or not
   anyone believed. Sincerity is not a Justice clause.
2. **Under sincere belief the structure is the wall at civilizational scale** —
   `08` §8b's driver who models concrete as paper, except the wall is other
   people. That is catastrophic false modelling, and it is a different category
   from evil.
3. **What cannot be said cleanly is which one obtained**, because belief is not
   observable at this distance. `[D]`.

**But sincerity does not rescue the system, and here is why.** A belief that
benefits its holders is **selected for**. The priesthood need not lie; the
arrangement need only persist, and it persists precisely because it pays the
party best placed to maintain it. **The egregore does the concealing.** That is
how a structure can be extractive with no extractor who intends it — and it is
why the corpus fences the informational architecture rather than only the
actors.

## 6 · Historical caution `[B]`

Use the sacrificial-cult case as **illustration, not evidence.** The scholarship
is genuinely contested: Spanish colonial sources inflated scale for their own
purposes; the priesthood bore substantial costs including ritual self-bloodletting;
and most victims were war captives, which is a different political economy from a
priesthood preying on its own population. **No historical claim is made or needed
here.** The structure is the point, and the structure is available from the
