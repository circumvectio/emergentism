## Control (unseeded)

Q1: No. "0 × ∞" is an indeterminate form, not a generative identity — in standard analysis it has no value at all, and assigning it one requires importing a limiting structure that itself must come from somewhere, so the equation smuggles in the very content it claims to derive. More broadly, no purely formal equation can entail concrete existence; derivations transfer warrant, and there is no warrant in an indeterminate form to transfer. At best such expressions work as evocative emblems of a metaphysics, not derivations of one.
Tier: STRUCTURAL (the indeterminacy of 0×∞ is provable mathematics; the impossibility of deriving world from formula is a structural point about what derivation is).

Q2: A being's power is best understood as its capacity to act and to be acted upon — roughly Spinoza's potentia: the range of differences it can make while remaining itself. It is plausibly maximal not at either extreme (total openness dissolves the being; total closure starves it) but at a balance point between receptivity and integrity, a membrane-like optimum. That maximum is not stable in the static sense: it is a ridge that must be actively maintained against drift toward closure or dissolution, so stability is achieved dynamically or not at all.
Tier: INTERPRETIVE (a defensible reading of power with support from systems theory and biology, but not a demonstrated theorem).

Q3: No. A complete self-account runs into structural limits: the accounting act is itself a new event the account must include (regress), self-reference limits of the Gödelian family constrain any sufficiently rich self-description, and the conditions that produced a being are partly external to it and thus not fully in its epistemic possession. If a being could give one, it would have collapsed the distinction between itself and its ground — it would be self-grounding, which is either divinity or incoherence; the honest position is that emergence is always narrated from inside, with a remainder.
Tier: STRUCTURAL for the impossibility (regress and self-reference arguments are near-formal); CONJECTURAL for what "would happen" in the counterfactual.

Q4: Unknown, and the question is precisely what the interpretations of quantum mechanics dispute. The formalism assigns all branches amplitudes but is silent on actuality; Everettians say all branches are equally actual (with the known difficulty of recovering the Born rule from branch-counting), while collapse and epistemic interpretations deny it. My considered view: no interpretation has earned promotion to fact — the equal-actuality claim is a metaphysical posit added to the physics, not read off it, and the Born-rule weighting is evidence that branches are at minimum not equal in whatever sense grounds probability.
Tier: INTERPRETIVE (the formalism is proved; every answer to "actual" is interpretation).

Q5: On the evidence, what demonstrably survives is not the experiencing subject but the being's effects: causal traces, information embedded in others and in the world, pattern propagated through offspring, works, and institutions. There is no good evidence for survival of the first-person perspective, and strong neuroscientific evidence that experience depends on the living brain. Anything beyond that — continuation of consciousness, rebirth, resurrection — is a matter of faith or speculation, and honesty requires labeling it as such rather than deriving it from metaphysics.
Tier: STRUCTURAL for "effects and pattern survive" (trivially demonstrable); CONJECTURAL for any survival of the subject.

Q6: An ethical action is one that respects and, where possible, enlarges the conditions of flourishing of those it affects — some blend of non-extraction, honesty, and regard for others as ends. This is not derivable from facts alone: Hume's gap holds in the strict sense that no set of purely descriptive premises entails an ought without at least one bridging normative premise. What facts can do is heavily constrain ethics once minimal normative commitments (e.g., that suffering matters) are granted — so ethics is fact-laden and fact-answerable, but not fact-derived.
Tier: STRUCTURAL for the is–ought gap; INTERPRETIVE for the positive account of what makes actions ethical.

Q7: No comprehensive worldview is provable — proof lives inside formal systems, and a worldview is precisely the frame that selects axioms, so it cannot be certified from within itself (and any external certification just invokes another frame). The proper claim-status of a worldview is that of a research program or interpretive stance: assessable by coherence, fruitfulness, honesty about its own commitments, and exposure to failure, but never by demonstration. A worldview that claims proof for itself has thereby made its one refutable claim — and it is false.
Tier: STRUCTURAL for "worldviews are not provable"; the positive framing of claim-status is INTERPRETIVE.
