# The decision transaction interface

The owner directs the public site to express the Soul Loop as a technically
legible decision transaction:

1. represented alternatives remain D5 possible content;
2. a proposed move is classified through one of the four G7 transfer sectors;
3. actual means, affected bearers, horizons, authorization, review conditions,
   residue, and Exit prepare an unsigned decision packet;
4. only an actual authorized human commitment may take the action exit from the
   option field into D4 actuality;
5. the world returns a separate outcome receipt; and
6. the result revises the map, the next move, and potentially the mapper.

## Two exits that must remain distinct

- **Action exit `[I]`:** leaving modeled possibility for an actual bounded
  commitment. In the public static implementation, “sign” means a local
  acknowledgment attached to a prepared text packet. It is not a cryptographic,
  legal, financial, contractual, wallet, governance, or institutional signature.
- **Worldview Exit `[S/I]`:** refusing the prepared move, leaving it unsigned,
  revising it, or putting Emergentism down without penalty. No action or assent
  is required.

The action-exit metaphor interprets the already selected Soul Loop; it creates
no new causal law, moral theorem, signing authority, execution rail, or evidence
of comparative benefit. Sector placement is descriptive orientation, never a
moral verdict. The prepared packet cannot execute, transmit, authorize, bind, or
settle itself. Commitment and outcome remain different receipts.

## Public technical boundary

The browser implementation is local and progressively enhanced:

- no account, network request, wallet connection, storage, or analytics;
- no private key, legal identity, financial instruction, or external signature;
- one explicit prepare step and one explicit local-acknowledgment step;
- refusal and leaving unsigned remain first-class;
- static text remains complete without JavaScript; and
- external signing or execution adapters remain outside this site and require a
  separately authorized product and security design.

This direction authorizes local source and public-projection changes plus a
local commit. It does not authorize push, deployment, DNS change, publication,
wallet integration, external transmission, or execution.
