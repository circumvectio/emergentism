# Two-sided rescoring — the five developmental registers

The discovery pass proposed the cheapest external receipt: rescore the five
developmental ladders with a two-sided rubric. This is the attempt. Scope:
the five **positive registers** D1–D5 (D0 and D6 are boundary markers, not
positive rungs — the Seed front page's own scoping). The rubric is two-sided
so it can fail: if any register's internal claim exceeded what its external
contact licenses, the gap would be recorded as a refutation, not smoothed.

## 1 · The rubric

**Side A (internal):** what the register claims at its own tier; what it can
show; its kills; what is owned versus inherited.
**Side B (external):** for each load-bearing claim, contact with a source the
corpus did not write — actually consulted, named, and dated; or explicitly
marked UNCHECKED. A side-B contact that contradicts side A is recorded as a
refutation. A side-A claim with no side-B contact is recorded as UNCONTACTED,
which is a finding, not a fault.

## 3 · The shape of the receipt

No register claims beyond its external contact. After the day's panels and
repairs, the corpus does not overclaim against any consulted source — the
gap the rubric was built to catch did not fire. What the rescoring leaves
standing is exactly what the fair re-hearing left: the articulations, the
discipline, and one joint (the boundary typed and kept). **The receipt's
content is the absence of the gap, and it is falsifiable: any future claim
that exceeds its external contact refutes this receipt.**

## 4 · The honest limit — and why this is still not the 156th

This receipt's outside side is genuinely external: every side-B contact is a
source the corpus did not write, actually consulted, named and dated. **The
scoring act is not.** The scorer read the corpus; the rubric was composed
inside the loop; the absence-of-gap finding is therefore a closed-loop result
about external contacts, not an external verdict on the corpus. The first
fully external receipt still requires what no agent in the loop can supply:
a cold reader who never read the corpus, or a resolved market, or an oracle
that did not read the side-A claims before scoring. This receipt is the
cheapest available external contact and is filed as an attempt, not as the
156th. The blank line in the ledger stays blank for the world to write.

**Kill for this receipt:** a cold reader scores any register's side A
differently, or any load-bearing claim is found to exceed its side-B contact
— either refutes this receipt and names the overclaim.
