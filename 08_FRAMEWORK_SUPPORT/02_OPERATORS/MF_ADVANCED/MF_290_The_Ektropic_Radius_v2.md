# MF-290: THE CONSEQUENCE HORIZON

**Evidence tier:** `[I/C]` for the model; domain measurements retain their own

## 1. The surviving insight

Actions often look beneficial inside a narrow boundary and harmful after
distant, delayed, ecological, institutional, or third-party consequences are
included. A **consequence horizon** is therefore a useful audit variable:

\[
\mathcal H(a)=(\mathcal B(a),T,\mathcal I,\mathcal U),
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal B(a)\) is the declared set of materially affected bearers;
- `T` is the temporal horizon;
- \(\mathcal I\) records information and observation limits; and
- \(\mathcal U\) records uncertainty, missing bearers, and model error.

Expanding this horizon can reveal hidden costs. It does not guarantee that one
scalar total becomes positive, that every effect is measurable in one unit, or
that the widest imaginable boundary supplies the correct decision.

## 2. Type separation

The following quantities are not interchangeable:

- physical entropy and energy;
- information access;
- reachable or modelable options;
- money, labor, biological viability, and institutional power;
- an individual's durable potential;
- a sustaining whole's durable potential; and
- moral standing or Justice.

Any scalarization requires declared units, normalization, payer, beneficiary,
and loss function. Bits, joules, dollars, deaths, consent, and extinction risk
must not be silently added.

The physical light cone remains fixed by spacetime and `c`. Information access
and option cones can differ among agents inside it. See
`MF_295_c_Bounds_the_Moral_Circle.md`.

## 3. Justice precedes aggregation

Let \(\mathcal B(a)\) include every materially affected person, whole, payer,
beneficiary, and exposed third party. Bearer-complete Justice is checked
conjunctively. An aggregate such as \(\sum_b \Delta W_b\) may be reported as a
diagnostic only after every bearer is visible; it cannot compensate for an
unconsented destroyed bearer or make the act moral.

Strict Syntropic Dyadism requires the focal individual and declared whole both
to rise under Justice. Voluntary sacrifice is a distinct costly class. It may
be honorable, but a collective may not demand it or relabel the sacrifice as
strict mutual gain.

## 4. Is and ought remain distinct

A wider model can improve factual consequence assessment. The normative rule
that those bearers matter is still a declared Justice commitment. Neither
thermodynamics nor a radius derives that commitment. The repaired relationship
is:

\[
\text{better consequence model}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{moral theorem},
\]

but better evidence can expose when an action violates an already declared
norm.

## 5. Human reach hypothesis

Humans may differ from many organisms in symbolic, counterfactual, social,
institutional, and intergenerational modeling. This is a hypothesis about
option/information structure, not intrinsic worth, a literal horn altitude, or
a wider physical cone. Comparison requires operational measures and
domain-native rivals such as working memory, planning horizon, ecological
niche, social learning, and tool-supported control.

## 6. Research protocol

For a proposed action:

1. name the initial bearer set and why each bearer is included;
2. expose payer, beneficiary, consent, custody, reversibility, and exit;
3. declare time horizon and uncertainty;
4. retain native units as a vector unless scalarization is justified;
5. search deliberately for omitted and future bearers;
6. compare at least one narrower and one wider model;
7. record commitment separately from world outcome; and
8. update the bearer map when the receipt exposes a gap.

## 7. Kill criteria

The consequence-horizon model fails or narrows if:

- wider models do not improve held-out consequence prediction or omission
  detection;
- results depend arbitrarily on one chosen radius;
- scalarization hides native-unit reversals;
- information/option measures outperform the radius metaphor without loss; or
- the framework again treats a positive aggregate as sufficient for Justice.
