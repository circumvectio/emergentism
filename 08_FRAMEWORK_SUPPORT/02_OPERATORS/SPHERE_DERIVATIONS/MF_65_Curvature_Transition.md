## 1. Established geometry `[A]`

For a regular embedded ring torus with major radius `R>r>0`, using poloidal
angle `v`,

\[
K(v)=\frac{\cos v}{r(R+r\cos v)}.
\]

Its Gaussian curvature is positive on the outer region, zero at the top and
bottom circles, and negative on the inner region. Gauss–Bonnet gives

\[
\int_{T^2}K\,dA=2\pi\chi(T^2)=0.
\]

For a round sphere of radius `a`, `K=1/a²` and

\[
\int_{S^2}K\,dA=2\pi\chi(S^2)=4\pi.
\]

Along a geodesic, infinitesimal transverse separation is governed locally by
the Jacobi equation. Positive curvature can focus neighboring geodesics and
negative curvature can defocus them under the relevant initial conditions.
This local statement is not the claim that every arbitrary trajectory must
meet every other trajectory. Complete geodesics of a round sphere are great
circles; distinct great circles intersect, but the fact supplies no theorem
about agents, memories, societies, or causal contact.

## 2. Topology-change boundary `[A]`

`T²` and `S²` have different genus and Euler characteristic. No smooth family
of regular embedded surfaces changes one into the other without a singularity
or surgery. At `R=r` the horn torus has a singular contact point and the regular
torus curvature formula is not globally licensed there. For `r>R` the familiar
parameterization is self-intersecting rather than an embedded sphere.

Gauss–Bonnet compares the total curvature of two different regular surfaces.
It does not say that negative curvature disappears and "liberates" `4π`, nor
does it supply an energy release. Any physical phase-transition model needs its
own dynamics, conserved quantities, and evidence.
