## 3. Trophic cascade — probably fatal `[C]`

### 3.1 The kill criterion, stated up front

> **KILLED if the mapping requires levels to consume levels — because trophic transfer runs
> at roughly 10% efficiency, losing ~90% at each step, and η = 0 forbids extraction beyond
> regeneration.**

Trophic structure is not a ladder of merit. It is a **dependency-and-consumption gradient**
in which each level lives off the one beneath and wastes most of what it takes. A framework
whose first constraint is zero extraction cannot adopt an organising structure that is
definitionally extractive and lossy. **The contradiction is architectural, not behavioural.**

### 3.2 If run honestly, it inverts the hierarchy it is recruited for

Apex predators are **fewer, more fragile, more extinction-prone, and wholly dependent** on the
productive base. Primary producers carry the system. Remove an apex and the system
reorganises; remove the producers and it ends.

So a serious trophic reading renders any apex position a claim about **fragility and
dependence on those below** — closer to an extraction critique than to a legitimation.

### 3.3 Where it might survive

Trophic *cascades* — as distinct from trophic *levels* — describe how a change at one point
propagates through a whole web, often non-obviously and with reversals. That is a real
candidate for the **capture question**: what happens system-wide when one function is removed
or monopolised. It says nothing about rank.

**Discriminator:** does a trophic reading predict anything about the rows that a plain
dependency graph does not? If not, the plain graph wins on description length and the pack
adds interpretive freedom without adding content.

## 4. Convergent evolution — the mechanism cannot supply the target `[S]`

Convergent evolution is real and well evidenced: independent lineages arrive at similar
solutions under similar constraints — eyes, wings, streamlining.

**It is not teleological.** Nothing is aimed at. "Convergent evolution **to ideals**" imports
a target the mechanism specifically excludes; selection has no destination, only a local
gradient. This is the same class as the "seven-as-necessity" error the seven-operator audit
already cut, and as the `φν=1` → equal-dignity derivation reversed in the drift
reconciliation.

Convergence may be cited as evidence that **similar constraints produce similar solutions**.
It may not be cited as evidence that solutions ascend toward an ideal.

## 5. `rival_map` — the Great Chain of Being is a RIVAL, not a container

The Great Chain (*scala naturae*) is a genuine and historically important ordering: a fixed
ladder of ontological perfection. It belongs in the Rosetta — as a **`CULTURE7` entry with
`dependency_status`**, alongside the other traditions.

**But it cannot contain `ECO7`, because they are rival explanations of the same phenomena.**

| | Great Chain | Evolutionary biology |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | ladder | branching tree |
| Direction | ascends toward perfection | none; no privileged tip |
| Position | fixed by nature | contingent, historical, reversible |
| "Higher" | ontologically superior | **not a defined term** |

The tree **replaced** the ladder; that displacement is one of the central conceptual events in
the history of biology, and *scala naturae* reasoning is the specific error working biologists
are trained to avoid.

So **"we follow biological laws" and "in the Great Chain of Being" cannot both be load-bearing.**
Biology's authority is not available to an ordering biology displaced. Choosing both is not
synthesis — it is citing a rival as if it were a corroboration, which is the dependency error
in its most costly form.

**The Rosetta's job here is to keep the rival visible, not to merge it.** Both may be
translated. Neither may borrow the other's warrant.

## 6. Niche partitioning — the additive part `[I]`

Distinct from trophic transfer, and the distinction matters: **trophic is vertical and
consumptive; niche partitioning is horizontal and non-consumptive.** Two warblers dividing a
spruce do not consume each other. There is no transfer between partitioners at all, so the
no-consumption clause of the governing contract is satisfied by construction rather than by
fence.

### 6.1 Competitive exclusion is a mechanism for "never merge the functions"

Gause's principle: two species cannot stably coexist on identical resources — one excludes
the other.

So merging two organs does not yield a larger organ. It yields **exclusion**: one function
drives out the other, and the survivor's outputs quietly stand in for both. That converts the
corpus's most load-bearing rule from doctrine into a prediction about *what merger actually
does*.

### 6.2 The correspondence is diffusion, not convergence — `dependency_status: direct diffusion`

Economics did not independently discover niche partitioning. The lineage is a documented loop:

> Malthus (political economy) → Darwin reads Malthus → struggle for existence formalised as
> natural selection → ecology develops the niche concept → economics borrows *niche* back as
> product differentiation and market segmentation.

"Ecology and economics agree" is therefore **one idea circulating through two disciplines**,
not two independent observations. It is worth exactly one observation and must be counted as
one. This is effect #3 with a traceable path in both directions — the cleanest instance
available to the corpus.

### 6.3 Cardinality discriminates between packs

Niche space has **no canonical dimensionality** — Hutchinson's formulation is an
n-dimensional hypervolume where n is whatever the system has. There is no seven.

> **Niche partitioning supports `SOUL4` and is silent on `GEN7`.**

Evidence for the four-organ functional split; nothing for the sevenfold rows. Normalising
niche axes to seven would be the Procrustean move the anti-normalisation rule forbids.

This is the first result in the ledger that discriminates *between* packs rather than
decorating both.
