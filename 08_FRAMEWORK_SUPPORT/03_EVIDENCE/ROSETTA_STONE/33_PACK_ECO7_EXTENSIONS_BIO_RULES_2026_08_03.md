## 0. The discriminator (one question per rule)

The parent pack established the test: *does a bio-rule predict anything about
the holobiont that a plain typed dependency graph does not?* If not, the plain
graph wins on description length and the rule adds interpretive freedom without
content. This extension adds a second test, specific to the "rules" family:

> **Pattern or mechanism?** The named bio-rules split cleanly into (a)
> **directional/statistical tendencies** (Bergmann, Allen, Foster, Cope, Dollo,
> Rapoport, Emery, taxon-cycle) and (b) **mechanistic causal claims** (Gause,
> Hamilton, R\*, character displacement, MacArthur–Wilson, hologenome).
> *Mechanistic* rules can earn a Rosetta entry if the mechanism names a typed
> dependency the organism actually has. *Directional* rules almost never do —
> they are post-hoc summaries of a trend, and importing them risks the
> "convergence to ideals" teleology error the parent pack already cut.

Apply both tests to each entry below.

## 1. Emery's rule — the additive candidate `[I]`

**Statement.** Social parasites (especially of ants, wasps, bees) are typically
close relatives — often sister taxa — of their hosts; in the strict form the
parasite is the sister species of its host. (Emery 1909; strict/loose refinement
Smith et al. 2007.)

**Mechanism.** Sympatric speciation from inside the host's own gene pool: a
reproductively-isolated mutant morph exploits the colony's recognition systems.
The parasite is a *degraded version of the host* because it arose from the
host's lineage.

**Empirical status.** Contested, taxon-dependent. Supported in several ant
clades (*Acromyrmex*; some *Polistes*; Rabeling 2014, Jansen & Savolainen 2010);
**rejected** in Vespinae, where molecular phylogenies contradict sister-species
origins (Lopez-Osorio & Pickett 2015).

**Type.** Directional/pattern (a phylogenetic tendency), with a mechanistic
gloss (sympatric speciation).

**Does it add prediction over a dependency graph?** *Yes — and this is the one
rule worth retaining.* Emery's rule predicts that **the most dangerous parasite
of a cooperative stack is one that arose inside it and shares its recognition
vocabulary.** Translated under `[I]`: the gravest extraction threat to a
holobiont-style stack is a *defector that looks like a cooperator because it
was built from the same substrate* — a rogue L4 executing what it was only
authorized to stage (the Sva-karma failure); a Pūtanā-form L2 offering
plausible-looking candidates; an L1 mistagging a cooperator because it shares
the firewall's own intake grammar. The parasite rides the host's own
recognition system. **This is a real prediction about WHERE extraction
threats cluster**, and it is not trivially reducible to a plain graph.

**Kill criterion.** KILLED if Emery's rule is used to claim the Magnum Opus
organism *has* social parasites as a biological fact. Survives only as `[I]`
image: "watch for defection that wears the stack's own livery."

**Verdict.** Retain `[I]`. The parent pack's Gause/competitive-exclusion entry
covers *between-function* overlap; Emery covers a distinct failure mode —
*inside-the-stack mimicry* — that the existing fences (Āma, Āmiṣa, Sva-karma)
already name but that the bio-image makes vivid.

## 2. Hamilton's rule (rB > C) — the kin-selection mechanism `[I]`

**Statement.** A costly/altruistic behaviour is favoured when `rB > C`
(relatedness × benefit to recipient > cost to actor). (Hamilton 1964; "kin
selection" coined by Maynard Smith 1964.)

**Mechanism.** Inclusive fitness: selection favours genes that boost their own
replication *through relatives*, not only through the actor's own descendants.

**Empirical status.** Central theorem of social-evolution theory; well-supported
comparatively and experimentally (Bourke 2014), with active debate on generality
and on whether strict `rb > c` holds under weak selection (Nowak/Tarnita/Wilson
2010 controversy; defended Abbot et al. 2011).

**Type.** Mechanistic — a quantitative selection condition.

**Does it add prediction?** *Yes, narrowly.* It supplies a mechanism for *why a
parallel cooperative stack holds together at all*: cooperation is favoured when
the "relatedness" (shared substrate/doctrine/fence-set) times the benefit exceeds
the cost. Translated under `[I]`: the holobiont image's "same replicator, no
extraction" claim gets a *selection-theoretic gloss* — a stack cooperates
internally because its parts share the substrate that makes defection
self-defeating (the 10%-rule floor, restated in fitness currency). **But:** this
is the *same claim* Syntropic Dyadism makes in moral-grammar currency. Hamilton
re-expresses it; he does not add a new prediction about the organism.

**Kill criterion.** KILLED if `rB > C` is presented as *deriving* Syntropic
Dyadism (the naturalistic-bridge transfer D5 forbids). The vow is chosen; rB>C
is a `[B]` mechanism that rhymes with it, not a proof of it.

**Verdict.** Retain as `[I]` cross-reference only — "the in-stack-no-extraction
rule rhymes with kin selection's rB>C; the rhyme is an image, not a derivation."
Do not give it its own Rosetta cell.

## 3. Character displacement — the divergence-on-contact mechanism `[I]`

**Statement.** Sympatric close relatives diverge more than allopatric ones;
competition in the overlap zone pushes traits apart (Brown & Wilson 1956; Grant
1972; Pfennig & Pfennig 2009).

**Mechanism.** Selection favours individuals that reduce resource/mating
competition with the heterospecific in sympatry.

**Empirical status.** Well-supported as a process (Galápagos finches, stickleback,
*Anolis*, *Plethodon*); hard to distinguish rigorously from founder effects /
plasticity in any single case.

**Type.** Mechanistic.

**Does it add prediction?** *Yes — and the parent pack's §6.4 already half-uses
it.* The L4-collapse rival (Agentz and Skyzai both at L4) is, under character
displacement, a *predicted active divergence*: overlapping functions in contact
show either competition or displacement. Character displacement names the
mechanism by which Gause's exclusion is *avoided* — the two functions diverge
until they no longer compete. **This adds to Gause:** Gause says "merge → one
excludes the other"; character displacement says "or they differentiate until
they stop overlapping." The latter is the *healthy* outcome the corpus wants for
overlapping castes.

**Kill criterion.** KILLED if displacement is read as the castes "evolving"
biologically. It is an `[I]` image for *functional differentiation under
contact pressure* — a prediction about what healthy overlap looks like, not a
claim about descent.

**Verdict.** Retain `[I]`. Pairs with Gause (the parent pack) to give the full
picture: overlap resolves by exclusion (unhealthy) or by displacement (healthy).

## 4. MacArthur–Wilson island biogeography — turnover equilibrium `[I]`

**Statement.** Island species count is a dynamic equilibrium of immigration
(declining) and extinction (rising), shifted by area (larger → more) and
isolation (nearer → more); composition turns over at equilibrium. (MacArthur &
Wilson 1963, 1967.)

**Mechanism.** Immigration/extinction rate curves cross at the equilibrium
richness; area and isolation shift the curves.

**Empirical status.** Broadly supported qualitatively (Simberloff & Wilson 1969
mangrove experiment); quantitative predictions often fail in real systems
(Gilbert 1980; Whittaker & Fernández-Palacios). The species-area relationship is
one of ecology's most robust patterns; the equilibrium model is partial.

**Type.** Mechanistic (immigration–extinction equilibrium), subsuming a pattern
(the species-area relationship).

**Does it add prediction?** *Marginal.* The "turnover at equilibrium" image
rhymes with the Soul Loop's "the ring never turns once" honest-state meta-fact:
the system can be in dynamic equilibrium (constant composition) without any
*cycle having turned*. That is a genuine conceptual contribution — equilibrium ≠
progress. But the area/isolation variables have no clean organism analogue, and
forcing one would be Procrustean.

**Kill criterion.** KILLED if area/isolation is mapped onto castes/organs to
derive a "species count" for the organism. The cardinality fence fires: the
organism does not have a biological species count.

**Verdict.** Retain as a `[I]` *cautionary* image only: "equilibrium of
composition is not the same as a cycle having turned; the honest-state
meta-fact is a MacArthur-Wilson-style equilibrium, not a progress claim." Do
not give it a cell.

## 5. Dollo's law (irreversibility) — DO NOT IMPORT `[I/C] → retracted`

**Statement.** Evolution cannot return to a prior state; lost complex structures
are not regained. (Dollo 1893.)

**Empirical status.** Treated as a defeasible tendency; numerous putative
exceptions (Collin & Miglietta 2008).

**Does it add prediction?** *No — and importing it is dangerous.* The corpus's
own K3 (archive-first) and K4 (Grace Exit) fences are built on the premise that
**the system must be reversible** — leave with everything, recover the
superseded state. Dollo's irreversibility, taken as image, would *contradict*
K3/K4 by suggesting once-archived structures cannot return. The doctrine
deliberately rejects irreversibility as a design principle. This is the
mirror-image of the trophic-as-rank kill: where trophic contradicts η=0, Dollo
contradicts K3/K4.

**Kill criterion (fires immediately).** KILLED — Dollo's irreversibility
contradicts the K3/K4 reversibility design. Retained in the ledger as a
*documented non-fit*: a bio-rule whose image actively fights the organism's
constitutional fences. Listed here so a future agent does not re-import it.

**Verdict.** Retracted on entry. Recorded as a non-fit (the informative failure).

## 6. The directional tendencies (Bergmann, Allen, Foster, Cope, Rapoport) — decoration, not prediction `[I/C]`

These five are **statistical ecogeographic/macroevolutionary tendencies**:
Bergmann (colder → larger body, 1847), Allen (colder → shorter appendages,
1877), Foster/island-rule (small mainland → island giant; large → island dwarf,
1964), Cope (body size increases over geological time, ~1880s), Rapoport
(higher-latitude → broader range, Stevens 1989 naming Rapoport 1975).

**Common empirical status.** All contested, all with documented exceptions and
reversals; all "rules of thumb" whose support varies sharply by clade, region,
trait. Modern literature treats them as statistical tendencies, not laws.

**Type.** All directional/pattern.

**Do any add prediction?** *No.* Each is a post-hoc summary of a trend along a
single environmental axis (temperature, island area, geological time, latitude).
**None of these axes has a typed analogue in the organism**, and inventing one
is the Procrustean move the parent pack's cardinality fence forbids. To say
"higher-L1 castes have 'broader range' (Rapoport)" or "the organism 'grows over
time' (Cope)" is to decorate — it adds interpretive freedom without adding a
testable claim about the dependency graph.

**The deeper hazard.** These are exactly the rules most easily recruited for
**scala naturae reasoning** — the Great Chain the parent pack named as the
ecology's displaced RIVAL. Cope's rule (bigger over time) and Bergmann's
(colder → bigger) can be misread as "the organism ascends / grows toward an
ideal," which is the teleology the convergence entry already cut. Importing
them risks borrowing a rival's warrant dressed as biology's.

**Kill criterion.** KILLED on entry — no typed axis analogue; decoration, not
prediction; recruitment hazard for scala-naturae reasoning.

**Verdict.** All five retracted on entry. Recorded as the decoration class —
the rules most likely to be misused because they sound like laws.

> **Bergmann mechanism/tendency register note (L3 audit 2026-08-03,
> `08_L3_AUDIT_HOLOBIONT_EKTROPIC_ALIGNMENT_2026_08_03.md` Finding 1).** What is

## 7. The taxon cycle — the staged-trajectory image `[I/C]`

**Statement.** Island lineages pass through stages: widespread colonist →
interior differentiating → range contraction/fragmentation → narrow endemic →
extinction; restarts with new colonists. (Wilson 1961, Melanesian ants.)

**Empirical status.** Mixed; supported in some systems (Economo & Sarnat 2012;
Caribbean reptiles/birds), criticised as underspecified.

**Type.** Directional/pattern (a staged trajectory), driven by competitive
displacement.

**Does it add prediction?** *No.* It is a staged *sequence*, and the corpus's
Soul Loop is explicitly **not** a staged trajectory toward an endpoint — the
Balance-of-Houses contract rules that the loop is a feedback graph, not a
seven-stage realization. The taxon cycle's endpoint (extinction/endemism) has no
organism analogue; the organism is not "heading toward endemic narrowness."

**Kill criterion.** KILLED — staged-trajectory image conflicts with the
loop-as-feedback-graph ruling; no typed endpoint analogue.

**Verdict.** Retracted on entry. Recorded as a non-fit (staged sequence vs.
feedback loop).

## 8. R\* (Tilman) — the lowest-resource-wins mechanism `[I]`

**Statement.** For consumers of a single limiting resource, the species with the
lowest R\* (lowest equilibrium resource concentration it can persist on)
excludes all others; with *n* resources, ≤ *n* species coexist when each is
limited by a different resource. (Tilman 1982.)

**Empirical status.** Well-supported in chemostat/lab; hard in the field.
Foundational for community assembly.

**Type.** Mechanistic.

**Does it add prediction?** *Yes, narrowly — and it sharpens Gause.* R\* says
*which* competitor wins: the one that can persist at the lowest resource level.
Translated under `[I]`: when two functions compete for the same bounded resource
(attention, compute, authority), the one that can operate at the lower resource
floor drives the other out. **This is a refinement of the parent pack's Gause
entry** — it predicts the *direction* of exclusion, not just its occurrence.
For the L4-collapse rival (Agentz/Skyzai), R\* predicts the survivor is whichever
execution surface can stage changes at the lower authority/compute floor.

**Kill criterion.** KILLED if "resource" is read as biological (nutrient,
light). Survives as `[I]` image for *bounded-stack-resource competition*
(attention, authority envelope budget, compute) — a prediction about which
overlap wins, not a claim about nutrients.

**Verdict.** Retain `[I]` as a refinement of the parent pack's Gause entry.
Names the *direction* of exclusion.

## 9. Hologenome theory — the already-canonical image, re-tiered `[I/C]`

**Statement.** The holobiont (host + microbiome) is the unit of selection; the
hologenome (host + microbiome genomes) supplies variation. (Zilber-Rosenberg &
Rosenberg 2008; term *holobiont* Margulis 1991.)

**Empirical status.** The *pattern* (hosts + persistent functional microbes)
is well-established. The *mechanism* (holobiont as a genuine level of selection)
is **actively contested** — critics (Moran & Sloan 2015; Douglas & Werren 2016)
argue horizontally-acquired microbiomes are not transmitted faithfully enough to
be stable selection targets; defenders (Rosenberg et al. 2018; Roughgarden et
al. 2020) offer formal models. An active controversy, not settled doctrine.

**Type.** Mechanistic (a unit-of-selection claim), with a pattern component.

**Already in the corpus.** This is the theory the holobiont-organs synthesis is
*built on*, and it has already been re-tiered `[I/C]` comparative-image (not
ontology) by the L3 register-correction
(`05_L3_AUDIT_HOLOBIONT_REGISTER_CORRECTION_2026_08_03.md`). The lesson of that
correction applies *a fortiori* to the theory itself: **the theory's contested
status is itself the register warning.** If working biologists have not settled
whether the holobiont is a unit of selection, the Magnum Opus organism certainly
cannot inherit that status by analogy.

**Does it add prediction?** *As theory, no — it is the contested claim being
imaged, not a mechanism that adds prediction over the image.* As *pattern*,
yes — the host–microbe entanglement pattern is the pedagogically useful core
(the "many roles, one lineage" image the holobiont-organs file uses).

**Kill criterion.** KILLED if hologenome theory is cited as *settled* biology
warranting the holobiont-as-organism claim. Survives only as: "the host-microbe
entanglement pattern is real `[B]`; the unit-of-selection claim is contested
`[B-contested]`; the Magnum Opus mapping is `[I/C]` image on top of a contested
`[B]` — two register steps removed from settled."

**Verdict.** No new cell. The existing `05_` correction governs. Re-stated here
only to record that **the biological theory's own contested status is a register
fence the image must carry.**

## 11. What this means for the holobiont

The holobiont frame, audited against the *full* bio-rules family rather than
just the three the parent pack examined, gains **three narrow images** and loses
nothing:

1. **Emery — watch for defection wearing the stack's own livery.** The most
   dangerous extraction is the one that shares the recognition vocabulary. This
   is what Āma (don't mis-tag a cooperator), Āmiṣa (don't accept poisoned
   nurture), and Sva-karma (don't execute what you were only authorized to
   stage) already fence — Emery gives the bio-image of *why those are the same
   failure mode*.

2. **Character displacement — healthy overlap diverges.** When two functions
   share a niche (the L4-collapse rival), the healthy resolution is divergence
   until they no longer compete — not merger (Gause says merger → exclusion).
   This is a *prediction about what good overlap looks like*, checkable against
   commit history.

3. **R\* — the lower-floor function wins.** In bounded-resource overlap, the
   function that can operate at the lower authority/compute floor drives the
   other out. Names the direction of exclusion.

The **directional-tendency family** (Bergmann/Allen/Foster/Cope/Rapoport) is the
class to be most careful about: they sound like laws, they are statistical
tendencies, and they are the natural recruiting ground for scala-naturae
("higher/larger/older = better") reasoning that biology displaced and that the
corpus explicitly rejects. **Dollo** is the one rule whose image actively fights
the constitution (K3/K4 reversibility).

The hologenome theory's contested status is the standing reminder: the holobiont
image rests on `[I/C]` mapping on top of `[B-contested]` theory — two register
steps from settled. The image is useful (the host–microbe entanglement pattern
is real); the ontology claim is not licensed.
