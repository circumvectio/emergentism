# Rosetta Stone: Mythology Comparison Prototype

> **[金] Crack.** The predecessor turned a framework-selected arrangement of
> mythic figures into “the same seven structural positions,” said operators
> determined what traditions discovered, and treated cultural distance as
> independent confirmation.
>
> **Repair.** The table is an optional comparative lens `[I]`. It records motifs
> a reader may compare; it proves no shared ontology, hidden doctrine, universal
> sevenfold structure, historical descent, or statistically independent result.

## Comparative boundary

Myths, Tarot, Jungian constructs, and modern animal symbolism are different
source classes. They were produced, transmitted, edited, and interpreted under
different conditions. Their placement in one table is selected by this
framework after the Rosetta rows were known. That selection prevents the table
from serving as independent confirmation of those rows.

The figures below are not identical, causally generated by operators, or
coordinates in their source traditions. A row is a prompt for close comparison,
not a claim that a culture “discovered” Emergentist geometry. Similarities and
mismatches must both remain visible.

The table does not claim exhaustive or specialist-approved descriptions. Every
attribution must be checked against sources from the named tradition before
publication. “Animal” entries are modern framework symbols, not claims about a
single Indigenous or cross-cultural totem system.

## PIE and physical boundary

`Dyḗus Ph₂tḗr` may be compared with a bright-sky-father motif `[B/I]` when the
linguistic reconstruction and daughter-language claims are cited precisely.
It does not establish a teleological force, the Rosetta's L7 row, or descent of
Zeus, Odin, and Anu from one metaphysical field. `*h₂r̥tó-`, Ṛta, Asha, and
*ordo* require separate philological histories; resemblance does not fuse them.

“Sky” is a vertical metaphor here, not an enlarged physical light cone.
Esoteric and quantum-retrocausal associations are outside this evidentiary
claim.

## Testing and failure

A serious comparative test must preregister a corpus, units of comparison,
coding rules, dependence model, rival row counts, and disconfirming cases before
the target traditions are coded. Specialists and blinded coders must be able to
reject proposed matches. Failure includes low inter-rater agreement, equally
good rival arrangements, dependence on cherry-picked episodes, or erasure of a
tradition's own categories.

Until such a design discriminates among alternatives, this remains an
illustrative prototype. A failed mapping is retained as a failure; it may not be
re-described as deeper confirmation.
