# Rosetta R12: Megalithic Coordination Questions

> **[金] Crack.** The predecessor treated monumental sites as receipts for
> equatorial, zero-extraction coordination; inferred aggregate `φ`, `ν`, and
> `B`; declared hierarchy unnecessary; and announced the coordination problem
> and Great Filter solved.
>
> **Repair.** Stones establish that construction occurred. Dating, labor,
> subsistence, ritual, authority, coercion, inequality, and institutional form
> require independent archaeological evidence and live scholarly comparison.

## Typed evidence ladder

```text
material remain
  -> dated construction episode
  -> labor and resource estimate
  -> coordination mechanism hypothesis
  -> distribution of cost and authority
  -> Emergentist interpretation
```

No arrow is automatic. A monument does not retain a moral receipt, and a labor
estimate is not a measurement of foresight, viability, Justice, or extraction.

## Fair rival models

For a named site, compare at least:

- seasonal or ritual aggregation;
- kinship and reciprocal obligation;
- specialist or household organization;
- competitive feasting and prestige;
- centralized authority or coercion;
- mixed and changing arrangements; and
- preservation and sampling bias.

The models may coexist across periods. “Mesh” and “institution” are not a
complete archaeological partition.

## What Emergentism may ask `[I/C]`

- Which durable traces coordinated later participants?
- Did the trace survive carrier turnover?
- Who supplied labor and food, who benefited, and who could refuse?
- Which signals or rituals reduced coordination uncertainty?
- What alternative explanations predict the same material pattern?
- Which observation would distinguish a distributed mechanism from a
  centralized one?

These questions may inform an Egregoreotype or ritual-coordination study. They
do not establish the candidate before intervention, turnover, and cost tests.

## Forbidden conversions

- site size or worker count -> `ν`;
- symbolic richness -> `φ`;
- monument completion -> `B=1`;
- absence of a known palace -> proof of no hierarchy;
- ritual -> non-extraction;
- ancient coordination -> solution to modern governance or a Great Filter.

## Upgrade and kill criteria

Upgrade a site claim to `[B]` only with precise archaeological sources and a
statement no stronger than those sources. Retain a coordination mechanism as
`[C]` until it predicts discriminating evidence. Kill the Emergentist reading
if ordinary archaeological models explain the observations as well with fewer
assumptions, or if evidence of coercion/cost transfer contradicts its stated
Justice interpretation.

## Public wording

> Megalithic sites are material evidence of large, durable construction. They
> invite testable questions about how people coordinated; they do not by
> themselves reveal a Burri coordinate, a non-extractive social order, or a
> solved civilizational problem.
