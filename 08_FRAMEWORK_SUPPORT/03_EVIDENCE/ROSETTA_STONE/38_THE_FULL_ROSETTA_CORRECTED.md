## 3 · The chart, and where P is maximal `[A]` given the selection

```text
φ = cot(θ/2)      ν = tan(θ/2)      φν = 1      B = 2/(φ+ν) = sin θ
```

**L4 is the equator:** `φ = ν = 1`, `B = 1`.

**`P_node := min(Φ̂₄, V₄)` is maximal there, and maximising IS balancing.** Under
`min` there is no compensation, so the only way to raise P is to raise the lower
term. The two operations are one.

> **This holds for `min` and does not generalise.**
> `00_THE_BALANCE_OPTIMUM_IS_CONDITIONAL.md`: *"The chart's equator remains
> mathematically balanced even when the real system's optimum lies elsewhere."*
> Its counterexample is Cobb–Douglas, whose optimum sits at `Φ/V = a/b` and is
> equal only when `a = b`. The balance-optimum identity is safe **because the
> corpus selected an AND-class score**, not because balance is optimal in general.

**At L7's pole `φ → ∞`, `ν → 0`, `B → 0`, and therefore `min → 0`** — maximum
coherence, no viability. (`02_THE_TRINITY.md`: *"φ = cot(θ/2) = 1/ν → ∞ at south
pole, 0 at north pole."* ⚠ **Naming defect, unresolved:** that pole is called
*south* there and *the top of the sphere* in owner usage. Same point, two names.)

**The reading this forces, and it is `[I]`:**

> **Viṣṇu preserves by holding the system at the equator, not by sitting there.**
> The seat that maintains the maximum is not the seat that occupies it — which is
> what `deployable = false` means at L5–L7. *"You can use Kālī. You can only
> experience Śiva."*
