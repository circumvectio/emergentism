## The apparent paradox

Unity and plurality become contradictory only if "one" must exclude internal
distinction or "many" must exclude every relation. Actual systems routinely
exhibit both: one organism has many cells; one language has many utterances; one
institution has many changing carriers. The work is to state the relation that
makes the unity non-arbitrary.

## The Emergentist lens `[I]`

Emergentism treats higher-level unity as a persistent constraint or trace that
is realized by, and can reweight, lower-level interactions. The whole is not an
extra substance floating beyond its carriers. The carriers are not thereby
illusory. A proposed whole earns standing only through operational evidence:
persistence, intervention effects, recovery of lower-level behavior, carrier
turnover, and visible costs.

The reciprocal chart can illustrate two coupled descriptions, and the selected
node model `P_node:=Phi*V` can model cases needing both foresight and means. That
is an analogy, not a proof that unity and plurality universally multiply to one.
Other relational models remain live.

Actual wholes, parts, interactions, traces, and records are D4. D5 contains the
possible organizations and futures represented by actual agents. Commitment to
one organization is a D4 act; it is not a `mu` crossing or quantum collapse.

## What remains unresolved

The lens does not prove that every apparent whole is real, that all emergence is
strong, that `S²` is uniquely generative, or that the metaphysical One becomes
the Many. It offers a test for when talk of a whole adds causal or predictive
work instead of merely renaming its parts.

## Kill criteria

The reframing contracts if persistent-whole variables add no intervention or
prediction gain over lower-level descriptions, if carrier turnover destroys the
supposed unity, if the selected conjunctive model loses to a simpler rival, or
if the D4/D5 distinction adds no clarity. The philosophical tension then remains
open; the mathematics survives only as mathematics.
