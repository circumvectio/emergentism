# PD-15 · THE PROBLEM OF EVIL

**Status:** ACTIVE philosophical clarification · repaired 2026-07-20
**Verdict:** the framework can separate kinds of harm; it does not solve the
theological problem of evil or derive moral truth from geometry.

## 1. Separate the questions

- **Suffering:** an actual D4 experience and condition, with biological,
  psychological, social, and environmental causes.
- **Natural harm:** injury not necessarily caused by an accountable agent.
- **Wrongdoing:** an action judged under a stated normative framework.
- **Theodicy:** whether suffering and wrongdoing are compatible with an
  omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good deity.

Confusing these makes one vocabulary appear to answer several different
problems. Emergentism supplies no proof for or against such a deity.

## 2. Descriptive measurement

`etaObserved` may report a declared extraction transfer. It does not classify a
being, person, species, pole, operator, or event as good or evil. Extraction can
be locally profitable; no analytic identity guarantees its collapse. Stability
claims require a specified game, horizon, coupling, observability, feedback,
enforcement, and exit conditions.

Actual harm, transfers, actions, and receipts are D4. D5 contains possible
alternatives an agent represented and could have selected. This distinction may
clarify responsibility; it does not make foresight metaphysically good.

## 3. The declared normative compass

Bearer-complete Justice `[I]` asks:

1. Who is materially affected, including hidden third parties and sustaining
   wholes?
2. Who pays, who benefits, and who holds custody?
3. Was the act authorized and consent informed, scoped, and revocable?
4. What was committed, performed, and actually receipted as outcome?
5. What is irreversible, contestable, reparable, or escapable?

Aggregate gains cannot offset the destruction of one bearer. Voluntary
sacrifice is a distinct informed costly class, never demanded by a collective.
Within this declared norm, "evil" is best reserved for grave, accountable,
unjust harm—not inferred from a coordinate or mythic name.

## 4. What is and is not dissolved

The framework dissolves one category mistake: suffering, natural cost,
extraction, wrongdoing, and theological evil are not synonyms. It offers a
receipt discipline for examining actual responsibility and alternatives.

It does **not** explain why innocent suffering exists, prove that all harm serves
a larger whole, show that wrongdoers self-terminate, or reconcile suffering with
divine attributes. Those ancient questions remain open.

## Kill criteria

This clarification loses practical value if `etaObserved` cannot be measured
without moral loading, if the Justice ledger hides bearers or excuses coercion,
if receipts add no accountability, or if simpler legal and ethical analyses
perform better. Failure leaves the reality of suffering and the theological
problem intact.
