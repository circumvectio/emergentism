# PD-13 — The Hard Problem of Consciousness

Emergentism does not dissolve the hard problem. Its useful contribution is a
typed epistemic asymmetry `[I]`:

- first-person experience is directly present to the experiencer;
- third-person science studies reports, behavior, physiology, computation, and
  causal structure;
- neither access route automatically contains or invalidates the other.

First-person salience is not public calibration. Third-person correlation is
not, by itself, an explanation of why experience occurs. Contemporary physical,
functional, representational, higher-order, predictive, global-workspace,
integrated-information, illusionist, and panpsychist approaches remain live
rivals; this note adjudicates none of them.

No claim is made that every philosopher made one error, that practitioners have
privileged public authority, that an AI system is aware or unaware, or that one
human plus one machine forms a complete cognitive unit. `S²`, the Bloch sphere,
`Φ`, and the D-ladder do not identify mind and matter as projections of one
point. The zombie argument is not refuted here.

The remembered-sunset case shows only that psychological vividness, actuality,
and dependency priority are distinct. It does not explain phenomenal character.

**Open conjecture `[C]`:** a multi-level causal/representational account may
reduce some apparent explanatory gaps. **Kill:** if the register distinctions
add no explanatory or empirical discrimination beyond existing theories, drop
the overlay. The hard problem remains open either way.
