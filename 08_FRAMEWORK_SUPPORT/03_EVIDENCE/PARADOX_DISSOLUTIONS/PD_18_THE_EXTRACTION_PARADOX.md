# PD-18 — The Extraction Heuristic

Extraction is one useful heuristic, not the hidden identity of every paradox.
A formalization can omit, idealize, or externalize something relevant; the task
is to name that loss locally and test whether restoring it changes the problem.

| Problem family | Standard issue/rivals | Possible extraction question `[I]` | Non-fit boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hume/induction | justification of projecting observed regularities | which background assumptions were hidden? | sitting or “Ground” does not justify induction |
| Münchhausen regress | justification ends in regress, circle, or stopping point | what warrant was treated as costless? | distinct from Gödel's formal hypotheses |
| Euthyphro | dependence of goodness on command or independent standard | whose authority was smuggled in? | not solved by a balance score |
| Liar/self-reference | semantic truth, syntax, hierarchy, fixed points | which language level was collapsed? | not primarily a resource-transfer problem |
| Zeno | limits, continuity, task decomposition | were points treated as stopping acts? | standard analysis already resolves key forms |
| Consciousness | first/third-person explanatory asymmetry | what does a public model omit? | omission does not prove a special substance |
| Trolley cases | competing duties, rights, uncertainty, action/omission | whose loss is hidden by aggregation? | no single algorithm follows |

Gödel's incompleteness theorems are established mathematics `[A]` under precise
formal hypotheses. They do not by themselves prove Hume, Euthyphro,
Münchhausen, consciousness, or apophatic Ground. Each crosswalk remains `[I]`.

**Kill:** reject the extraction reading in any case where it makes no new
distinction, misstates the standard problem, or loses to a domain-native
account. The other cases receive no support from one fit.
